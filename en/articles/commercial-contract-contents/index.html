The commercial contract with the client: what it contains

In the worker cooperative model there are two separate contracts: one between member and cooperative (the employment relationship) and one between cooperative and client. The latter is the assignment agreement, the commercial contract. The member does not sign it but lives with its consequences every day, so it pays to know how to read it.

What the agreement is for

It defines what is supplied to the client, on what terms and for what fee. It protects both sides: the client knows what to expect, the cooperative has a clear commitment, the member has a defined scope of work.

Typical clauses

Subject and scope

Describes the service: activities, expected results, any exclusions. A vague scope is the main source of later disputes.

Duration and renewal

Start date, end date, how renewal works, notice of termination for both parties.

Fee and invoicing

Rate (hourly, daily, fixed), currency, invoicing frequency, payment terms, any late-payment interest. For foreign clients, invoicing details and VAT treatment must be checked case by case.

How the work is performed

Place of work (remote, at the client’s site), tools, availability, meetings, contact people. Note: it describes organisation, and should not be read as unlimited power of direction.

Confidentiality and intellectual property

Confidentiality duties, use of results, ownership of what is produced. These clauses directly involve the member.

Liability and insurance

Liability limits, insurance cover, handling of errors and complaints.

Governing law and jurisdiction

Which law governs the contract and where disputes are settled. With foreign clients this deserves a careful read.

Expense reimbursement

Which expenses are reimbursable, with what documents and limits.

Example: Elena, data analyst

A fictional example: Elena does data analysis for a British company. The agreement provides for 80 hours a month, an agreed hourly rate, delivery of a monthly report, invoicing at month end with payment at 30 days, confidentiality on the data and ownership of reports by the client. Elena notices the scope does not say who supplies the software tools: she flags it before signing and the clause is added. Without that reading, the doubt would have surfaced in the first month.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Collecting terms. The member tells Tesla3000 the terms agreed with the client.
  2. Draft agreement. The cooperative prepares the contract (or adapts the client’s) and shares it with the member.
  3. Reading and comments. The member checks that scope, timing and rate reflect the deal and flags changes.
  4. Negotiation with the client. The cooperative handles critical clauses.
  5. Signature and start. Once signed, work is recorded on the platform.

What to check

  • Consistency between the agreement and your employment contract (duties, hours).
  • Exclusivity or non-compete clauses: they may limit other assignments.
  • Payment terms: they affect cash flow and the advance fund.
  • Penalties and liability.

The agreement is not paperwork: it is the map of your relationship with the client. Reading it well is the cheapest way to avoid problems.

Frequently asked questions

Who signs the commercial contract?

The cooperative and the client. The member is not a party to the commercial contract but follows its scope when carrying out the work.

Can I ask for changes to the contract?

Yes, before signing: the member in practice negotiates working conditions and rate, which the cooperative turns into the contract. Some standard clauses may have limited room for change.

What language is the contract in?

Often Italian or English, depending on the client. If it is in a language you do not master, have the main points explained before proceeding.
