Tour guide and escort for foreign tour operators: mind the requirements

A Canadian tour operator sells group trips in Italy and needs someone to escort them for ten days: Rome, Florence, Venice, Cinque Terre. A Japanese operator wants someone to follow small groups of art lovers. It is a sector with a lot of demand, but also with requirements not to be underestimated: whoever acts as a guide or escort must understand which activity they are carrying out and which qualifications it requires.

Guide or escort: the difference that matters

  • Tour guide: explains sites, museums and places. It is a regulated profession, with qualifications and licences set by law; in some places a licensed guide is legally required.
  • Tour escort: follows the group through the whole trip, handling logistics, hotels, transfers and solving problems. Here too requirements and rules are set and need checking.
  • Assistant or tour leader for the foreign operator: coordinates, but does not explain sites that require a licensed guide.

The practical advice is simple: before accepting, ask what exactly you will do and check whether those activities need specific qualifications. The cooperative manages the employment relationship and administration, but cannot replace a licence the law requires. Professions with a register or licence and exclusivity rules cannot be handled as free activities.

What to negotiate with the tour operator

  1. Programme: days, stops, times, included activities.
  2. Rate: per tour day, with a separate line for transfers and free days.
  3. Expenses: meals, local transport, entrance fees; who advances and who reimburses.
  4. Group cash: if you handle money for local payments, define responsibility.
  5. Contingencies: delays, strikes, participant illness; who decides and who pays.
  6. Seasonality: from April to October work is intense, in winter almost absent.

Preparing a tour well

Even with every requirement in order, a tour’s quality depends on preparation. Check museum and restaurant timetables and bookings a few days ahead, keep local suppliers’ emergency numbers, and prepare a fallback plan for strikes and bad weather. Log attendance and expenses daily, uploading receipts to the platform. At the end of the trip, collect participants’ comments and share them with the tour operator: they are the basis for winning new assignments.

Example: a ten-day tour

A fictional example. Imagine Sofia, a cultural-heritage graduate who already holds the qualifications required for her activity. A Canadian tour operator engages her for four ten-day tours in spring. After checking qualifications and the scope of the assignment, the cooperative signs the contract and Sofia is engaged as a worker-member. On the platform she logs days and expense claims; the tour operator receives an invoice at the end of the cycle and Sofia the monthly payslip.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Check requirements: qualification, type of activity, any registrations required. If they are missing, we talk before anything else.
  2. Membership with a share of capital refundable on exit (Law 142/2001).
  3. Contract with the tour operator: days, rates, expenses, contingencies.
  4. Platform: attendance per day, expense claims with receipts, documents.
  5. Invoicing the foreign tour operator under B2B rules verified case by case.
  6. Protections: INPS, INAIL, holidays, sick leave, severance pay; professional liability included in the package.

VAT number or cooperative

With a VAT number under the flat-rate regime (85,000 euro threshold, 15% substitute tax, 5% in the first five years with requirements) independent guides work with several operators, but handle invoices and Separate Management contributions. In the cooperative, seasonal work is framed as an employment relationship, with less paperwork, provided the activity does not require qualifications you do not hold.

Frequently asked questions

Can I work as a tour guide without a licence?

Tour guiding is a regulated profession with specific requirements. Before accepting, check the rules that apply to your case: the cooperative cannot replace a qualification required by law.

What is the difference between a guide and an escort?

The guide explains monuments and sites; the escort follows the group for logistics, assistance and coordination. Rules and requirements may differ and must be checked.

Are travel days paid?

Set it in the contract: tour days, transfer days and rest days are often distinguished.
