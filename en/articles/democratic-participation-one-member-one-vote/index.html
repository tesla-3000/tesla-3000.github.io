Democratic participation: one member, one vote

In many companies, people who work have no say in the direction of the business. In a worker cooperative it is different: members are also owners and have a voice. The principle is simple and fits in one sentence: one member, one vote. Here is what it means for those working with Tesla3000.

The one-member-one-vote principle

In a capital company, voting weight usually depends on the shares held. In a cooperative the rule is normally one vote per member, regardless of how much capital they subscribed, how long they have been in, or the revenue they generate. Someone who has just joined counts as much as someone who has been there for years. The precise rules, including majorities and proxies, are in the by-laws.

Where participation happens

The members’ assembly. This is the body that decides on approval of the accounts, appointment of directors, by-law changes and use of surpluses. It meets as the by-laws require, at least once a year, and the rules may allow remote participation, useful for those working in other cities or countries.

The internal regulation. Law 142/2001 provides that the cooperative has a filed regulation governing the organisation of members’ work. Its approval and amendments go through the assembly.

The community. Not everything is formal: members compare notes, propose services and training, and flag problems. It is where many ideas are born that are later brought to the assembly.

Example: Beatrice and her proposal

Example: imagine Beatrice, a translator who has been a member for a year. She notices that many colleagues ask for legal English courses. She brings the proposal to the community and then to the assembly. Members discuss it, weigh costs and interest, and decide whether to include it among training activities. There is no guarantee the proposal passes: cooperative democracy does not mean the proposer wins, but that everyone can contribute and vote.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Becoming a member. You subscribe the share capital quota and are admitted.
  2. Notice. You receive the assembly notice with agenda and documents.
  3. Information. The accounts and proposals under discussion are made available to you.
  4. Participation. You speak and vote, in person, remotely or by proxy, under the rules.
  5. Resolutions. Decisions apply to all members.
  6. Updates. You learn the outcome through the platform and community.

Responsibilities that come with the vote

Taking part also means taking on responsibilities: informing yourself about the topics, respecting majority decisions, behaving fairly towards other members. The vote concerns the life of the cooperative, not individual work contracts: your pay and conditions follow your contract and the regulation, and are not reopened each time by other members’ votes.

You do not need to be an expert

You do not need to know company law to take part: documents should be understandable and you can always ask support for explanations. If you work from afar, ask which remote participation methods are provided. If you prefer not to speak up, you can remain a member who works and votes when you see fit.

Transparency and information

Real participation requires understandable information. Before the assembly you are entitled to know the documents being voted on, in particular the accounts and proposed amendments. If a point is unclear, ask support for explanations before the meeting: a question raised in advance often leads to a more useful discussion than an improvised objection. Remember too that you can stand for office, if the by-laws allow, and contribute directly to guiding the cooperative.

Frequently asked questions

Does every member really have one vote?

Yes. In cooperatives the rule is normally one vote per member, regardless of the share capital subscribed, under the by-laws.

Am I required to attend assemblies?

Taking part is a right and an opportunity. The methods, including remote participation, and any proxies are set out in the by-laws and regulation.

What is voted on?

Topics such as approval of the accounts, appointment of officers, by-law changes and use of surpluses, under the by-laws and the law.
