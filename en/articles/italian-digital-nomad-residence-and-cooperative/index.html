Italian digital nomad: what to weigh on residence and the co-op

The digital nomad works from a laptop, often for foreign clients, and changes city every few months: Lisbon in spring, Bali in winter, Mexico City in between. For an Italian the idea is appealing, but between freedom and reality there are three concrete questions: where am I resident? Where do I pay taxes? Where am I covered by insurance and social security? This article does not give answers, but indicates what to assess before choosing a form of work.

Questions that come first

  1. Tax residence: it depends on elements such as registration in the civil registry, domicile, centre of interests, days of presence. Whether you remain registered with Italian authorities changes a lot. There is no simple rule valid for everyone.
  2. Double taxation treaties: they decide where an income is taxed when two states claim it. Each treaty has its own clauses.
  3. Local rules: many countries have specific visas or permits for digital nomads, and limits on length of stay or activity.
  4. Social security and healthcare: cover for illness, accident and pension depends on the system you are in.
  5. Client’s permanent establishment: for the foreign company, your presence in a given country can have effects: not to be overlooked.

All these points should be assessed with a tax professional before starting. No cooperative can replace this assessment, nor promise advantages.

Where the cooperative can help, and where not

The cooperative manages the employment relationship: contract with the foreign client, member’s contract, payslip, invoicing, reporting platform, professional liability insurance. Generally the worker remains tax resident in Italy; for those who live abroad stably, tax residence and treaties must be assessed case by case, and the model may not suit.

It is better suited to those who keep residence in Italy and travel for limited periods than to those who have moved permanently elsewhere. In the latter case, a structure in the country of residence is often preferable.

Example: six months around, base in Italy

A fictional example. Imagine Nicola, a front-end developer resident in Bologna, who works for a Dutch company and spends three months a year in Portugal and Spain on short stays, without changing residence. With a tax professional he checks that short stays do not alter his position. Through the cooperative, Tesla3000 signs the contract with the Dutch company, Nicola is a worker-member with a monthly payslip and logs activities on the platform wherever he is. If in future he decided to move permanently, he would discuss it with us and his adviser beforehand.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Tell us about your working life: where you live, how long you travel, which client.
  2. Preliminary check: we tell you which aspects to explore with a professional, without giving answers we cannot give.
  3. Membership with a share of capital refundable on exit (Law 142/2001).
  4. Contract with the client, B2B rules verified case by case.
  5. Platform: activities, expenses, documents and signing accessible from anywhere; WhatsApp and email support.
  6. Reassessment: if conditions change (relocation, new country), the set-up is reviewed.

The alternatives

If you live abroad stably, the most linear solution is often a local structure in your country of residence, or an Italian VAT number under the flat-rate regime (85,000 euro threshold, 15% substitute tax, 5% in the first five years with requirements, where applicable), with Separate Management contributions. Here too specific checks are needed, because the regime has access conditions and your residence situation may matter.

Frequently asked questions

Does the cooperative work if I live abroad?

It depends on your residence situation, the country where you live and the rules on work and social security. It is not an automatic solution: assess case by case before starting.

Can I work from another country without changing residence?

The subject is delicate: stays and presence in another state may affect taxes, social security and permits. A specific check is needed.

How many days can I stay abroad?

There is no single answer: it depends on residence, treaties and local legislation. Consult a professional.
