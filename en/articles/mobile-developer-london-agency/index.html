Mobile developer for a London agency

London agencies build apps for banks, retail, media and startups, and often bring external developers into projects with deadlines tied to product launches. For an iOS, Android or cross-platform (Flutter, React Native) developer it is a market with attractive rates, but it requires a contract, invoices and punctuality. Tesla3000 handles the administrative side.

How a mobile project runs

An app project usually follows two-week sprints, with design handoff from Figma, development, testing, release via TestFlight or Play Console, and finally store publication. Typical work includes API integrations, push notifications, in-app payments and accessibility. Agencies pay per day (day rate) or per month, invoicing per sprint or at month end. Agree on:

  • stack (Swift, Kotlin, Flutter, React Native) and supported versions;
  • code review process and definition of done;
  • who manages Apple/Google accounts, certificates and signing;
  • responsibility for post-release bugs and warranty period.

The cooperative’s role

The cooperative signs the agreement with the agency and invoices in pounds. Since Brexit the UK is outside the EU: generic B2B services are normally not subject to Italian VAT, checked case by case. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have a work relationship: INPS contributions, INAIL and, if employment, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. Code produced follows the assignment clauses of the agreement.

Typical pitfalls

  • Store review. An Apple rejection can delay release: agree who handles fixes and how hours are counted.
  • Personal accounts. Never publish apps with your own credentials.
  • Post-release warranty. Define duration and scope.
  • Dependencies and licences. Third-party libraries with incompatible licences are a risk.
  • Launch-day availability. Release day may need extra presence: agree it.

Example

Example: imagine Nicola, a Flutter developer from Lecce, hired by a London agency to build a booking app for a gym chain: five months, day rate, two-week sprints. The agreement includes a 30-day post-release warranty. The cooperative invoices in pounds per sprint; Nicola logs days on the platform and receives a monthly payslip. Fictional character.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Intro call. You present project, stack, days, rate.
  2. Joining. Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
  3. Agreement. Contract with the agency, with code assignment and warranty.
  4. Activity. Days and expenses on the platform.
  5. Invoicing. The cooperative invoices and collects, also in foreign currency.
  6. Payslip. Monthly pay and documents online.

INAIL, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

Compared with a VAT number

The flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) is widely used by freelance developers; the cooperative makes sense if you want worker protections and less admin.

Checklist before signing

Before starting, ask the agency for: final designs and the design system; documented APIs and test environments; devices and OS versions to support; rules for third-party libraries; the release procedure and store accounts. Ask who does QA and how bugs are reported. Agree how fix hours are counted after a release rejection. A contract with a defined warranty duration and scope avoids endless change requests after publication.

Frequently asked questions

Who owns the Apple and Google developer accounts?

Normally the end client or the agency. Never publish apps under your personal account unless agreed in the contract.

How is sprint-based development paid?

The agreement can set a daily or monthly rate with invoicing per sprint, with defined approval times.

Does the cooperative invoice in pounds?

Yes, it can invoice and collect in foreign currency. Your pay on the payslip remains in euro.
