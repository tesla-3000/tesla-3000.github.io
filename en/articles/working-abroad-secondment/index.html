Working abroad on assignment: secondment and what to know

It happens that a foreign client asks you to work in person for a few days or months: meetings, installations, training, field projects. “Remote” work becomes physical work in another country. Here the rules change, and not always intuitively. This guide gives a basic map and invites you to check each case.

Three different situations

Business trip

A temporary, generally short move to carry out an activity at the employer’s or client’s premises: the place of work stays in Italy. Normally the employment relationship and cover remain Italian, but permits or notifications may be needed in the destination country, depending on duration and activity.

Secondment

The worker is sent for a temporary period to perform their activity in another country, keeping the relationship with the home employer. Within the EU there are specific rules, with minimum guarantees for the worker in the host country and documentation for social security. Outside the EU, national rules and bilateral agreements apply.

Permanent relocation

The habitual place of work and often residence move. Taxes, contributions, permits and labour rules change. It is not a business trip and should be treated as a project in its own right.

What to check before leaving

  • Duration and nature of the activity. A meeting differs from continuous operational work.
  • Residence and work permits. In the EU free movement simplifies; outside the EU, visas and specific authorisations are often needed.
  • Mandatory notifications. Some countries require notification for seconded workers.
  • Social security. Which system applies and with what documentation.
  • Tax. Tax residence, taxation of income earned abroad and double taxation treaties: assessed case by case.
  • Health and accident insurance. Cover during the stay; do not assume it.
  • Costs and reimbursement. Travel, accommodation, allowances, and who bears them.
  • Contract and agreement. Whether the trip falls within the agreement and under what conditions.

Beware “remote from abroad”

A frequent case: the member wants to work remotely from another country for months, as a digital nomad. Here tax residence, contributions and permits need careful assessment. No outcomes should be promised: a specific analysis is needed and it may not be possible.

Example: Stefano, installation technician

A fictional example: Stefano, a cooperative member, must go for two weeks to a client’s premises in another EU country to install a system. He informs the cooperative in advance. Together they check duration, notifications needed in the host country, health cover and insurance cover for work abroad. The agreement provides that the client reimburses travel and accommodation. Stefano notes expenses and uploads them to the platform with receipts. For a longer trip or one outside the EU, the check would be broader.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Notification. You tell Tesla3000 as soon as you know of the trip, with dates and place.
  2. Verification. Permits, notifications, social security and cover are checked.
  3. Agreement. We check that travel and expenses are provided for with the client.
  4. Travel documents. The cooperative supplies the necessary documentation, if required.
  5. Expense reimbursement. During and after the trip, you upload expense claims with receipts to the platform.

Still to check

  • Whether a specialist adviser is needed for the destination country.
  • Whether the duration passes thresholds beyond which tax or social security rules change.
  • Whether the activity is allowed under the type of permit you hold.

Frequently asked questions

Can I work abroad for a few weeks without problems?

It depends on the country, duration and activity. Even short periods can involve formalities (permits, notifications, health and social security cover). Check before leaving.

Does secondment change who pays the contributions?

Under certain conditions, a seconded worker may remain in the home country's social security system, with the appropriate documentation. Conditions must be checked case by case.

Does it change anything if I live abroad permanently?

Yes: tax residence, contributions and permits change. It is a topic to assess with an adviser and should not be taken for granted.
