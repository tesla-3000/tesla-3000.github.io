Contributions and pension: what changes when you work via a cooperative

Pension is one of the topics that worries self-employed people most. With a VAT number, contributions are something you manage and pay yourself; in a cooperative, in the case of employment, the mechanism changes. Let us see how, without promising anything we cannot guarantee.

How an employee’s contributions work

For a member with an employment relationship the cooperative pays INPS contributions into the Employees’ Pension Fund (FPLD) and insures the worker with INAIL. The worker’s share of contributions is withheld from the payslip; the employer’s share is paid by the cooperative. The worker does not have to calculate or pay anything personally.

Contributions paid build your pension record, which you can check in the contribution statement on the INPS portal. The amount of the future pension depends on the accumulated balance, length of contributions and the rules in force at retirement: nobody can predict it with certainty today.

Comparison with a VAT number

Those working under a VAT number pay their own contributions, for example to the INPS Separate Management or the Artisans and Traders schemes, depending on the activity. It is a legitimate choice and for many professionals the best one; however it requires discipline: advance payments, balances, deadlines, and the risk of missing payments in weak cash-flow periods.

In an employment relationship in the cooperative, contributions are paid regularly month by month, tied to the payslip, and contribution continuity is easier to maintain. The comparison is not made in figures: it depends on the schemes, income and individual contribution histories.

Example: Valentina and her contribution history

Example: imagine Valentina, 34, a translator. For years she worked under a VAT number and paid contributions irregularly, with some gap years. She joins the cooperative with an employment relationship: from then on the cooperative pays contributions every month. Valentina asks her advice centre or the INPS portal for her statement, finds the uncovered periods and checks with an expert whether options exist to improve them.

She does not know what her pension will be, but she has a more regular base to build on.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Call. You tell us your work and contribution situation.
  2. Type of relationship. We define whether your relationship is as an employee or another form: contributions change accordingly.
  3. Contract and payslips. Each month the payslip shows the contributions withheld.
  4. Payments. The cooperative handles what is due to INPS and INAIL.
  5. Check. You can verify your position on the INPS portal.
  6. Documents. At year end you receive the CU certificate.

What we cannot tell you

We do not run pension simulations or forecast amounts: they depend on too many variables. Nor can we say whether one regime or another is preferable for you in pension terms: for important decisions, such as buy-backs or consolidation of periods, consult an advice centre or a pension adviser. If you work abroad or have had positions in other countries, periods may be considered under European rules or applicable treaties: to be checked on your case.

Inactive periods and continuity

Between projects there may be months without work. With a VAT number, minimum contributions or advance payments can keep weighing even without revenue, depending on the scheme. In an employment relationship in the cooperative, contributions are tied to periods actually paid; if you lose work involuntarily you can look at NASpI, which under the legal conditions is covered by notional contributions. For details, always check current rules with an advice centre.

In short

An employment relationship in the cooperative brings regular payments and less paperwork for you. To talk about your case, write to us.

Frequently asked questions

Who pays INPS contributions?

For an employment relationship the cooperative pays INPS (FPLD), with the worker's share withheld from the payslip.

Can I see my contribution record?

Yes, through the INPS contribution statement available on the institute's portal with your credentials.

Is a pension guaranteed?

No. It depends on contributions paid and the pension rules in force: we cannot promise amounts or dates.
