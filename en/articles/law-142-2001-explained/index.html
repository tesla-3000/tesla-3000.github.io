Law 142/2001 explained simply: the worker-member

Italian Law no. 142 of 3 April 2001 is the reference text for anyone working as a member of a cooperative. You do not need to be a lawyer to grasp its essence: a few ideas are enough. This guide explains them simply, with due caution: for specific cases, professional advice is always advisable.

The central idea: a dual relationship

Before the law, people working in a cooperative were in a grey area: member or worker? Law 142/2001 answers: both. Besides the membership relationship, the member enters into a “further and distinct working relationship” (art. 1, para. 3), which may be as employee, self-employed or another form.

This means two levels:

  • Membership relationship: you are part of the cooperative, take part in its life, subscribe a share of capital and contribute to decisions under the by-laws.
  • Working relationship: you carry out an activity under a contract with its protections.

The internal regulation

Another pillar: the cooperative must adopt an internal regulation, filed with the competent office (art. 6). The regulation describes how members’ work is organised and the applicable rules. It is the document that makes the law’s principles concrete.

Protections for the worker-member

The law refers to respecting the rights provided for workers. In the case of employment, in practice this means:

  • INPS contributions paid by the cooperative;
  • INAIL workplace accident insurance;
  • holiday, sickness, maternity and paternity leave;
  • severance pay (TFR);
  • NASpI unemployment benefit on involuntary job loss, under legal conditions;
  • monthly payslip and annual tax certificate (CU).

The member does not open a VAT number or keep accounts. Income is taxed as employment income, with withholding at source and year-end adjustment.

Pay

For members with an employment relationship, pay must not be lower than the minimums in the reference collective agreements. The cooperative may also distribute member refunds if there is a surplus (art. 2545-sexies of the Civil Code), but amounts and distribution are not guaranteed.

What the law does not do

It does not set every member’s conditions by itself: the individual contract, regulation and by-laws define the details. It does not make the cooperative suitable for every profession: those with a professional register and exclusive rights have their own rules. And it does not ensure work: continuity depends on assignments.

Example: Matteo, IT consultant

A fictional example: Matteo, an IT consultant, joins a cooperative. He signs the admission application and subscribes the share (membership relationship). Then he signs an employment contract (working relationship) that refers to the regulation. For the foreign client he works remotely, the cooperative invoices, he receives a payslip. If one day he wants to leave, he must consider both relationships: withdrawal as a member and the end of the employment contract. To know precisely what he is due, he reads contract and regulation.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Admission. As a member, with share and acceptance of by-laws and regulation.
  2. Employment contract. Linked to the regulation, with duties, hours and pay.
  3. Agreement. Tesla3000 signs the commercial contract with the client.
  4. Activity. The member works and reports through the platform.
  5. Pay. Monthly payslip with contributions and withholdings.

What to check

  • Type of working relationship proposed (employee, self-employed, other).
  • Reference collective agreement.
  • Rules on withdrawal and ending both relationships.
  • Any clause on member refunds.

Frequently asked questions

Is the worker-member an employee?

They can be: the member can have an employment, self-employed or other type of relationship with the cooperative. In the most common model for services it is employment.

What does dual relationship mean?

The member has a membership relationship (is part of the cooperative) and a further, distinct working relationship. The two are linked but do not coincide.

Does the law guarantee minimum pay?

The rules require pay to respect certain minimums under the applicable collective agreements. For a concrete case, check the contract and regulation.
