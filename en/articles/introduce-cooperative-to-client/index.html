How to introduce the cooperative to your foreign client

The most delicate moment is not the signature but the first conversation. Telling a foreign client “from now on the contract will be with a cooperative” can raise doubts if it is not accompanied by a clear explanation. The goal is simple: make clear that little changes for the client, and that everything becomes tidier.

Start from what the client cares about

A procurement office wants to know four things: who invoices, how much it costs, who is responsible if there is a problem, whether the paperwork is in order. Introduce the cooperative starting from these answers, not from the history of the cooperative movement.

A short formula you can adapt:

“To simplify administration, my work will be contracted through an Italian worker cooperative, Tesla3000. They will sign the contract, issue your invoices and manage my employment, insurance and payroll. My role and the scope of work stay exactly as we agreed.”

What to prepare beforehand

  • A one-page profile in English (or the client’s language) with company name, VAT number, address, administrative contact.
  • The commercial contract text, prepared by the cooperative: read it before sending.
  • Details of insurance cover (INAIL, professional liability) if the client asks.
  • Invoicing and payment terms, including currency.

Common objections and answers

“We don’t work with cooperatives.” Ask what the concern is: often it is a matter of information. Explain that the client gets a company as supplier, with a regular invoice.

“Why doesn’t the individual invoice us directly?” Because the work is organised through a structure that handles administrative obligations; for the client only the invoicing entity changes.

“Who is responsible for the work?” Contractual liability is governed by the commercial contract: read it and, if needed, negotiate it.

Example: Marco, project manager

A fictional example: Marco is a project manager who has worked for two years for a Swedish company as a freelancer. He wants to move to the cooperative but fears the client will stiffen. He prepares an email with three points: why he is changing structure, what stays the same (duties, timing, contact person), what changes (contract and invoice header). He attaches the cooperative’s profile. The client only asks for the company record and the new IBAN: the process is closed in a few days. A real client may ask more questions: that is normal.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Preparation. With the cooperative you define the message and gather documents.
  2. Telling the client. You send the email or have a short call; the cooperative can support you.
  3. Commercial contract. The cooperative sends the draft agreement to the client.
  4. Any supplier onboarding requests. The client may ask for forms or certificates: the cooperative provides them.
  5. Start. You begin under the new set-up; you work on the platform and the cooperative invoices.

What to check

  • Whether the client has a supplier accreditation procedure and how long it takes.
  • Contract clauses involving the worker (confidentiality, intellectual property).
  • Any regulatory requirements in the client’s country: to be checked case by case.

The right tone

Avoid defensive or overly technical language. Be brief, concrete, transparent. A client who understands what changes (and what does not) usually accepts without problems.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to tell the client I am a cooperative member?

Yes, it is the simplest and most transparent route: the counterparty of the commercial contract is the cooperative, so the client is informed anyway.

What if the client does not know cooperatives?

Explain it as an Italian company that handles the contract, invoicing and obligations towards the worker. A short one-page profile in English may be enough.

Can the client refuse?

Yes, some companies have internal supplier rules. If that happens, assess with the cooperative whether alternatives exist or whether the solution is not suitable.
