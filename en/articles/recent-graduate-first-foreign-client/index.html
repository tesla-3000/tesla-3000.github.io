Use cases

Recent graduate with a first foreign client: starting without a VAT number

You have just defended your thesis and a foreign company offers you a first assignment: a Northern European startup looking for data analysis, Italian content or market research. It is a great opportunity, but it immediately raises practical questions: how will they pay me? Do I need a VAT number? What happens when the project ends?

The situation of a first assignment

A first foreign client has recurring features:

  • short duration (three to six months) and modest amounts;
  • tasks still being defined: you are learning, the client is testing;
  • no track record: no previous invoices, no established portfolio;
  • a client unfamiliar with Italian contracts, who often asks “send us an invoice”.

That word, “invoice”, presupposes a VAT number, with its obligations: opening, tax regime, contributions, returns, often an accountant. For someone who does not yet know whether this activity will last, it is a non-trivial commitment.

What to know before accepting

  1. What you must deliver, in what time and how it is verified.
  2. How much time it takes: first jobs often take more hours than expected.
  3. When you are paid and in what currency.
  4. Whether the client will give feedback: for a graduate the quality of feedback is professional value.
  5. Whether you can cite the work in your CV and portfolio.
  6. Who supports you if you have doubts: a human contact helps a lot.

Building a portfolio from the first assignment

Ask the client what you may show and in what form, and keep a description of the work with objectives, tools and results without breaching confidentiality. Keep a diary of activities with dates and deliveries: it will serve for your CV and to understand how long different tasks take. Ask for written feedback at project end. If the client is satisfied, a short reference is worth more than many lines of self-presentation, and it can open the door to the second assignment.

Example: three months of analysis for a startup

A fictional example. Imagine Lorenzo, an economics graduate of two months. A Finnish startup offers him three months of market analysis on the Italian food sector: twenty hours a week, interim and final report. Lorenzo does not know whether he will continue as a freelancer and does not want to open a VAT number for one client. Through the cooperative, Tesla3000 signs the contract with the startup, Lorenzo is engaged as a worker-member with an employment contract for the project’s duration, logs activities on the platform and receives the payslip. Contributions, INAIL and professional liability are included; if he wants to continue with other clients he can consider it at the end.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Describe the offer: client, tasks, duration, pay.
  2. Membership as a member with a share of capital refundable on exit (Law 142/2001).
  3. Contract signed by the cooperative with the foreign client: the member does not sign the commercial contract.
  4. Platform: activities, expenses, documents, signing; WhatsApp and email support for doubts.
  5. Invoicing: the cooperative invoices the foreign client under B2B rules verified case by case.
  6. Training and community: access to materials and to experienced members.

And the VAT number?

It is a legitimate and sometimes convenient choice: the flat-rate regime (85,000 euro threshold, 15% substitute tax, reduced to 5% for the first five years with requirements) is designed also for those starting out, but it brings Separate Management (or other scheme) contributions, e-invoicing, returns and usually an accountant. If you foresee several clients already in the first months, opening one may make sense. With a single first assignment, an employment relationship with a payslip is often simpler.

Frequently asked questions

Can I start with no experience?

Yes, if the client has chosen you. The cooperative manages the employment relationship; the choice of professional remains the client's.

What if the relationship with the client ends after a few months?

The member's contract follows the duration of the commercial contract. At the end you receive what is due and, if eligible, can consider protections such as unemployment benefit under legal conditions.

Can I work while still an intern or student?

It depends on your situation (internships, grants, ongoing studies). Check case by case before signing.
