Regulated professions: when the cooperative is not the solution

The worker cooperative is an effective solution for many service activities, but not for all. There is an area where the model does not work, or works only in very limited cases: regulated professions. Saying so clearly saves everyone time.

What regulated professions are

They are activities whose practice requires by law registration with a professional order or register, with a qualifying exam and ethical rules: lawyers, doctors, engineers, architects, accountants, psychologists and others. For these professions the law often sets reserved activities, meaning services only registered members can perform, and personal duties towards the client and the order.

Why the cooperative model does not fit

In the cooperative model the client signs a commercial contract with the cooperative, and the member works for the cooperative under their own employment relationship. For professions with a register and exclusive rights, things get complicated:

  • The relationship is personal. The reserved professional service belongs to the registered person, with direct liability to the client.
  • Ethical rules. Independence, professional secrecy, conflicts of interest, client relationships: not always compatible with an intermediary organisation.
  • Prescribed corporate forms. Some professions may practise in professional partnerships or specific structures, with their own requirements.
  • Specific pension arrangements. Many categories have dedicated pension funds.
  • Insurance. Professional liability has particular policies and obligations.

For these reasons, professions with a register and exclusive rights cannot be handled through the worker cooperative model for the reserved activity.

What may work, with caution

A registered professional can also carry out non-reserved activities (training, general consulting, content writing, administrative or technical work not covered by a reservation). In those cases the model may work for that part, but only if:

  • the activity is not covered by legal reservations;
  • there are no incompatibilities with registration;
  • ethical rules do not prohibit it;
  • your order or an adviser confirms it.

This is not an assessment to make alone: the responsibility is yours.

Example: Anna, engineer

A fictional example: Anna is a registered engineer who signs structural calculation designs for foreign clients. She would like a structure to handle invoices and contributions. After a conversation with the cooperative and a consultation with her own order, she understands that signed design work is reserved and cannot go through the cooperative model. She then evaluates with an accountant other forms: a VAT number, an associated practice or a professional partnership. For an ancillary, non-reserved training activity, the cooperative may instead be an option, with the appropriate checks.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Preliminary conversation. You describe your activity and your registration, if any.
  2. Compatibility assessment. Tesla3000 checks whether the activity is reserved.
  3. Honest indication. If it is not suitable, we tell you plainly.
  4. Any non-reserved activity. If it exists and checks are positive, we proceed with admission and contract.
  5. Verification with the order. We advise confirming with your order or an adviser.

What to check

  • Whether your activity falls within legal reservations.
  • Your order’s ethical and incompatibility rules.
  • Forms of practice allowed under your rules.
  • Pension and insurance specific to your category.

If you are not sure whether your activity is reserved, contact us: we would rather tell you straight away if the model is not for you.

Frequently asked questions

Which professions are regulated?

Those for which practice requires registration with a professional order or register (for example lawyers, doctors, engineers, architects, accountants and others). The list and rules must be checked with the relevant order.

Can I work in a cooperative on activities other than my profession?

In some cases yes, if the activity is not covered by legal reservations and there are no incompatibilities. Check with your order and the cooperative.

What can I do if I am a registered professional?

Consider the forms provided by your professional rules (practice, professional partnership, collaboration) with your order or an adviser.
