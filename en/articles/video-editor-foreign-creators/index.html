Video editor for foreign creators and YouTubers

The creator economy runs on channels publishing at a relentless pace: two or three videos a week, with deadlines that do not move. Behind many channels there is an editor, often remote and not always in the same country. For editors, the challenge is moving from informal payments to invoices and a stable relationship. Tesla3000 helps with that.

How work with creators runs

Creators usually pay per video (with a rate tied to final length and style), by monthly package (say eight videos a month) or via a fixed retainer. Deliveries include editing, colour, audio, subtitles, thumbnails and social versions (shorts, reels). More structured creators have a manager or a company: they ask for an invoice and a contract. Smaller ones often pay informally, which is where formalising becomes the issue.

Agree on: maximum video length, revision rounds, delivery formats, tools (Premiere, DaVinci, Final Cut), when raw files are returned and storage space.

The cooperative’s role

The cooperative signs the agreement with the creator or their company and invoices. For EU clients the reverse charge normally applies (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72); for non-EU clients as generic B2B services outside Italian VAT: checked case by case. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have a work relationship with INPS contributions, INAIL and, if employment, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. No VAT number needed.

If the creator is an individual without a company, we check case by case how to set up invoicing.

Typical pitfalls

  • Informal payments. Without a contract, no protection if the creator does not pay.
  • Endless revisions. A “nearly done” video drags for weeks: cap the rounds.
  • Music rights. Music and third-party footage need licences; define who is responsible.
  • Peaks. A viral video or launch blows up volumes: set rush rates.
  • Raws and archive. Who keeps files and for how long.

Example

Example: imagine Matteo, a video editor from Verona, cutting six videos a month for a tech channel run by a small company in Toronto. He used to be paid by PayPal with no contract. Now the cooperative signs the agreement with the company: six videos of 10 to 15 minutes, two revision rounds, delivery within 72 hours of raw footage. Matteo logs deliveries on the platform and receives a payslip; the cooperative invoices in Canadian dollars. Fictional character.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Intro call. You present creator, volumes, rates.
  2. Joining. Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
  3. Agreement. Contract with the creator or their company.
  4. Activity. Deliveries and hours on the platform.
  5. Invoice and collection. The cooperative invoices and collects, also in foreign currency.
  6. Payslip. Monthly pay and documents online.

INAIL, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

Compared with a VAT number

The flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) is popular with editors who have many small clients: autonomy and low costs, but contributions, invoices and returns are yours. The cooperative is an alternative for those who want protections and less bureaucracy.

Checklist before signing

Before starting with a creator, agree: a paid test video; the style guide (fonts, graphics, music, pacing); the procedure for sending raw footage and receiving feedback; the maximum delivery time; how many revision rounds are included. Ask who is responsible if a video gets a copyright claim for music or third-party clips. If the creator grows, review rates and volumes every three months. Put payment times in writing too: many creators receive revenue late and risk pushing your pay back.

Frequently asked questions

Can the foreign creator pay me via platforms like PayPal?

With the cooperative, payment is made to the cooperative by bank transfer against an invoice. For small creators suitable arrangements are agreed.

Can I work with several creators at once?

In general yes, with one agreement each and within confidentiality and any exclusivity.

Who owns the project files?

It is set in the agreement: typically the creator gets the final videos and sometimes the source files. Put it in writing.
