Leaving the cooperative: withdrawal and ending the relationship

No working relationship lasts forever, and it pays to know in advance how yours ends. Leaving a cooperative means closing two things: the membership relationship and the working one. Doing it in an orderly way protects you, the client and the cooperative.

Two relationships, two closures

The member is tied to the cooperative by a membership relationship (participation, share capital) and by a working relationship (employee, self-employed or other), under Law 142/2001. Exit rules are found in three places: the by-laws (for membership), the internal regulation and the employment contract (for the work). Read them all, because the two relationships do not always end together or on the same timetable.

Ways to exit

  • Resignation from the employment with the required notice.
  • Withdrawal as a member, under the by-laws’ conditions.
  • Expiry of a fixed-term contract, if the work was fixed-term.
  • End of the client agreement: it does not automatically end the relationship, but may affect the contract.
  • Other causes provided by by-laws and regulation (for example disciplinary measures or expulsion).

What happens afterwards

Pay and severance

The final payslip includes accrued entitlements (untaken holiday, accruals) and, under the rules, severance (TFR). Timing and method should be asked about beforehand.

Share capital

The share is refundable under the by-laws. Check timing, any set-offs and required documents.

Documents

Ask for end-of-relationship documents: annual tax certificate (CU), final payslips, any statements of work performed.

Unemployment benefit (NASpI)

In case of involuntary job loss, NASpI may be due under legal conditions. Voluntary resignation generally does not qualify: there are exceptions, to be checked with a pension advice office.

The client: managing the handover

The agreement belongs to the cooperative, so you cannot take the client with you “automatically”. The handover must be regulated:

  • Transparent communication. Inform the cooperative first.
  • Notice period. It lets you finish ongoing work and hand over.
  • Replacement or closure. The cooperative decides with the client whether to replace you, close the agreement or agree other solutions.
  • Any non-compete or non-solicitation clauses. Check whether they limit direct dealings with the client after you leave.

Example: Federico, consultant

A fictional example: Federico wants to reopen his freelance VAT number after two years. He rereads contract and regulation: thirty days’ notice, a handover required, no non-compete clause. He gives written notice to the cooperative, agrees an end date with the client, and prepares documentation for the replacement. He requests the share refund and receives a final payslip with end-of-relationship entitlements. He closes open issues with a checklist and can restart his own activity.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Written notice. You send the request to Tesla3000 stating the desired date.
  2. Checking terms and notice. The cooperative confirms dates and procedures.
  3. Client handling. Handover or closure of the agreement is agreed.
  4. Administrative closure. Final hours, last payslip, severance, documents.
  5. Share refund. Under the by-laws, with set timing and method.

What to check

  • Notice and form of communication (written, certified email).
  • Non-compete or non-solicitation clauses.
  • Timing of severance and share payout.
  • Any sums owed to the cooperative.

Better to think about the exit when you join: it helps you sign with more awareness. Contact us for the details that apply to your case.

Frequently asked questions

Can I leave whenever I want?

Withdrawal is possible under the by-laws, regulation and contract, often with notice. Terms and procedures must be read in the documents and respected.

What happens to my severance pay (TFR)?

For employment, TFR accrues under legal and contractual rules; how it is paid out should be checked with the cooperative.

What happens to the client?

The agreement belongs to the cooperative: handover, closure or replacement must be managed according to the commercial contract's clauses.
