Journalist and content writer for foreign publications: getting paid

A London news site wants someone to cover Italian fashion, a German magazine needs a local politics correspondent, an American travel site wants guides written by people who actually live there. For writers, the work exists. The problem is admin: the foreign newsroom does not know how to pay an Italian author without a VAT number and often asks for an invoice.

The work in practice

Journalism and content-writing collaborations have a recognisable structure:

  • accepted pitch: you propose a topic, the editor approves and sets length and deadline;
  • per-piece rate (per article or per word) or a monthly package of content;
  • editing with one or two rounds of changes, then fact-checking;
  • payment on publication or 30-60 days after delivery.

It is a model with many small clients and variable amounts. Three recurring difficulties are late payments, last-minute cuts (“we will move it to the next issue”) and the transfer of rights.

Rights and publication

Before accepting, clarify three points. First, whether the text is “work for hire” (the publication owns it) or whether you keep reuse rights. Second, whether there is an exclusivity period on a subject. Third, how your byline is credited and whether you can use the piece in your portfolio. Copyright in creative works has specific rules to be verified case by case, and the contract with the publication deserves a careful read.

Example: a part-time correspondent

A fictional example. Imagine Luca, who writes about food and lives in Bologna. A Canadian magazine commissions four articles a month on Emilian food supply chains, paid monthly. Luca already has two collaborations with Italian outlets and a part-time editing job. Through the cooperative, the contract with the magazine is signed by the cooperative; Luca logs deliveries and research hours on the platform and receives a monthly payslip including INPS contributions. When the magazine pays late in dollars, an advance from the fund keeps the month’s expenses off his own account.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Write to us with the publication’s name, the proposed rate and the type of collaboration.
  2. We check that the activity fits the chosen form of work (employee, self-employed or other, art. 1 para. 3 of Law 142/2001) and whether a specific qualification is needed.
  3. Join as a member with a share of capital refundable on exit.
  4. Commercial contract signed by Tesla3000 with the publication: rates, deadlines, assignment of rights.
  5. Day to day: you log deliveries and expenses (interview travel, database subscriptions) on the platform; support by WhatsApp and email.
  6. Payslip and collection: the cooperative invoices, in foreign currency too; you receive the payslip with contributions, INAIL cover and professional liability insurance included in the package.

VAT number or cooperative

A journalist with many small clients can stay in the flat-rate regime (85,000 euro threshold, 15% substitute tax, 5% in the first five years with requirements), paying Separate Management contributions and handling invoices and returns. It is a valid choice, especially with high revenue and stable clients. Working through the cooperative moves the paperwork to the structure and offers holidays, sick leave, maternity and unemployment benefit under legal conditions; in exchange you work inside an employment contract rather than as an entrepreneur of yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be registered with the journalists' professional body?

Not necessarily to write as a content writer or correspondent for foreign outlets. If the activity requires a professional journalist qualification, the rules of the professional body must be checked separately.

How is per-piece payment handled?

The cooperative agrees a per-article or package rate with the publication and invoices it; you receive a payslip reflecting the activities you logged.

Do I keep the rights to my texts?

It depends on the contract with the publication. State in writing whether rights are assigned, whether exclusively and for how long.
