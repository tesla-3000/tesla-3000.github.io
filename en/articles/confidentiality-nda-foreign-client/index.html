Confidentiality and NDAs with a foreign client

Before work begins, many foreign clients ask for an NDA (non-disclosure agreement) to be signed. It is not a sign of distrust: it is standard practice, especially in fields like software, finance, research and marketing. Understanding what it implies saves you from unintended mistakes.

What it is and what it covers

An NDA commits the signatory not to disclose, or use for other purposes, the confidential information received. Typically it defines:

  • What is confidential: data, code, strategies, client lists, prices, internal documents.
  • What is not: information already public, already known to the recipient, or lawfully obtained from others.
  • How it may be used: only to carry out the work under the assignment.
  • Duration: for the whole relationship and often for a period afterwards.
  • What happens at the end: return or deletion of materials.
  • Exceptions: legal obligations, requests from authorities.
  • Consequences: penalties or damages for breach.

Who is bound when there is a cooperative

The contracting party is the cooperative, but the one accessing the information is the member. It is therefore normal for the NDA to be signed by the cooperative and for the member to commit in turn, through the employment contract, regulation or a specific declaration. In some cases the client asks the member to sign directly as well. This should be clarified beforehand, because it changes personal liability.

How to respect it day to day

The risk is not deliberate disclosure but small slips:

  • Separate environments. Computers, accounts and folders per client.
  • Do not use unauthorised tools. Personal chats, private cloud storage, AI tools not approved for uploading client documents.
  • Watch public spaces. Screens visible in a café, calls in crowded places.
  • Clean finished material. Remove sensitive data from examples and screenshots.
  • Do not talk on social media about what you do for whom.
  • Report incidents at once (file sent by mistake, lost device): hiding makes it worse.

Watch for hidden clauses

  • A definition of “confidential information” that is too broad (everything the client says).
  • Unlimited duration.
  • Disproportionate penalties.
  • Foreign governing law and forum for disputes.
  • Bans that prevent you from working for other clients in the same sector (that is a non-compete, not an NDA).

Example: Tommaso, financial analyst

A fictional example: Tommaso works for a foreign investment firm. The NDA provides that client data stays confidential for five years after the relationship ends. Tommaso reads it with the cooperative, notes that the definition of “confidential information” includes “any knowledge acquired”, and asks for a clarification that it excludes general skills and public information. The client accepts. During the work, Tommaso uses a dedicated computer, avoids uploading data to external tools and ends each session by deleting temporary files. A lost device is reported immediately, following the procedure.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Receiving the NDA. You send it to Tesla3000 before signing.
  2. Review. The cooperative reads definitions, duration, penalties and governing law.
  3. Any changes. They are proposed to the client.
  4. Signature and member’s commitment. The NDA is signed by the cooperative; the member commits under the regulation or contract.
  5. Filing. The document is kept on the platform.

What to check

  • Who signs and with what personal obligations.
  • Definition of confidential information, duration and penalties.
  • Rules on personal data, if the work involves third parties’ data (data protection law must be respected; check case by case).

Frequently asked questions

Who signs the NDA, me or the cooperative?

It depends on the client. Usually the agreement is between client and cooperative, and the member is bound to respect it through their contract or a specific declaration. It should be agreed in advance.

How long does the obligation last?

The contract sets it: it may last for the whole relationship and continue for a period afterwards. Check the stated duration.

Can I mention the client in my portfolio?

Only if the contract allows it. If in doubt, ask for written permission.
