Patronage refunds (ristorni): how they work in a cooperative

Among the words you hear when talking about cooperatives, ristorno (patronage refund) is one of the least clear. Yet it is a distinctive feature: a way of returning to members, in proportion to their work, a share of the positive results of management. Here is how it works, without promising anything we cannot guarantee.

What a patronage refund is

A ristorno is a way of distributing among members part of the management surplus arising from mutualistic exchanges, that is, from the work members performed with or through the cooperative. The reference basis is Article 2545-sexies of the Civil Code, which provides that the articles of association or by-laws set the distribution criteria, in proportion to the quantity and quality of mutualistic exchanges.

It is not an extra salary and not a dividend: a dividend rewards invested capital, a ristorno recognises the member’s work contribution.

When there can be a refund

Several conditions are needed: management must close with a surplus, the members’ assembly must approve the accounts and resolve on use of surpluses, and the by-laws or regulation must provide clear criteria. Part of the surplus may also go to reserves, which strengthen the cooperative, or to other purposes provided by law. That is why it can never be said in advance whether, when and how much will be distributed.

Example: year-end refunds

Example: imagine the cooperative closes the year with a management surplus. The assembly approves the accounts and decides, under the regulation, to allocate part to members as a refund, in proportion to hours or fees worked during the year. Dario worked all year on several engagements, Elisa only six months: the distribution criterion takes each one’s work into account, so Dario’s share is proportionally larger. In the example we give no figures, because they depend on the result, assembly decisions and the regulation.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Rules in advance. By-laws and regulation set out the criteria for any refund.
  2. Recorded activity. Your work is tracked on the platform and forms the basis of the calculation.
  3. Accounts. At year end the management result is determined.
  4. Resolution. The assembly approves the accounts and decides whether and how to distribute surpluses.
  5. Distribution. If provided, the refund is allocated to members under the criteria.
  6. Communication. You receive information on the amount and applicable tax treatment.

Tax and principle

Refunds may have specific tax treatment and, if paid in connection with employment relationships, be classified under particular rules. We do not go into detail or run simulations: they depend on the form of distribution and your situation. So when you receive a refund, you will be given the necessary information.

A point of principle: it should not be regarded as expected income. The member’s employment relationship rests on the agreed pay. The refund, if any, is an additional recognition depending on the result achieved by everyone.

Why it makes sense

The refund puts mutuality into practice: those who work create value and, if management goes well, part can return to those who produced it. It is an incentive for prudent management and participation.

Reading the figures when they come

If a refund is distributed, take a few minutes to understand it. Ask which criterion was used (hours, fees or another measure), what period it covers and how it appears in your documents. Compare it with your own records on the platform to check that your work was counted correctly. If something does not match, ask support right away: corrections are easier when the data is fresh. Understanding the mechanism also helps you make sense of the accounts at the assembly.

Questions?

Frequently asked questions

Are patronage refunds guaranteed?

No. They can be distributed only if management produces surpluses and the assembly decides so, under the by-laws and regulation. No amount can be promised.

How are they calculated?

Normally in proportion to the quantity and quality of mutualistic exchanges, that is the member's work, under the criteria in the regulation.

Are they like dividends?

No. Dividends reward capital; patronage refunds share with members part of the mutualistic advantage in proportion to work, not to the capital quota.
