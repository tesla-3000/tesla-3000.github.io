The internal regulation of a worker cooperative

When you sign up to a worker cooperative, there is a document few people read in full that nevertheless has a concrete weight on working life: the internal regulation. Understanding what it contains helps avoid surprises.

Why it exists

Law 142/2001 provides that the cooperative adopts an internal regulation defining how members’ work is organised. The regulation must be filed with the competent office (art. 6). In other words: besides the by-laws, which govern the company, there is a text dedicated to how members’ work operates.

Remember that a member has a double relationship: as an associate (participation in the cooperative) and as a worker (employee, self-employed or other). The regulation describes the work side.

What it typically governs

Content varies, but usually you find:

  • Types of employment relationship that can be set up with the member.
  • Work organisation: how assignments are allocated, hours, places.
  • Pay: criteria for setting pay, reference to the applicable collective agreement.
  • Timesheets, holiday, leave, sickness.
  • Expense reimbursement and how to claim.
  • Rights and duties: confidentiality, use of equipment, conduct towards the client.
  • Disciplinary measures, consistent with the rules.
  • Withdrawal and end of the relationship, both employment and membership.
  • Member refunds: general criteria for any distribution, with no promise of amounts.

Difference between by-laws and regulation

The by-laws govern the cooperative as a company: members, bodies, capital, meetings. The regulation governs members’ employment. The individual contract is the third level: the person’s specifics (duties, hours, pay, duration). If in doubt about a concrete issue, check the contract first, then the regulation, then the by-laws.

Example: Roberta, virtual assistant

A fictional example: Roberta is about to join and asks for the regulation. She reads it with three questions: how are hours logged, what happens in case of sick leave, how is withdrawal communicated. She finds that hours are logged on the platform and withdrawal requires written notice. On one point, reporting of hours, she has a doubt: she asks support, gets an answer and saves the clarification. She joins with fewer uncertainties.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Delivery. Tesla3000 provides the regulation before or together with the admission application.
  2. Reading. The prospective member reads it calmly, highlighting unclear parts.
  3. Clarifications. Support answers by email or WhatsApp.
  4. Acceptance. With the application, the member declares acceptance of by-laws and regulation.
  5. Updates. If the regulation changes, the cooperative communicates changes under the set procedures.

What to check

  • How pay is calculated and which collective agreement is referenced.
  • Rules on holiday, leave and sickness.
  • Procedure and notice for withdrawal.
  • Any exclusivity obligations or limits on other activities.
  • Disciplinary sanctions and how they are raised.

A practical tip

Save the regulation as a PDF with the delivery date, and keep later versions too. You will need it if one day you want to know on the basis of which text a decision was taken.

Frequently asked questions

Is the regulation mandatory?

For worker cooperatives, Law 142/2001 provides for an internal regulation, which must be filed. Its concrete content varies between cooperatives.

Can the regulation worsen collective agreement rights?

The law sets limits: minimum pay and protections must respect the applicable rules. If in doubt, ask the cooperative or an adviser for clarification.

Can I ask for a copy before joining?

Yes, and reading it before signing the admission application is recommended.
