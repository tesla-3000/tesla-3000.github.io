Virtual assistant for foreign entrepreneurs: structuring the work

A Canadian founder runs a consulting firm alone and spends half her day on email, calendars and invoices. A German real-estate entrepreneur wants someone to follow appointments with Italian suppliers. The virtual assistant is the answer, but you need a contractual form that satisfies both sides: the worker wants stability, the payer wants simplicity.

What the role includes

A virtual assistant is not a single role. Typical activities are:

  • managing diary and inbox, triaging requests;
  • bookings of travel and meetings across time zones;
  • light admin: chasing invoices, preparing monthly reports;
  • research and document preparation;
  • handling relationships with Italian suppliers, where language is an advantage.

The most frequent agreement is a monthly hour package (say twenty or forty hours) with an end-of-month report. Payment is regular, but requests are not: quiet weeks and urgent peaks.

Common mistakes

  1. Vague scope: if “general assistance” means everything, it ends up including the founder’s personal errands. Better a list of included tasks.
  2. Confidentiality: the assistant accesses sensitive data; you need a written clause and proper use of shared tools.
  3. Availability: expecting replies in minutes is not compatible with a ten-hour package.
  4. Untracked hours: without a log, disputes are inevitable.

How to work well with several clients

Prepare a sheet for each client with preferences, email tone, hours they can be reached and what can be decided without asking. Use reply templates and recurring checklists to cut time per task. Hold a short weekly alignment call and send a written summary at month end: it is the best way to justify hours and renew the assignment. Always keep calendars, accounts and documents of different clients separate to avoid mistakes and respect confidentiality.

Example: three entrepreneurs, one payslip

A fictional example. Imagine Giulia, a former executive secretary. She has three clients: a Canadian consultant (20 hours a month), a German estate agent (30 hours) and a small London architecture studio (10 hours). With a VAT number she would issue three invoices each month in three currencies. Through the cooperative, each client signs a contract with Tesla3000, Giulia logs the hours for each on the platform and receives a single payslip. The client knows who gets paid, and she keeps no books.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Analysis: we list clients, hours and currencies.
  2. Membership with a share of capital refundable on exit (Law 142/2001).
  3. Separate contracts for each entrepreneur, with hour package and confidentiality.
  4. Hour log on the platform: the client can check how much was worked.
  5. Invoicing: the cooperative invoices foreign clients under the B2B rules to be verified case by case, in foreign currency too.
  6. Monthly payslip with contributions, INAIL and professional liability; advance available if a client pays late.

The VAT number route

Many virtual assistants choose the flat-rate regime (85,000 euro threshold, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if requirements are met), which with modest revenue is very efficient and leaves freedom on rates. The trade-off: Separate Management contributions, e-invoicing also towards foreign clients, returns, an accountant, and no holiday or sick-leave protection. With few stable clients it can be enough. With many small ones, the cooperative reduces the admin load.

Frequently asked questions

Can I work for several entrepreneurs at once?

Yes, with several contracts, respecting confidentiality and any exclusivity clauses.

Are unused hours lost?

It depends on the agreement. For hour packages, decide in advance whether unused hours roll over or expire at month end.

How are client credentials handled?

With a shared password manager provided by the client and no personal accounts. It is good practice to write this into the agreement.
