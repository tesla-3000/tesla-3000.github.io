Invoicing EU and non-EU clients: the basic VAT rules

When the client is abroad, the invoice looks like a puzzle: VAT or not? With what wording? To whom? This guide gives a general picture and does not replace a tax adviser’s opinion: every transaction must be checked case by case.

A premise that simplifies

If you work as an employee-member of a cooperative, you are not the one invoicing. The invoice is issued by the cooperative, which holds the VAT number and is the client’s counterparty. You need no VAT registration, e-invoicing or ledgers. Knowing the basics is still useful to understand what the cooperative writes in the agreement and how payment is organised.

The general criterion: place of supply for B2B services

For services between businesses (B2B), in principle VAT is due in the client’s country. In Italy this rule is in Article 7-ter of Presidential Decree 633/1972. The practical consequences depend on where the client is.

Client in another EU country

If the client is a VAT-registered business in the EU, the generic B2B service is not taxed in Italy; the client accounts for VAT in its own country through the reverse-charge mechanism. The invoice carries the required indications and the client’s VAT number, which should be verified. EU reporting obligations may also apply, handled by the invoicing party.

Client outside the EU

For a generic B2B service supplied to a client outside the EU, the transaction is normally outside the scope of Italian VAT. The invoice carries the wording about lack of territorial connection. Taxes or withholdings may apply in the client’s country, to be checked locally.

Private client (B2C)

Rules change and depend on the type of service. In these cases a specific check is needed.

Cases that need attention

  • special services (training, intermediation, services tied to property or events): they may follow different rules;
  • clients without a VAT number or with unclear status;
  • withholding taxes applied by the foreign country;
  • double taxation treaties.

Example: Chiara, copywriter

A fictional example: Chiara writes content for a German and a Canadian company. The cooperative receives the two clients’ details from Chiara and verifies the EU VAT number (for the German one) and company name (for the Canadian one). It issues two invoices with the appropriate wording, one in euros and one in Canadian dollars, agreeing in advance how exchange is handled. Chiara sees payment status on the platform and receives her monthly payslip without dealing with invoices. For cases like this, confirmation from an adviser is always advisable.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Collecting client details. Name, address, country, VAT number (if EU).
  2. Verification. The cooperative checks the client’s status and the VAT treatment to apply.
  3. Agreement. Currency, payment terms and invoicing details are specified.
  4. Invoice. The cooperative issues invoices at the agreed frequency.
  5. Collection. Once payment is recorded, the cooperative runs payroll; with the advance fund, timing can be shortened.

What to check

  • The client’s VAT number and its validity.
  • Nature of the service and its place of supply.
  • Currency, exchange rate and bank fees.
  • Any withholdings in the client’s country.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to invoice the foreign client myself?

No, if you work as an employee-member: the invoice is issued by the cooperative, which is the counterparty of the commercial contract.

Is VAT always charged?

Rules depend on the type of service, the client's status (business or consumer) and the country. For generic B2B services the principle is that taxation falls in the client's country, but each case must be checked.

Can I be paid in dollars or another currency?

The cooperative can invoice and collect in foreign currency; details on exchange rates and fees must be agreed and verified.
