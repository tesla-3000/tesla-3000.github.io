NASpI: protection when your work comes to an end

The end of a project or a relationship is a delicate moment for anyone. For the self-employed there is normally no equivalent safety net. For an employee there is NASpI, the monthly unemployment benefit managed by INPS. Here is what it means for those working with Tesla3000.

What NASpI is

NASpI is a financial benefit supporting those who involuntarily lose employee work. It is granted under the conditions set by law: unemployment status, and contribution and work requirements in the reference period. Duration and amount are calculated under the rules in force, which may change: that is why we give no figures and refer you to the INPS website or an advice centre.

The point of interest to a worker-member is this: if your employment relationship with the cooperative is as an employee and ends against your will, for example because the engagement ends or activity is reduced, you can consider applying for NASpI, if you meet the requirements.

Why it matters for those working with foreign clients

Relationships with foreign clients are often tied to a project: once it ends, the client may not renew. For a freelancer with a VAT number, that simply means invoices stop. For a member with an employment relationship, the involuntary end of the relationship may give rise, under legal conditions, to temporary financial support while looking for new opportunities. It is not insurance against every event: it depends on the requirements, the type of termination and the contribution history.

Example: Roberto and the end of the engagement

Example: imagine Roberto, a developer, employed through the cooperative on a one-year engagement with a Finnish company. The client does not renew and the engagement ends. The cooperative has no other suitable activities for him at that moment and the relationship ends.

Roberto receives his end-of-employment entitlements, including TFR, and checks with an advice centre whether he qualifies for NASpI. If the requirements are met, he applies to INPS within the deadline. Meanwhile he stays in the members’ community and looks for a new project, with the cooperative’s help too.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Termination notice. The cooperative handles termination formalities and sends the mandatory notifications.
  2. Final documents. You receive the last payslip, end-of-employment entitlements and documents for INPS.
  3. NASpI application. You submit it to INPS, online or via an advice centre, within the deadlines.
  4. Requirement check. INPS assesses your position; the cooperative does not decide the outcome.
  5. New opportunity. You can remain a member and propose or receive new engagements.

What not to forget

NASpI is not guaranteed: it depends on requirements and the reason for termination. Voluntary resignation, as a rule, does not qualify, except in cases provided by law. There are deadlines for applying, and missing them can compromise the right: enquire as soon as the relationship ends. While receiving the benefit, obligations apply, such as reporting any new work activity.

If you work abroad or have had relationships in other EU countries, European coordination rules may come into play: an advice centre can help you check them.

Getting ready before the engagement ends

The most useful move is to anticipate: as the engagement nears its end, talk to the cooperative about renewals or new activities. Update your profile, recontact past clients and look at opportunities in the members’ community. Keep useful documents to hand, such as payslips and the CU certificate, and note the termination date: you will need them if you decide to apply to INPS.

Frequently asked questions

Who is entitled to NASpI?

Employees who lose work involuntarily, provided they meet the legal requirements on contributions and unemployment status. Requirements must be checked on the case.

If I resign, do I get NASpI?

In general voluntary resignation does not qualify, except in particular cases provided by law, such as just cause. Always check first with an advice centre.

Who submits the application?

The worker applies to INPS, normally online or through an advice centre, within the legal deadlines.
