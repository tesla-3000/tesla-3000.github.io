Graphic designer with UK clients: contracts and rights

The British market is close and attractive to Italian designers: branding studios in London, agencies in Manchester, scale-ups looking for a remote art director. The difficulty is practical: clients want a supplier with a clear invoice and contract, while you want to spend your time on the project. With Tesla3000 the admin moves to the cooperative.

How work with British clients tends to run

Payments are often per project with a deposit (typically a share at kickoff and the balance on delivery), or per day (a day rate), common in the UK for remote studio work. Typical deliverables are logos, brand identities, social templates, editorial layouts and interfaces. Put in writing:

  • how many revision rounds are included;
  • delivery formats (source files or exports only);
  • client approval timelines;
  • licensing for fonts and images.

Rights and licences

File ownership is the delicate part. A British client usually wants full ownership of commissioned work. Copyright assignment and licensing follow specific rules and must be checked before signing: the agreement defines what is assigned, for which uses and for how long. We do not promise one formula for everyone; we review it case by case.

Contract and invoices

Since Brexit the UK is outside the EU: for generic B2B services the supply is normally not subject to Italian VAT, and the cooperative checks the treatment case by case. It signs the agreement and issues the invoice in pounds; as a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have an employment relationship with social security, INAIL, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. The client pays the cooperative; your pay follows payroll.

Typical design pitfalls

  • Scope creep. Endless revisions: set the rounds in the agreement.
  • Forgotten deposits. Even with a cooperative, new clients should usually pay a deposit.
  • Payments in pounds. Exchange rates affect what is collected; agree conversion timing.
  • Portfolio. Ask whether and when you can show the work.

Example

Example: imagine Giulia, a brand designer in Turin, commissioned by a Bristol studio to rework the identity of a coffee-shop chain: 12 days of work over three weeks at a day rate. The cooperative signs the agreement with the studio, providing for two revision rounds and delivery of final files. Giulia logs her days on the platform and submits expenses for test prints; at month end the cooperative invoices in pounds and, after collection, runs payroll. Fictional character.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Intro call. Describe client, scope, days, rate.
  2. Joining. Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
  3. Agreement. Commercial contract between the cooperative and the UK studio.
  4. Activity. Attendance, deliverables and expenses on the platform.
  5. Invoicing. Invoice and collection, also in foreign currency; then payslip.
  6. Protection. INAIL cover and professional liability insurance included; WhatsApp and email support.

The VAT-number alternative

The flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax or 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) is competitive if you have many clients and want full control; but you handle e-invoicing, returns and contributions. If you work with a few studios and want to focus on design, the cooperative may suit you better.

Checklist before signing

Before starting a project with a British client, prepare: a written brief with goals, audience and references; the quote split by phase (research, concept, development, delivery); deposit and balance terms; a list of what is excluded (printing, photography, translations, trademark registration); final formats and font licences. For logos, remember that prior-rights searches and registration are separate activities. A detailed quote protects you and the client: when a new request arrives, everyone knows whether it is included or quoted separately.

Frequently asked questions

Is the UK treated as a non-EU client?

Yes, after Brexit the UK is a third country. Generic B2B services are normally outside the scope of Italian VAT, checked case by case.

Can I be paid in pounds?

The cooperative can invoice and collect in foreign currency. Your pay on the payslip remains in euro.

Who owns the rights to my design work?

It depends on the commercial agreement and your contract. Copyright assignment has specific rules and should be checked before you start.
