Interpreter for trade fairs and conferences abroad: working properly

Interpreters work in waves. A month full of trade fairs (Frankfurt, Milan, Barcelona), then two quiet weeks, then a four-day medical congress. Foreign organisers and event agencies look for Italian native-speaking professionals, but rarely have the structure to deal with an occasional invoicing freelancer holding an Italian VAT number.

Types of work

  • Consecutive interpreting in business negotiations, company visits, fair stands.
  • Simultaneous interpreting from the booth, always in pairs, with turns of roughly thirty minutes.
  • Whispered interpreting (chuchotage) for small delegations.
  • Sight translation of documents during meetings.

Everything changes with the type of day. In the booth you need glossaries and materials well in advance; at a stand you need stamina and the ability to adapt to the exhibitor’s pace.

What to negotiate with the organiser

The contract should state clearly:

  1. the rate for full day, half day and overtime;
  2. preparation materials: whether study time is paid or included;
  3. cancellation: what happens if the event is called off days before;
  4. travel and lodging: arranged by the client or reimbursed;
  5. recording of the performance and rights over the audio, which often require written consent.

Late cancellations are the most common sore point: without a clause, the lost day is entirely on the interpreter.

An overlooked trap: overlapping dates

Anyone working for several organisers risks accepting two events in the same period. Keep one calendar, block dates as soon as you have written confirmation, and tell the agency promptly about “pending” options. By logging availability on the platform, the cooperative sees overlaps in advance and can help you manage them with the client, for example by proposing a colleague from the member community as a replacement.

Example: three events in a quarter

A fictional example. Imagine Silvia, an Italian-English-Spanish interpreter. An events agency in Madrid offers her seven days at a food trade fair, then a London agency three days of simultaneous interpreting at a congress. In between she has occasional jobs for Italian companies. Through the cooperative each contract is signed by Tesla3000 with the organiser; Silvia logs her days on the platform and uploads train and hotel receipts as expense claims. At month end she gets a single payslip covering the activities for all clients, with no invoices to issue in three languages.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Tell us your calendar of upcoming fairs and your clients.
  2. Membership: share of capital and internal regulation (Law 142/2001).
  3. Contract per event or a framework agreement with the agency: rates, cancellation, travel.
  4. Logging: days and expenses on the platform, with digital signing of documents.
  5. Pay: monthly payslip with INPS contributions and INAIL cover; professional liability insurance included.
  6. Invoicing abroad: for organisers in other countries the cooperative invoices applying the B2B rules, to be verified case by case.

Weighing the alternatives

With a flat-rate VAT number (revenue under 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if requirements are met) the interpreter has maximum freedom on rates and clients, but handles e-invoices, returns and Separate Management contributions. Some choose a mixed solution: a VAT number for stable clients, the cooperative for foreign events where organisers want a structured counterpart. If your work is very discontinuous, having a payslip that follows the days logged simplifies personal bookkeeping.

If your calendar is full of events abroad, talk to us through the contact page: we will see how to manage contracts and travel.

Frequently asked questions

How are conference days paid?

Usually by the day or half day, at a rate agreed in the contract with the organiser. The cooperative invoices and the member receives a payslip for the days logged.

Are trips abroad covered?

Travel, board and lodging are agreed in the contract and reported as expense claims. Rules on cover and tax treatment of trips must be checked case by case.

Can I work for several organisers at once?

Yes, generally with several contracts; declaring your availability helps avoid overlapping dates.
