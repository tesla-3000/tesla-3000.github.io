Dancers and choreographers on tour: contracts and protection

A contemporary dance company leaves for six weeks with stops in Spain, Germany and Poland; a choreographer creates a piece for a Dutch festival; a dancer is hired for a residency show in Paris. Professional life is made of fixed-term contracts, intense rehearsals and travel. Tesla3000 provides a structure that adds continuity and protection.

How an engagement works

Dancer engagements usually follow a pattern: a rehearsal period (often at a reduced or daily rate), performances paid per show or weekly, and touring with a per diem or reimbursement of meals and lodging. For choreographers, fees concern creation (fixed or per studio day), any revivals and rights linked to the choreographic work. The contract should specify: rehearsal and performance dates, working hours, rest days, transfers, insurance and responsibility for injuries.

The cooperative’s role

The cooperative signs the agreement with the company or festival, invoices and collects. The worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) has a work relationship with the cooperative: if employment, INPS contributions, INAIL, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. The performing-arts sector has specific social security and contractual rules: classification is checked with us case by case before departure. Rights in choreographic works are also a specific matter, to be verified. For EU clients the reverse charge normally applies (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72); for non-EU clients as generic B2B services outside Italian VAT: to be verified.

Typical pitfalls

  • Unpaid rehearsals. Define what is paid and at what rate.
  • Injuries. Frequent in dance: check INAIL cover and who bears recovery time.
  • Tour reimbursements. Meals, lodging and travel must be agreed and documented.
  • Costumes and shoes. Who pays for professional materials?
  • Choreographic rights. Who holds the rights to the piece and how it is reused.
  • Work permits. Outside the EU, visas or authorisations may be needed.

Example

Example: imagine Elisa, a dancer from Reggio Emilia, hired by a German company for six weeks of rehearsals and fourteen performances in three countries. The agreement between cooperative and company sets rehearsals, shows, reimbursements and rest days. Elisa logs days and expenses on the platform, the cooperative invoices the company and she receives a monthly payslip, with INAIL cover during work. Fictional character.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Intro call. You present engagement, dates, locations, fee.
  2. Check. We review classification, collective agreement and permits.
  3. Joining. Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
  4. Agreement. Contract with the company.
  5. Activity. Rehearsals, shows and expenses on the platform.
  6. Invoicing and payslip. The cooperative invoices and collects; you receive the payslip. The fund advance helps between tour stops.

INAIL, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

Compared with a VAT number

For those on short projects with many clients, the flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) remains an option, with contributions and admin on you. For long engagements with worker protections, the cooperative deserves a comparison.

Checklist before signing

Before a tour, check: the full calendar with stops and transfers; accommodation and per diem; working conditions in the theatre (warm-up, floor, load-in times); procedures in case of injury and recovery period; reimbursement for transporting personal gear or costumes. For a choreographer, specify what is created, for whom, for how many performances and how revivals are handled. Keep a copy of travel documents and contracts with you at all times. Share the calendar with us so we can plan payslips and advances.

Frequently asked questions

How do injuries during rehearsals and performances work?

INAIL cover is included for the work relationship. For injuries and specific benefits, conditions are checked case by case.

Can I be choreographer and dancer in the same production?

Yes, they are two roles with different fees and rights. The agreement distinguishes them.

How are tours in different countries handled?

Tax, social security and permits depend on the country and duration. Each tour is assessed case by case.
