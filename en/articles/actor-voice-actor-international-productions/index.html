Actors and voice actors in international productions

An Italian actor may be called for a supporting role in a series shot in Prague, a voice for a video game produced in Canada, a dub for a streaming platform in London. Each production has different timelines, fees and contracts, and payments arrive in no particular order. Tesla3000 proposes a structure that gathers these engagements into a single work relationship.

How engagements work

Acting: the engagement is for shooting days, with a daily or per-role fee, often with costume-fitting and reading days. The contract states role, days, location, fee, any repeat uses and use of likeness. Dubbing and voice: work by studio session, shift or script page, for films, series, ads, audiobooks and games. Payments arrive over weeks, sometimes after release. For both, clauses matter greatly on: duration of exploitation, territories, advertising uses and AI (use of voice and likeness for synthesis).

The cooperative’s role

The cooperative signs the agreement with the production, invoices and collects (also in foreign currency). The worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) has a work relationship with the cooperative; if employment, INPS contributions, INAIL, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. For actors and voice actors specific rules apply on social security, collective agreements and neighbouring rights: classification must be checked with us case by case before accepting, and not every situation can be handled. For EU clients the reverse charge normally applies (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72); for non-EU clients generic B2B services are normally outside Italian VAT: checked case by case.

Typical pitfalls

  • Likeness and voice rights. Read clauses on AI, reuse and advertising carefully.
  • Delayed payments. They can come months later: the fund advance can help.
  • Expenses. Travel, lodging and meals must be in the contract and documented.
  • Work abroad. Permits, contributions and tax depend on country and duration.
  • Exclusivity. Some productions bar other jobs in certain windows: write them down.

Example

Example: imagine Lorenzo, an actor and voice actor from Milan, who gets a role of four shooting days in a series filmed in the Czech Republic and, in the same period, eight voice sessions for a video game from a Canadian studio, recorded from a professional home studio. The cooperative signs the two agreements; Lorenzo logs days and sessions on the platform, uploads travel expenses and receives a payslip. The character is fictional; eligibility of each case is checked first.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Intro call. You present engagements, dates, locations, fees.
  2. Check. We review classification, collective agreement and permits.
  3. Joining. Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
  4. Agreement. Contract with the production.
  5. Activity. Days, sessions and expenses on the platform.
  6. Invoicing and payslip. The cooperative invoices and collects; you receive the payslip.

INAIL, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

When something else fits better

For sporadic engagements, a flat-rate VAT number (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) may be enough, with admin on your side. If you want one continuous relationship between engagements, let us talk.

Checklist before signing

Before signing an engagement, read: the duration of exploitation and territories; clauses on image, voice and use of synthesis technologies; rehearsal and shooting days and rest days; expense reimbursement and accommodation; payment terms and repeats. Ask for the script or at least your character’s scenes. For work abroad, check visas and permits with the production. If you have an agent, coordinate the contract with them. Send us the text before signing: we assess together whether it falls within what the cooperative can handle.

Frequently asked questions

Can I work through a cooperative as an actor?

It depends on the type of engagement and the collective agreement applicable to the sector. Classification must be checked case by case before you start.

Who manages neighbouring rights and repeat fees?

Neighbouring rights have specific rules and are not automatically part of the work relationship. They must be checked case by case.

Can I work remotely for foreign productions?

For dubbing or voice work yes, in a studio or from a home studio if the production allows. For filming, the place of work changes tax and social security rules.
