Independent researcher for foreign foundations: contract and co-op

Foundations, think tanks and research institutes abroad often need specific expertise for a limited time: a literature review, a data analysis on a European country, a policy report. A researcher based in Italy with the right skills then runs into a practical obstacle: how do you get paid by a foreign body without opening a VAT number for a couple of projects a year?

What this kind of work looks like

Research commissions follow a different rhythm from commercial ones. There is usually a call for proposals, a statement of objectives, a delivery calendar and payment tied to milestones rather than to months worked.

Typical deliverables:

  • a literature review or state-of-the-art overview;
  • a curated dataset with methodological documentation;
  • a short policy brief for non-specialist decision-makers;
  • a final report reviewed by the foundation’s internal referees.

Payments often follow a “30-40-30” pattern: a first instalment on signing, a second on delivery of the draft, the balance when the final report is accepted. Cash flow is lumpy, very unlike a monthly payslip.

Common pitfalls

  1. IP and publication. Some foundations want exclusive rights over the text; others let the researcher publish a derived academic paper. Put it in the contract.
  2. Eligible costs. Paid datasets, software licences, interview travel: agree in advance what is reimbursable.
  3. Unlimited revisions. Without a set number of review rounds, the final payment can slip by months.
  4. Currency. Many foundations pay in dollars or pounds: know who bears the exchange-rate risk.

Example: a six-month project

A fictional example. Imagine Chiara, a labour economist who finished her PhD two years ago. A Dutch foundation asks her for a study on researcher mobility between Italy and Northern Europe: six months, three milestones, a final policy brief. Chiara has no VAT number and does not want to open one for a single assignment. She joins the cooperative, which signs the commercial contract with the foundation and gives her an employment contract for the duration of the project. At each milestone the cooperative invoices; Chiara logs her activities on the platform together with expense claims for the interviews in Berlin.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Share the assignment. Send us the call for proposals and the foundation’s terms.
  2. Join. You become a worker-member by subscribing a share of capital, refundable on exit under the by-laws (Law 142/2001).
  3. Commercial contract. Tesla3000 signs with the foundation, not you; your employment relationship is separate and governed by the internal regulation.
  4. Work and reporting. You log activities and expenses on the digital platform; you receive a monthly payslip with INPS contributions, INAIL cover and professional liability insurance included.
  5. Invoicing and collection. The cooperative invoices the foundation, in foreign currency if needed, under the B2B rules for cross-border services, to be verified case by case.
  6. Advance. If milestones are far apart, you can ask for an advance from the dedicated fund.

Cooperative or VAT number?

With a VAT number under the flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, or 5% for the first five years if requirements are met) you keep full control, but you pay Separate Management INPS contributions, handle e-invoicing and tax returns, and often need an accountant. With the cooperative you have an employment-type relationship: holidays, sick leave, severance pay, unemployment benefit under legal conditions and no bookkeeping. For a few irregular assignments the second path can be lighter; for many stable clients the first can fit better. Look at your commission calendar for the next twelve months to decide.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foundation grant be handled through the cooperative?

It depends on the nature of the payment. A fee for a research service delivered to a foundation can be handled through a commercial contract. True scholarships or stipends have their own tax rules and must be checked case by case.

Who signs the contract with the foundation?

The cooperative signs and invoices the foundation. The researcher is a worker-member who carries out the assignment under a separate employment relationship.

Can I keep my university affiliation?

Often yes, but it depends on the university's rules on outside activities. Check before accepting the assignment.
