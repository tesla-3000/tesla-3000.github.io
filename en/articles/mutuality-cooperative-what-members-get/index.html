Mutuality: what it is and what it can give members

When people talk about cooperatives the word mutuality often appears, but it is not always clear what it means in practice. In short: it is the purpose for which the cooperative exists. Whereas a capital company exists to generate profit for those who invest in it, a worker cooperative exists to give its members work opportunities and services on favourable terms. Tesla3000 works on this logic.

The mutualistic purpose: three elements

The member is also a worker. The member is not just a financier: they work for the cooperative or through it. Under Law 142/2001 this gives rise to a membership relationship and a further and distinct work relationship.

Benefits go to members. Services, protections and organisation are designed for those working in the cooperative: platform, support, training, insurance, invoicing and collection handled on members’ behalf.

Democratic governance. Each member has one vote in the assembly, regardless of capital quota, and takes part in the main decisions.

What mutuality can give in practice

  • Shared services. Instead of everyone organising alone, the cooperative pools administration, contracts and insurance, with costs spread among members.
  • Bargaining power. A structured entity can negotiate better than an individual, for example on payment terms.
  • Network and community. Members exchange contacts, experience and opportunities.
  • Training. The cooperative can organise courses useful for members’ professional growth.
  • Possible patronage refunds. If the year closes with surpluses, the assembly may decide to share part among members in proportion to work done, under Article 2545-sexies of the Civil Code. No amount is guaranteed.

Example: Lorenzo’s community

Example: imagine Lorenzo, a sound designer. At first he works alone with a German client. After a few months, in the members’ community he meets a video editor and an illustrator: a small collaboration starts for a new client who wanted several skills together. The cooperative handles a single contract and each has their own employment relationship. This exchange is mutuality in action: not a profit to distribute, but an opportunity that arises from belonging to the same body.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Membership. You become a member by subscribing the share capital quota, refundable on exit under the by-laws.
  2. Employment relationship. We define your classification and activity.
  3. Services. You access the platform, WhatsApp and email support, training, insurance and invoicing.
  4. Assembly. You take part in decisions with one vote.
  5. Accounts. The assembly approves the accounts and decides on any surpluses.
  6. Community. You share experience and collaborations with other members.

Limits to know

Mutuality is not a promise of wealth or better conditions in every case. The benefit depends on each person’s situation: for those with a solid activity and an already efficient set-up it may be smaller; for those starting from zero or wanting to concentrate on work it may be larger. The cooperative also has internal rules that members accept: regulation, respect for assembly decisions, cooperation. Moreover, regulated activities or those reserved to professions with exclusive rights cannot be handled.

How to judge mutuality for yourself

Rather than taking the idea on trust, test it with concrete questions. What services do I actually use, and what would they cost me alone? Do the by-laws and regulation say clearly how decisions are taken and how any surplus is used? Can I read the accounts? Do I feel listened to when I make a suggestion? A cooperative whose mutuality is real answers these questions openly. If you find the answers convincing, the model is likely to suit you; if not, better to know before joining.

Frequently asked questions

What does it mean that a cooperative is mutualistic?

It means the main purpose is to give members work benefits and services on favourable terms, not to maximise profit for distribution to investors.

Does mutuality guarantee extra earnings?

No. If there are surpluses the cooperative may distribute patronage refunds, but there is no guarantee on amounts.

Who decides how surpluses are used?

The members' assembly, under the by-laws and the law.
