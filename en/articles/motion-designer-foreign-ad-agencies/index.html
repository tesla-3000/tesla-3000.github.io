Motion designer for foreign ad agencies: deadlines and contracts

A New York ad agency has won a campaign for a beverage brand and must produce twenty social animations in two weeks. An Amsterdam studio wants a motion designer for a software explainer video. This is a market with tight deadlines, changing briefs and defined budgets. Those who can do 2D and 3D animation and are available at short notice are in high demand.

How the work goes

The typical flow of a motion design project:

  1. Brief and moodboard: goal, tone, length, formats (16:9, vertical, square).
  2. Storyboard with approval.
  3. Style frames to fix the look.
  4. Animation and first version.
  5. Revision rounds, usually two or three.
  6. Delivery in several formats and with subtitles or language versions.

Rates are per second of finished video, per day or flat fee. In advertising, payments are often at 45-60 days from invoice, while delivery deadlines are immediate. This mismatch is the main source of financial stress.

Where hours burn

  • Revisions beyond plan: without a cap on rounds, the flat fee erodes.
  • Concept changes after approved storyboard: treat as new work.
  • Multiple formats: each adaptation takes time and must be in the quote.
  • Client materials late or of poor quality.
  • Rendering times and the cost of any cloud services.

Organising files and delivery

Structure the project with clear folders and naming, because the agency may ask for changes months later. Save intermediate versions with date and comments and keep the source files, agreeing whether they will be delivered. Prepare a list of export formats and check texts with the agency before final rendering: fixing a typo after render costs time. If the client wants the source files, the transfer should be stated in the contract with any extra fee.

Example: twenty animations in two weeks

A fictional example. Imagine Irene, a freelance motion designer in Bologna. An American agency commissions twenty short animations for a campaign, flat fee per video, three formats each, delivered in two weeks. Payment at 45 days. Irene does not want to wait a month and a half for pay, and the agency wants a single invoice in dollars. Through the cooperative, Tesla3000 signs the contract; Irene logs days and deliveries on the platform and receives the monthly payslip, while an advance from the fund covers the collection delay.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Send us the brief and the agency’s terms.
  2. Membership with a share of capital refundable on exit (Law 142/2001).
  3. Contract: rate, formats, number of revisions, rights, embargo.
  4. Platform: days, deliveries, expenses, document signing.
  5. Invoicing: the cooperative invoices the non-EU agency under the B2B rules for services, verified case by case.
  6. Payslip, with INPS, INAIL and professional liability included.

A note on rights and creative works

Animations, illustrations and creative works are subject to copyright rules, with particular aspects: assignment, uses, duration. These must be checked case by case and specified precisely in the contract.

VAT number or cooperative

With the flat-rate regime (85,000 euro threshold, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if requirements are met) you can work with several agencies and keep all the margin, with Separate Management contributions and invoice handling. It is the typical choice of motion designers with repeat clients. For those working for one foreign agency with tight deadlines, the cooperative simplifies collection and admin.

Frequently asked questions

Is a day rate or a project rate better?

It depends on the brief. For well-defined jobs a flat fee is clear; for projects with many revisions a day rate protects the professional.

Can I show the videos in my portfolio?

Only after publication and if the contract allows it. Campaigns are often under embargo until launch.

Who provides software licences?

Usually the professional uses their own, with a fee that includes them; sometimes the agency provides access. Agree it.
