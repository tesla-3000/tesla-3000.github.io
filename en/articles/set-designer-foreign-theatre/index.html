Use cases

Set designers working on productions for foreign theatres

A set designer works by project: a season, a show, a touring installation. The phases are intense, with site visits, sketches, construction, assembly and strike. When the production is abroad, travel, materials and contracts in other languages add to the complexity. Tesla3000 can take on the administrative side.

Why theatre projects need organisation

A theatre production has rigid timing: opening night does not move. Work is concentrated in a few weeks of preparation and assembly, then stops until the next project. A professional with a VAT number must invoice and keep up with accounts and deadlines even in the busiest periods. In the cooperative these tasks belong to the structure: you work, log your activities and receive your payslip.

Law 142/2001 provides that the member has a work relationship separate from the membership relationship, which can be as an employee. On site, INAIL coverage and safety training are not minor details.

Example: Ilaria and a theatre in Lisbon

Example: imagine Ilaria, a set designer, hired by a Lisbon company for a new production. She must design, coordinate construction and oversee assembly for six weeks, with two trips. Ilaria joins the cooperative.

The cooperative signs the contract with the company: scope, schedule, fee and responsibility for materials. Ilaria logs activities and site days on the platform; travel and accommodation are reimbursed under the engagement; materials are bought by the company or advanced by Ilaria and repaid via expense claim. Each month she receives a payslip.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Production sheet. You tell us the company, period, places, type of work and trips.
  2. Membership. You subscribe the share capital quota.
  3. Engagement with the company. The commercial contract sets fee, schedule and reimbursements.
  4. Employment relationship. Defined for the length of the production.
  5. Safety. We check training and coverage suited to site work.
  6. Delivery. You log activities and expenses; WhatsApp support is available if doubts arise.
  7. Invoicing. The cooperative invoices the company and handles collection, including in foreign currency.

Practical points to clarify

Materials. State in the contract who buys them, who owns them at the end of the production and how any advances are repaid.

Assistants and crew. If you need assistant designers or craftspeople, the cooperative can include them in the engagement as other members with their own relationships, or the client can supply them: decide beforehand.

Rights on sketches. Sketches and designs may enjoy copyright protection. Their assignment or licence to the theatre should be defined in writing: we check case by case.

Work abroad. Documents, taxation and host-country rules must be assessed on the individual case; we give no general answers. If you live abroad, tax residence also needs a separate check.

Professional liability insurance is included in the package and covers liabilities linked to your work, within policy limits.

After opening night

Once the set is built, maintenance or adaptations for repeat performances and tours often remain. It helps to foresee them in the engagement, with estimated days and rates, instead of treating them as favours. That way work after the premiere is logged, paid and covered too.

Language and communication in production

In an international production technical instructions flow through direction, stage management and technical offices in different languages. Ask that drawings, dimensions and materials lists be shared in a common format with clear units of measure. A misunderstanding over a measurement costs more than one extra meeting: the platform and the cooperative’s support also help keep a record of what was agreed.

Frequently asked questions

Is work for a foreign theatre covered by INAIL?

INAIL coverage is provided for members with an employment relationship. For work done abroad, the conditions must be checked on the individual case.

Who advances stage materials?

It is agreed in the engagement: they can be paid by the client or advanced by the member and reimbursed under the cooperative's rules.

Can I work on several productions at once?

Yes, with separate engagements and clear time and role indications for each.
