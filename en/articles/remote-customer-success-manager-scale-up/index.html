Remote customer success manager for a scale-up: how to be set up

A Berlin scale-up has three hundred enterprise customers and a customer success team entirely in Germany. To serve Italian and Spanish companies it wants a remote CSM who knows the market. Hiring directly would require an Italian entity; a freelance contract is not designed for a continuous role. In between lies an area where many professionals find no clear solution.

The role, day by day

A customer success manager is not a support agent. The goal is to make sure the customer uses the product and renews. In practice:

  • onboarding new customers with a 30-60-90 day activation plan;
  • quarterly business reviews with usage metrics;
  • churn-risk management, with signals such as drops in logins or repeated tickets;
  • expansion: flagging upgrade opportunities to sales;
  • collecting feedback for the product team.

Metrics are renewal rate, adoption of core features and satisfaction. A CSM works with sales and support, so overlap in working hours with the foreign team is a concrete condition of the role.

Where people stumble

  1. Hybrid hours: meetings at 5 pm Italian time with California are normal. Clarify the availability window upfront.
  2. Renewal targets on inherited accounts in poor shape: ask how they are assessed.
  3. Tools: CRM, ticketing and licences should be provided by the client.
  4. Role boundaries: without a written scope, the CSM ends up doing technical support and sales at once.

Signals the role is healthy

Before accepting, ask for the churn rate of the accounts you would be given, the number of accounts per person and who decides renewal discounts. An overly large portfolio makes quality work impossible. Check also that there is an escalation path to product and technical support: without it, the CSM becomes everyone’s lightning rod. Write these conditions into the contract and review them after the first quarter, when real numbers are visible.

Example: twelve months with a single client

A fictional example. Imagine Elena, four years in customer care at a Milan software house. The German scale-up offers her a twelve-month assignment, agreed hours, twenty Italian accounts to look after. She prefers a stable relationship with holidays and sick leave, without opening a VAT number for one client. Through the cooperative the contract is signed and she is offered an employment contract as a member. Each month she logs days and activities on the platform; the payslip includes INPS contributions and INAIL cover.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Interview with us: role, duration, hours, tools.
  2. Share of capital and filed internal regulation (Law 142/2001).
  3. Contract with the scale-up: Tesla3000 signs and invoices, in foreign currency if needed, with B2B rules for services to the EU verified case by case.
  4. Employment contract and monthly payslip with holidays, sick leave, severance pay, unemployment benefit under legal conditions.
  5. Operations: attendance, expenses, documents and signing on the platform; WhatsApp and email support.
  6. Renewal or closure, with training and the member community available.

And if I prefer a VAT number?

For a full-time role with a single client, a VAT number can raise the issue of economic dependence, to be verified case by case. Under the flat-rate regime (85,000 euro threshold, 15% or 5% in the first five years with requirements) you gain flexibility but bear Separate Management contributions and paperwork. The cooperative is often more straightforward when the assignment is continuous and hours are set by the client.

Frequently asked questions

Is a full-time job for a single client compatible with the cooperative?

Yes, it can be handled with a dedicated contract and an employment contract for the member. Hours, tools and responsibilities must be clarified.

Who provides the computer and licences?

Normally the client provides licences and access; equipment is agreed in the contract or with the cooperative. Decide it in writing.

How are holidays and leave handled?

A member with an employment relationship has holidays and sick leave under the applicable rules; absences are logged on the platform and coordinated with the client.
