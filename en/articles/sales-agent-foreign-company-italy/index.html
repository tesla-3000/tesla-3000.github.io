Use cases

Sales rep for a foreign company entering Italy

A German packaging-machine maker, an Israeli software house, a UK food producer: all want to sell in Italy but do not yet know whether the market will respond. Setting up a company, hiring a salesperson and renting an office is a premature investment. For the sales professional it is a chance to represent a brand without handling contracts alone. Tesla3000 acts as the bridge.

The problem when entering a new market

The foreign company needs a local presence: someone who speaks the customers’ language, knows distributors, attends trade fairs and makes first visits. But hiring in Italy means a contract, payroll, contributions and tools. Salespeople, for their part, often prefer a structure with a stable base pay plus commission rather than purely variable income.

How the role is structured

The cooperative signs a commercial contract with the foreign company for field sales services: territory, product lines, targets for visits and quotes, reporting, price list. The worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) has the work relationship agreed with the cooperative, which can include a fixed part and a variable part linked to results. The foreign company pays the cooperative, which invoices (for EU clients normally under reverse charge; for non-EU clients as generic B2B services not subject to Italian VAT: always checked case by case).

Note: commercial agents, with Enasarco and an agency mandate, have their own rules. If your role is that, check first. Here we refer to work carried out as a worker-member.

Typical pitfalls

  • Timing expectations. B2B sales cycles in Italy can take months: the fixed part is there to carry them.
  • Client portfolio. Decide who owns the contacts won.
  • Territory and exclusivity. Define area and any product exclusivity.
  • Car and costs. Travel and trade-fair expenses should be in the agreed budget.
  • Foreign company duties. Warranty and after-sales support must be settled in the agreement.

Example

Example: imagine Roberta, with fifteen years in food sales, asked by a Greek olive-oil producer to develop sales to restaurants and wine shops in Lombardy for six months. The cooperative signs the agreement: territory, price list, 25 visits a month, samples paid by the client. Roberta logs visits and activities on the platform, uploads travel expenses and receives a monthly payslip. Character and data are fictional.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Briefing. The company (or you) describes product, territory, goals.
  2. Joining. Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
  3. Agreement. Commercial contract between the cooperative and the foreign company.
  4. Activity. Visits, reports and expenses tracked on the platform.
  5. Invoicing. The cooperative invoices and collects, also in foreign currency.
  6. Payment. Monthly payslip; advance from the fund if needed.

Professional liability insurance, INAIL and support are included.

If you already have a VAT number

The flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) can remain a good choice if you work with several principals. With a single foreign principal and a structure to build, the cooperative cuts the bureaucracy.

Checklist before signing

Before starting, ask the foreign company for: a price list in euro and sales terms for Italy; documentation in Italian (data sheets, certifications, manuals); who handles shipping, returns and warranty; a budget for samples and trade fairs; who issues quotes and with what approval. Define your goals clearly for the first 90 days (qualified leads, quotes, first orders) and how they will be measured. A realistic plan avoids misunderstandings: sometimes the first order takes months. Share the plan with us and we add it to the agreement as indicative goals, with no promises of results.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a commercial agency contract (Enasarco)?

It depends. Commercial agents have specific rules, including Enasarco pension cover, which must be checked. Here we mean a worker-member with a work relationship agreed with the cooperative.

Does the foreign company need an Italian branch?

Not necessarily to get started: it contracts with the cooperative. Any obligations of the foreign company are assessed case by case.

How are commissions and bonuses set?

They are defined in the agreement and in the member's contract. We promise no amounts: they depend on the terms and on results.
