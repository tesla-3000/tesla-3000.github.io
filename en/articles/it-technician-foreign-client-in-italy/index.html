Use cases

IT technician supporting a foreign client's office in Italy

A company in Sao Paulo has opened a sales office in Milan with twelve people. Headquarters has an IT department, but everything is far away and in another time zone: someone is needed to configure computers, solve everyday problems, manage the office network and bridge to central IT. The IT technician who follows a foreign client in Italy is a very concrete figure, halfway between sysadmin and local support.

What an assignment like this involves

  • Onboarding new employees: preparing laptops, accounts, access, software.
  • Daily support: printers, VPN, email, video-conference links.
  • Local network management: Wi-Fi, firewall, dealings with the connectivity provider.
  • Inventory of equipment and warranty tracking.
  • Basic security: updates, backups, credential management and lost devices.
  • Liaison with the central IT team, via tickets and periodic meetings.

Such an assignment often translates into a fixed number of days per month (for example four on site) with remote on-call for emergencies.

The delicate points

  1. Scope: where local support ends and systems administration, which stays with central IT, begins.
  2. On-call: should a network outage at 6 pm be fixed immediately? If yes, it needs pay.
  3. Purchasing materials: authorisation processes for spending, so you do not advance large sums.
  4. System access: minimum privileges and traceability. A technician with too many permissions is a risk for everyone.
  5. Continuity: record configurations and passwords in a shared manager, so the client does not depend on one person.

Documenting so you are not indispensable

A technician following a site alone risks becoming the only one who knows where everything is. Keep a configuration log, a list of accounts and a calendar of licence and warranty expiries, shared with central IT. Agree a stand-in for holidays, even among members of the community. A written procedure for employee joining and leaving reduces errors and security risks and gives even a distant client the impression of an orderly service.

Example: an office of twelve

A fictional example. Imagine Fabrizio, a sysadmin with fifteen years’ experience. A Brazilian company opens an office in Milan and entrusts him with IT support: four on-site days a month, remote on-call, direct contact with the IT manager in Brazil. Fabrizio does not want a VAT number with a single client; the company has no Italian entity that could hire him. Through the cooperative, Tesla3000 signs the contract, Fabrizio logs days and interventions on the platform, with INAIL cover and professional liability included, and receives the monthly payslip.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Framing the assignment: sites, days, on-call, tools.
  2. Membership as a member with a share of capital refundable on exit (Law 142/2001).
  3. Contract with service levels, expenses and liability.
  4. Platform: day log, tickets, expense claims, documents.
  5. Invoicing the non-EU client under B2B rules verified case by case, in foreign currency too.
  6. Employment contract and payslip with holidays, sick leave, severance pay and unemployment benefit under legal conditions.

Comparison with the VAT number

Under the flat-rate regime (85,000 euro threshold, 15% substitute tax, 5% in the first five years with requirements) a technician with many small clients has excellent margins, paying contributions to Separate Management or to the craftsmen/traders scheme depending on the activity, and handling invoices and returns. With a single stable foreign client, the cooperative reduces paperwork and offers ordinary employment protections.

Frequently asked questions

Can the cooperative also handle on-site interventions?

Yes, the contract can include interventions at the client's sites in Italy, with logging of days and trips and INAIL cover.

How is on-call duty regulated?

It is defined in the contract with time slots, response times and extra pay.

Who buys spare hardware?

Normally the client. If the technician advances purchases, they are reimbursed as expense claims with a receipt and authorisation.
