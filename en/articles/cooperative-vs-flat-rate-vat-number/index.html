Cooperative or flat-rate VAT number: an honest comparison

Every freelancer sooner or later asks the same question: is it better to open a VAT number under the flat-rate regime or to work as a worker-member of a cooperative like Tesla3000? There is no absolute best. There are situations where one fits and situations where the other does. Here is a comparison without slogans.

The flat-rate VAT number

The flat-rate regime is designed for small businesses: revenue threshold of 85,000 euro, a 15% substitute tax, reduced to 5% for the first five years if the requirements are met. Social security contributions are paid by the professional, for example through the INPS Separate Management or the Artisans and Traders schemes. Paperwork remains: electronic invoicing, tax returns, often an accountant, constant monitoring of the revenue cap.

Strengths: full autonomy, rates and clients of your choice, no intermediary, a simplified regime for those with few costs. For people with many clients who want maximum management freedom it is often a solid choice.

Points of attention: there is no employer. Sick leave, holidays, maternity, unemployment support and severance do not accrue as in employment; the bureaucracy is on your shoulders; unpaid invoices are your problem.

The cooperative worker-member

Under Law 142/2001 the member enters, besides the membership relationship, a further and distinct work relationship, which can be as an employee. In that case: INPS contributions paid by the cooperative, INAIL, holidays, sick leave, maternity and paternity leave, TFR, a monthly payslip and, if you lose work involuntarily, NASpI under the legal conditions. No VAT number, no bookkeeping.

Strengths: employee protections, paperwork handled by the cooperative, invoicing and collection managed, ongoing support.

Points of attention: you must subscribe a share capital quota; income is taxed as employment income with progressive income tax, withholding at source and surtaxes; autonomy follows the cooperative’s regulation; not every profession can be handled, such as regulated professions with exclusive rights.

Example: Matteo at the crossroads

Example: imagine Matteo, a marketing consultant with two foreign clients. He is undecided. With a flat-rate VAT number he would keep full control but would have to manage invoices, deadlines and contributions, and if he fell ill for a month his income would stop. In the cooperative he gives up some formal autonomy but gains a payslip and protections, and can focus on clients.

Matteo does not choose on tax convenience alone, which for various reasons we do not compare in figures. He asks himself: do I prefer to manage or to delegate? Do I need a safety net? How much will I really invoice?

How to decide in four steps

  1. Estimate realistic revenue for the next twelve months, not hoped-for revenue.
  2. Count your clients: one or two stable clients favour an employment relationship; many small clients may favour a VAT number.
  3. Assess the protections you need: family, mortgage, health, plans for parental leave.
  4. Consider time: how many hours a month do you want to spend on admin, invoices and chasing payments?

How it works with the cooperative

We listen, explain how the model works and tell you frankly if, in your case, a VAT number might suit better. If the cooperative fits, you join with the share capital quota (refundable on exit according to the by-laws), sign the employment relationship, use the platform for activities, expense claims and payslips, and we invoice your clients, including foreign ones. VAT treatment of those invoices is assessed case by case.

Time and peace of mind

Beyond the numbers, what counts is how much time and attention you are willing to give administration. Those who like to keep everything under control and have few clients often find the flat-rate regime manageable. Those who prefer to spend hours on the work itself, or already have a full life between family and projects, may appreciate having a body that takes care of invoicing, collections and reminders. Neither preference is wrong: they are different working styles.

Conclusion

The two routes are not enemies: they are different tools for different needs. If you want an opinion on your actual case, contact us.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, cooperative or flat-rate?

There is no answer valid for everyone: it depends on revenue, number of clients, need for protections and appetite for paperwork. It must be assessed on your case.

Does the flat-rate regime have a revenue cap?

Yes, the threshold is 85,000 euro of annual revenue, with a 15% substitute tax, reduced to 5% for the first five years if the requirements are met.

Can I switch from one to the other?

Yes, but the switch should be planned carefully around closures, deadlines and ongoing client relationships.
