What it means to be a worker-member of a cooperative

There are confused ideas about worker cooperatives: some think the member is just an investor, others that they are a mere employee. In reality the worker-member is both, with precise rules. Understanding what it means helps you choose knowingly.

Two distinct relationships

Italian Law no. 142 of 3 April 2001 states that a member of a worker cooperative, besides the membership relationship, enters a further and distinct work relationship. This second relationship can be as an employee, self-employed or in another form. The first, the membership relationship, carries rights and duties as a member; the second, the work relationship, governs duties, hours, pay and protections.

That is why the member has a dual position: taking part in the life of the enterprise and, at the same time, working for it. At Tesla3000 this means a concrete employment relationship with payslips and contributions, in addition to participation in the cooperative.

What being a member involves

  • Share capital quota. You subscribe a quota, refundable on exit according to the by-laws.
  • Voting right. At the assembly each member has one vote, regardless of quota.
  • Internal regulation. The cooperative must have a regulation, filed as required by Article 6 of Law 142/2001, governing work organisation.
  • Mutuality. The cooperative works to give members work opportunities on favourable terms; if there are surpluses, it may distribute patronage refunds, with no guarantee on amounts.

What being a worker involves

In the employee case: INPS contributions (FPLD) paid by the cooperative, INAIL insurance, holidays, sick leave, maternity and paternity leave, TFR and, if you lose work involuntarily, NASpI under the legal conditions. You receive a monthly payslip and the annual certificate (CU). You do not open a VAT number or keep accounts.

Employment income is subject to progressive income tax with withholding at source and year-end adjustment, plus regional and municipal surtaxes. We promise no net amounts because they depend on your situation.

Example: Sofia’s first weeks

Example: imagine Sofia, a freelance graphic designer who has invoiced foreign clients for years. She decides to become a member. She subscribes the quota, reads the internal regulation and signs the employment relationship. The cooperative signs the commercial contract with her Swiss client; Sofia logs activities on the platform, downloads her first payslip and takes part in the annual assembly, where she votes like every other member.

At first it feels odd not to issue invoices any more; then she realises her work is the same: what changes is who handles the administration.

How it works with the cooperative

  1. Getting to know you. You tell us about your activity and clients.
  2. Documents. You receive the by-laws, internal regulation and information on the quota.
  3. Membership. You subscribe the quota and are admitted as a member.
  4. Employment relationship. We define type, pay and duration.
  5. Day to day. You use the platform for engagements, activities, expense claims, payslips and document signing.
  6. Participation. You can take part in assemblies and the members’ community.

Duties and limits

Being a member also means respecting the regulation and assembly decisions, cooperating with other members and behaving fairly towards the cooperative and clients. Some activities, such as regulated professions with exclusive rights, cannot be handled. Anyone living abroad must have their tax residence checked case by case.

Questions worth asking yourself

Am I ready to take part, even partly, in the life of the cooperative? Do I accept working within shared rules in return for protections and services? Do I need an organisation to take care of contracts, invoices and collections? If the answer is yes, the worker-member model may suit you. If instead you want maximum independence and already have a solid set-up, weigh the alternatives calmly.

Frequently asked questions

Is a worker-member an employee?

Can be: besides the membership relationship, the member enters a work relationship which may be as an employee, self-employed or another form, depending on the case.

How much does it cost to become a member?

You subscribe a share capital quota, refundable on exit according to the by-laws. The amount is stated in the cooperative's documents.

What is the internal regulation?

It is the document, filed as the law provides, that governs work organisation and members' economic treatment.
