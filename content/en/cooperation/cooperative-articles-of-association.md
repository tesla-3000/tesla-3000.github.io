When you join an Italian worker cooperative you sign two separate things: the application for membership and the employment contract. The first means accepting the **articles of association** (*statuto*), the document governing the cooperative as a company. Many people sign without reading them. That is a shame, because the articles answer very concrete questions: who can join, how you leave, what happens to your share, who decides what.

This guide covers the main parts, with references to the Italian Civil Code. For details, the text of each cooperative's articles always prevails.

## Founding deed and articles

The Civil Code (art. 2521) lists what a cooperative's founding deed must contain; the articles, which form an integral part of it, set out the operating rules. In practice "articles" refers to the whole set of fundamental rules. They include, among other things:

- name, registered office and duration;
- the business purpose, meaning the activity carried out, and the **mutual purpose**;
- requirements and procedures for admitting members;
- members' rights and obligations;
- rules on contributions and shares;
- criteria for distributing patronage refunds and any profits;
- rules on governing bodies (members' meeting, board of directors, any supervisory body);
- grounds and procedures for withdrawal and exclusion.

## The mutual purpose

This is the cooperative's reason for being. In a worker cooperative, it means offering members work opportunities on better terms than they would have alone. The articles describe it and, in cooperatives with prevalent mutuality, include the clauses required by art. 2514 of the Civil Code (limits on dividends, indivisible reserves, allocation of assets on winding up).

## Admission

Admission of new members is governed by art. 2528 of the Civil Code: the applicant submits a request, the directors decide, and the decision is communicated and recorded in the members' register. If the application is rejected, the reasons must be given and the applicant can ask the members' meeting to rule on it. The articles set the requirements, for example professional skills consistent with the cooperative's activity.

## Share capital and membership share

Each member subscribes a share of capital. The articles set its value and payment terms. The share is not a lost entry fee: it is a stake, refundable on leaving under the articles and depending on the financial statements. See [share capital: what it is](/en/articles/share-capital-what-it-is/).

## Withdrawal

Withdrawal is the member's voluntary exit (art. 2532 of the Civil Code). The articles set when it is allowed and how: usually a written notice on which the directors decide. For a worker-member, withdrawal must be read together with the employment contract, because the two relationships are linked. See [leaving the cooperative](/en/articles/leaving-the-cooperative/).

## Exclusion

Exclusion is an exit decided by the cooperative (art. 2533 of the Civil Code), in the cases set by law and the articles: for example serious breach of members' obligations or loss of the requirements. The articles describe grounds and procedure, and the member can challenge the decision within the legal time limits. Here too, for worker-members, the effects on employment must be assessed case by case.

## Governing bodies

The articles describe the members' meeting (which approves the accounts, appoints the directors and decides on amendments to the articles), the board of directors (which runs the cooperative) and, where required, the supervisory body. They also state how members are convened and informed.

## Articles and internal regulation

Law 142/2001 (art. 6) requires cooperatives with worker-members to adopt a filed **internal regulation** describing how work is organised, the types of working relationship and the reference collective agreement. The regulation ranks below the articles: it implements them on the work side and cannot contradict them. More in [the cooperative's internal regulation](/en/articles/cooperative-internal-regulation/).

## Fictional example

Fictional example: imagine Sara, a UX designer weighing up whether to join a cooperative. She reads the articles with three questions: how much is the share and how is it refunded; how does she withdraw if she lands a permanent job; what are the grounds for exclusion. Then she reads the regulation to understand contract and working hours. With these answers she signs the application knowing exactly what she is accepting.
