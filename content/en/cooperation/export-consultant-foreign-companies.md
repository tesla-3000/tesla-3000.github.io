Internationalisation runs both ways. On one side there are foreign companies that want to sell in Italy and need someone who knows the market, distribution, trade fairs and buyers' habits. On the other there are Italian small and medium-sized businesses that want to go abroad and lack the in-house skills to do it. In between sits the export consultant: someone who works by project, with clear goals and often long timelines.

This page describes how an internationalisation consultant can work as a member of {{< brand >}}, with an employment contract and no VAT number, and how to set up contracts based on phases (milestones).

## What the work actually involves

It varies a great deal, but often includes:

- **market analysis:** competition, prices, channels, labelling or certification requirements to be checked;
- **partner search:** distributors, importers, retailers, local agents;
- **trade fair and mission preparation:** meetings, materials, follow-up;
- **support during negotiations** and first supplies;
- **market entry plan:** timing, budget, country priorities.

This is consulting, not selling. The line matters: if the fee becomes a commission on sales closed on the client's behalf, the activity may come close to that of a commercial agent, which has its own rules (for example regarding Enasarco). There is a dedicated page on the [sales agent for foreign companies](/en/articles/sales-agent-foreign-company-italy/). Before including percentages on sales, check first.

## Milestone-based contracts

Internationalisation projects lend themselves to being split into phases. A milestone contract could be structured like this:

1. **Analysis phase:** delivery of a market report.
2. **Partner search phase:** a qualified list of potential distributors and first contacts.
3. **Meetings phase:** an agenda of appointments, possibly around a trade fair.
4. **Launch phase:** support with the first negotiations for a set period.

For each phase the commercial contract states what is delivered, by when, how the client approves the result and how much it pays. This protects both sides: the client pays for what it receives, and the consultant does not work for months without being paid. What to put in a contract like this is explained in [commercial contract contents](/en/articles/commercial-contract-contents/).

One important detail: the client's payments to the cooperative follow the milestones, while the member's salary follows their employment contract. How to bridge the two (for example with the payment advance tools the cooperative provides) is agreed together before starting.

## Fictional example

Fictional example: imagine Lorenzo, an export consultant with years of experience in food and drink, living in Verona. A Canadian maple syrup producer wants to enter Italian specialty retail. At the same time a winery in the Veneto asks him to help find importers in Japan.

For the Canadian client, Lorenzo proposes four milestones: market analysis, distributor list, meetings at a trade fair, three months of launch support. The cooperative signs the contract, invoices at each approved phase and collects in Canadian dollars. For the Veneto winery the contract is Italian, with different invoicing rules, but the structure is the same.

Lorenzo logs activities and fair travel expenses on the platform; he receives his payslip every month, with contributions and protections.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **You become a member** by subscribing your share of capital and signing an employment contract, under Law 142/2001 and the internal regulation.
2. **You design the project** with the client: phases, deliverables, timing, fee for each milestone.
3. **The cooperative signs the commercial contract**, with clear criteria for approving each phase.
4. **You work and document:** activities, reports delivered and expenses on the digital platform.
5. **Phased invoicing:** the cooperative invoices at each milestone, in foreign currency too, and follows up collection.
6. **Payslip and protections:** monthly salary, INPS contributions, INAIL, professional liability insurance.

## Things to check

- **Commissions on sales:** assess carefully, as noted.
- **Travel and fairs:** who pays for the stand, flights and hotels? Put it in writing. For long periods abroad, social security and tax are assessed case by case.
- **Delays:** long projects are exposed to late payments; read [late payments from foreign clients](/en/articles/late-payments-foreign-client/).
- **Confidentiality:** prices, margins and customer lists are sensitive; a confidentiality clause is almost always advisable.
