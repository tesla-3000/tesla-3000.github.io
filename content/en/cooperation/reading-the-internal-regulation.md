What the internal regulation is and why it exists is covered in [the cooperative's internal regulation](/en/articles/cooperative-internal-regulation/). Here we do something different: we give you a reading method. The regulation is short but dense, and people often sign it without really reading it. With an ordered checklist, half an hour is enough to see whether the rules fit the way you work and which questions to ask.

## Before you start: three things to have at hand

1. **The current version of the regulation.** Ask for confirmation that it is the one approved by the members' meeting and filed, as required by article 6 of Italian Law 142/2001.
2. **The by-laws.** Many rules on membership (admission, withdrawal, expulsion, share capital) live there, not in the regulation.
3. **Your draft employment contract.** The regulation sets general rules; the contract applies them to you.

Keep a sheet to mark each section with one of three signs: clear, to ask, problematic.

## The clause checklist

### 1. Types of working relationship

The regulation must state which working relationships the cooperative intends to use with members (employment, self-employment, other forms). Check that the one offered to you is among them and how it is described.

### 2. Collective agreement referred to

Look for the collective agreement applied to members in employment. It sets the pay minimums (art. 3, Law 142/2001) and many entitlements: holidays, leave, sick pay. Note the agreement's name and the grade proposed for your profile.

### 3. Work organisation

How activities are assigned, how place and hours are agreed, how remote work operates. If you work with foreign clients, check how time zones, travel and work abroad are handled. See also [hours, autonomy and remote work](/en/articles/hours-autonomy-remote-work/).

### 4. Reporting and tools

What attendance or activity must be logged, with which tool and by when. A clear rule here avoids payroll delays.

### 5. Pay and payment timing

How pay is built, when it is paid and how it relates to client payments. If there are advance mechanisms or common funds, read the conditions. No serious regulation promises a net amount: be wary of any that does.

### 6. Expenses and reimbursements

Which expenses are reimbursable, with which documents and within what time. Also useful: [travel and equipment expense reimbursements](/en/articles/travel-equipment-expense-reimbursements/).

### 7. Member obligations

Confidentiality, use of the cooperative's name, any limits on parallel activity, direct relations with clients. These clauses affect your professional freedom: read them carefully.

### 8. Disciplinary measures

Which behaviour is sanctioned and through what procedure. A written procedure, with a formal notice and a chance to respond, protects you too.

### 9. Crisis situations

The law allows the regulation to provide special measures during a business crisis, resolved by the members' meeting, within precise limits. Check whether the regulation addresses this and how.

### 10. Patronage refunds

The regulation or by-laws may set criteria for sharing any patronage refunds. Remember they depend on the surplus and on the members' meeting: see [how patronage refunds work](/en/articles/patronage-refunds-cooperative/).

### 11. End of the relationship

The regulation may refer to the rules on withdrawal and expulsion and their effects on the working relationship (art. 5, Law 142/2001). Check notice periods and practical steps.

### 12. Changes to the regulation

How it is amended, who proposes changes, how members are told about new versions. The regulation is approved by the members' meeting, and so are its changes: see [how the members' meeting works](/en/cooperation/members-meeting-how-it-works/).

## Fictional example: Paolo, motion designer

Fictional example: imagine Paolo, a motion designer working with two agencies in London and Berlin. He receives the regulation and reads it with the checklist. He marks points 1, 2 and 4 as clear. He puts "to ask" on point 7: he wants to know whether he can keep selling his templates online. He flags point 6 to understand how to claim a trip to Berlin. He sends the three questions by email, gets written answers and files everything with his draft contract. He signs knowing what he is accepting.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Request the documents.** {{< brand >}} sends the by-laws and regulation before admission.
2. **Read with the checklist.** The prospective member notes doubts and questions.
3. **Clarifications.** Answers via WhatsApp or email, in writing.
4. **Admission and contract.** The individual contract applies the regulation to the specific case.
5. **Ongoing reference.** The documents stay available on the member's digital platform.

## One last tip

Reread the regulation whenever it changes and whenever something important changes in your work, such as a new client abroad or a long trip. It is a living document, not a formality to file away.
