There is a concrete difference between teaching online and physically walking into a company to run a workshop. The latter means a flight, materials to carry, two days in a room with twenty people, a hotel, and a pile of receipts on the way home. For a corporate trainer working with foreign clients, logistics weigh as much as teaching.

This page is about the in-person trainer: someone who runs courses on leadership, negotiation, safety, communication, software tools or working methods at the premises of foreign companies. If you work on online platforms instead, there is a dedicated page for [online trainers and teachers](/en/articles/online-trainer-teacher-foreign-platforms/).

## The contract: what the client is really buying

A company hiring a trainer buys a day in the classroom, but also preparation, tailoring of materials and possibly follow-up. The commercial contract between the cooperative and the client should state:

- **days and venue:** dates, location, expected number of participants;
- **preparation:** included in the rate or charged separately;
- **materials:** who produces them, who prints them, who keeps them afterwards (see also [intellectual property in delivered work](/en/articles/intellectual-property-delivered-work/));
- **travel costs:** who pays and how;
- **cancellations:** what happens if the client moves or cancels a few days before the event, when flights and hotels are already booked.

That last point is typical of in-person work and often overlooked. A clear clause avoids arguments over costs already incurred.

## Travel, refunds and business trips

In general terms, when an employee works away from their usual place of work for a limited period, it is a business trip (in Italian, *trasferta*). For a worker-member of the cooperative it works in a similar way: travelling to the client is part of the job, and expenses incurred can be refunded under the cooperative's rules and the applicable legislation.

There are two typical routes:

1. **the client pays directly** for flights, hotel and transfers, and the trainer advances nothing;
2. **the trainer pays upfront** and submits an expense claim with receipts; the cooperative refunds and, where the contract provides, recharges the client.

Which is better depends on the client and the amounts. How to document expenses and what can be refunded is explained in our page on [travel and equipment expense reimbursements](/en/articles/travel-equipment-expense-reimbursements/). For long or frequent stays, where a business trip may turn into something else, read also [working abroad: business trips and secondment](/en/articles/working-abroad-secondment/): there, social security and tax must be assessed case by case.

## Fictional example

Fictional example: imagine Davide, a project management trainer living in Turin. An industrial group based in Lyon asks him for three two-day workshops, one a month, for department heads at three different plants in France.

Davide agrees programme, daily rate and a flat preparation fee with the company. The cooperative drafts the commercial contract: dates, venues, a cancellation clause and a clear expense rule, with hotels booked directly by the client and train tickets advanced by Davide and refunded. After each workshop Davide uploads the days worked and his train receipts to the platform; the cooperative invoices the client, collects payment and pays him salary and refunds through his payslip.

In the fourth month the company proposes extending the programme to two more countries. New dates, same rules, updated contract.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **You become a member** by subscribing your share of capital and signing an employment contract, under Law 142/2001 and the internal regulation.
2. **You agree with the client** programme, days, rate and expense rules.
3. **The cooperative signs the commercial contract** with the foreign company, including travel and cancellation clauses.
4. **Before you leave** you check with the cooperative how expenses will be handled and whether the trip is an ordinary business trip.
5. **After the workshop** you log activity and expense claims on the digital platform, with receipts.
6. **Invoice, collection, payslip:** the cooperative invoices (in foreign currency too), collects, pays your salary and refunds, pays contributions and provides INAIL cover and professional liability insurance.

## A few practical tips

- **Keep every receipt**, even small ones: taxis, meals, luggage. Photographing them straight away means you won't lose them.
- **Clarify currencies:** if you pay in pounds or Swiss francs, the refund follows the conversion rules set by the cooperative.
- **Materials and equipment:** projector, flipcharts, participant kits. Decide in advance who supplies them.
- **Insurance:** INAIL cover applies within the terms of the law; for particular activities (for example training on factory floors) ask beforehand about the client's safety rules.
