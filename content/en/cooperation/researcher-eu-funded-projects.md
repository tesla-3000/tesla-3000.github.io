EU-funded research projects often work through consortia: universities, research centres, companies and associations from different countries working on a shared plan, each with its own share of the work and its own budget. Sometimes a foreign partner needs skills it lacks in-house and looks for an external researcher for a specific task: data analysis, a set of interviews, a software module, a technical report.

This page explains how a researcher can work on these projects as a member of {{< brand >}}, with an employment contract and no VAT number, and which points to clarify straight away. If you work for foreign foundations or private bodies instead, which follow a different logic from consortia, see the page on the [independent researcher for foreign foundations](/en/articles/independent-researcher-foreign-foundations/).

## The main issue: cost eligibility

In EU projects, the partner hiring you usually does not pay out of free funds: it pays from the project budget and must then report that expense to the funding body. The programme rules and the grant agreement set out:

- which **cost categories** are eligible (personnel, subcontracting, services, travel, other);
- how costs must be **documented** (contracts, invoices, evidence of work done, timesheets);
- which **limits** apply to each category.

The decisive question is: which category does your work fall into if you do it through a cooperative that invoices the partner? There is no universal answer: it changes from programme to programme and also depends on how the partner built its budget. That is why the partner must check it with its project management office **before** signing. We do not promise that a cost is eligible: that check belongs to whoever manages the budget.

## Timesheets and evidence of work

Many programmes require time spent to be documented. In practice:

- agree at the start on the **timesheet format** the partner needs (daily, monthly, by work package);
- log your activities regularly on the cooperative's digital platform;
- keep **interim outputs**: drafts, datasets, minutes of consortium meetings;
- keep track of **missions**: project meetings, workshops, conferences.

Tidy records are not just for the partner: they protect you too, if during an audit someone asks what you did and when.

## Fictional example

Fictional example: imagine Elena, a sociologist with a PhD, living in Padua. A Belgian research institute, a partner in an EU consortium on migration and the labour market, asks her to conduct thirty interviews in Italy and write a chapter of the final report.

First, the institute's project office checks how the cost of Elena's work can fit into its budget under the programme rules. Once that is clear, the cooperative signs the commercial contract: activities, deliverables, monthly timesheets in the consortium's format, rules on data ownership and publication. Elena logs her days on the platform, attends a project meeting in Brussels with her expenses refunded, and receives her payslip every month.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **You become a member** by subscribing your share of capital and signing an employment contract, under Law 142/2001 and the internal regulation.
2. **The partner checks eligibility** of the cost under its programme rules.
3. **The cooperative signs the commercial contract** with the partner, including deliverables and documentation duties.
4. **You work and log your time** on the platform, in the agreed format.
5. **Invoicing and collection**, in foreign currency too, with documentation useful to the partner.
6. **Payslip** with INPS contributions, INAIL and the protections of employment.

## Other aspects to consider

- **Ownership of results and publications:** consortia have internal agreements on who owns the results and how publication works. Ask to see them; see also [intellectual property in delivered work](/en/articles/intellectual-property-delivered-work/).
- **Personal data:** interviews and sensitive data need clear processing rules.
- **Duration:** EU projects last years, but partner budgets come in phases; one contract per period is often easier to manage.
- **Academic affiliation:** if you also have a relationship with a university, check for any incompatibilities.
