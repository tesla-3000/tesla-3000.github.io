It happens more and more often: a university student can do something a foreign company needs. They build an app, translate, run social media, analyse data, do research. The first client arrives, perhaps through a professor's contact, an open source project or an online community, and with it the first practical question: how do I get paid properly without complicating my life while I study?

This page is for people still enrolled at university who work a few hours a week for a foreign client. If you have already graduated, see the page on the [recent graduate with a first foreign client](/en/articles/recent-graduate-first-foreign-client/).

## Why a student's situation is different

Compared with a recent graduate or a professional, a student usually has three extra things to consider:

- **family dependency:** many students are tax dependants of their parents, and employment income can affect that status;
- **grants and benefits:** right-to-study grants, university fee waivers, housing, often linked to income indicators;
- **time:** lectures, exams and exam sessions have to fit around commitments to the client.

On the first two points we give no figures or thresholds: they change over time and depend on specific tax rules and grant calls. They must be checked case by case, before starting, with whoever prepares the family's tax return and with the office managing the grant or benefit. That is the first thing to do, not the last.

## An employment contract instead of a VAT number

For a student, opening a VAT number for a single short project can be disproportionate: e-invoicing, contributions, tax returns, often an accountant. For truly occasional work there is also the *prestazione occasionale*, with its limits (see [occasional work, cooperative or VAT number](/en/articles/occasional-work-cooperative-or-vat/)).

With the cooperative the route is different: you become a member, you have an employment contract, part-time if needed, and you receive a payslip. The foreign client signs a commercial contract with the cooperative, which invoices and collects. You receive your salary with INPS contributions and INAIL cover. For many students it is also their first real payslip, and the first contributions toward a future pension.

## Fictional example

Fictional example: imagine Tommaso, a third-year computer engineering student in Pisa. During an online hackathon he meets the founders of a small Dutch startup, who offer him work on their website and some internal automation, about twelve hours a week for six months.

Before accepting, Tommaso talks to his parents and to whoever prepares their tax return, and reads the rules of his scholarship. With the picture clear, he becomes a member of the cooperative on a part-time contract. The cooperative signs a commercial contract with the startup stating hours, activities and a clause allowing Tommaso to reduce his commitment during exam weeks, agreed in advance. Tommaso logs his hours on the platform, receives his payslip every month and, at year end, the Certificazione Unica.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **You check your situation first:** family dependency, grants and benefits, any rules of your degree course.
2. **You become a member** by subscribing your share of capital and signing an employment contract, part-time if needed, under Law 142/2001 and the internal regulation.
3. **You agree with the client** activities, hours and rate, taking the academic calendar into account.
4. **The cooperative signs the commercial contract** and handles invoicing and collection, in foreign currency too.
5. **You work and log your hours** on the digital platform.
6. **You receive your payslip** with contributions and protections, and the CU at year end.

## Tips for your first client

- **Don't undervalue your rate:** being a student doesn't mean working for free. Read [negotiating your rate with a foreign client](/en/articles/negotiating-rate-foreign-client/).
- **Put exam periods in writing:** an informed client plans ahead; a surprised one complains.
- **Watch out for internships and thesis projects:** if the work overlaps with a curricular internship or your thesis, different rules may apply; talk to your university first.
- **Studying abroad:** if you leave for Erasmus or a period abroad, check how your situation changes.
