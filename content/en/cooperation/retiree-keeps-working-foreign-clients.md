Many professionals reach retirement with skills the market still wants: a process engineer who knows a sector like few others, a translator with decades of experience, a former manager whom foreign companies seek as an adviser. Clients are often abroad and the work is project-based, a few weeks or a few days a month. The question is simple: how do you keep working properly without making life complicated?

## First of all: compatibility with your pension

The main issue is not the cooperative but your pension. In Italy, the rules on combining a pension with work income **depend on the type of pension**:

- some pensions are fully compatible with new work;
- others, especially some early-retirement schemes, impose limits or bans on combining them with work income, sometimes until a certain age;
- disability and survivors' pensions have their own rules;
- those with a foreign pension or a pension under international arrangements must also consider the rules of the paying country.

These rules change over time and have exceptions. That is why we give no figures or thresholds: before accepting an engagement, **check your case with INPS or a patronato** (a free welfare advice office), bringing your pension documents. It takes little time and avoids nasty surprises, such as suspension or recovery of amounts already received.

## Why consider working as a member

Once compatibility is clear, you still need to decide how to work. Opening a VAT number at the end of a career can feel out of proportion for a few projects a year: bookkeeping, e-invoicing, an accountant, tax returns. At the {{< brand >}} cooperative, the retiree joins as a member and is hired with their own employment contract under Law 142/2001. The foreign client signs the commercial contract with the cooperative, which invoices, collects and pays the salary.

For project work, the cooperative lets you activate the contract when there is an engagement, with no fixed structure to maintain in between. On short engagements, see [a short three-month project](/en/articles/short-project-three-month-engagement/).

## Common questions from retirees

### Contributions and pension

With employment, the cooperative pays the required contributions. Whether these can lead to a pension supplement, and when, depends on pension rules: again, check with INPS or a patronato. For the general mechanism, see [pension contributions when working through a cooperative](/en/articles/pension-contributions-working-through-cooperative/).

### Taxes and tax return

Pension and work income are added together for IRPEF income tax. The cooperative withholds tax through the payslip and issues the CU, but with two sources of income it is normal for the tax return to produce a balance to settle: it is worth talking to a CAF or an adviser. See [730 and CU for the employee member](/en/articles/tax-return-employee-member-730-cu/).

### Living abroad

Many retirees spend part of the year outside Italy. Tax residence and double taxation treaties must be assessed case by case, for both the pension and the work income.

## Fictional example

Fictional example: imagine Roberto, 68, a former quality manager at an engineering company, on an old-age pension for a year. An Austrian company asks him to oversee the certification of a new plant for six months, with some site visits. Before accepting, Roberto goes to a patronato with his pension certificate and confirms that his pension can be combined with work income. He then becomes a member of the cooperative: the Austrian company signs the engagement, Roberto is hired for the project, logs his days and uploads travel expenses. When the project ends the contract closes and Roberto remains a member, ready for the next assignment.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Pension check.** Confirm with INPS or a patronato that your pension and work are compatible.
2. **Contact.** Tell us about the project and the client; we check the activity fits.
3. **Admission.** You become a member by subscribing the share and accepting the by-laws and regulation.
4. **Engagement and contract.** The client signs with the cooperative; you are hired with your own contract.
5. **Work and payslip.** You log activities and expenses; the cooperative invoices and pays you monthly.

## What to bring to the check

- Your pension certificate or award letter.
- A description of the planned work: duration, commitment, type of contract.
- Any foreign pensions or periods of work abroad.
