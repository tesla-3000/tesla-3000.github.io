Becoming a parent changes priorities, and for people who work alone with foreign clients it raises very practical questions: who pays me in the months I am not working? Will the client wait? What happens to my contributions? This page looks at the topic from one angle: the difference between working as an employed member of a cooperative and working as a freelancer when a child arrives.

For a general overview of sick leave, holidays and maternity, see also [sick leave, holidays and maternity with foreign clients](/en/articles/sick-leave-holidays-maternity-foreign-clients/).

## Three different schemes

People often say "maternity" in general, but the protections differ and are worth telling apart:

- **Maternity leave:** the mother's period off work around childbirth, with an allowance paid under INPS rules.
- **Paternity leave:** days off granted to an employed father in the first months of the child's life.
- **Parental leave:** an additional, optional period each parent can use in the child's early years, with an allowance that depends on the periods used and the rules in force.

Durations, allowances and conditions are set by law and updated from time to time: for your specific case, always check the INPS website or ask a patronato (a free welfare advice office).

## What changes with a worker-member contract

At the {{< brand >}} cooperative, the member is hired with their own employment contract, as Law 142/2001 provides. That brings employee protections for parenthood too:

- the leave application follows the procedure for employees;
- the allowance is handled under INPS rules, usually advanced through the payslip by the employer;
- during compulsory maternity leave, notional (figurative) contributions accrue under pension rules;
- the employment relationship continues: the absence does not end it.

There is also a practical side: the foreign client does not sign with you but with the cooperative. How the engagement is handled during your absence is agreed with the client through the cooperative, so you do not have to negotiate your leave alone.

## What freelancers often lack

Freelancers with a VAT number are not without protection: those registered with the Separate Scheme and the self-employed schemes have parenthood allowances. But the rules differ, contribution requirements must be checked and some typical employee protections are missing or work differently. Moreover, during the months off:

- turnover stops, while some fixed costs (accountant, minimum contributions for those who owe them) carry on;
- the client may go elsewhere, because there is no relationship that pauses, only a collaboration that ends;
- the paperwork for the application rests entirely on the professional.

This is not a judgement on freelancing, which remains a sound choice for many. It is a structural difference worth knowing before, not after. A wider comparison is in [cooperative vs flat-rate VAT number](/en/articles/cooperative-vs-flat-rate-vat-number/).

## Fictional example

Fictional example: imagine Sara, a front-end developer working from Turin for a Swedish software house through the cooperative. In her fifth month of pregnancy she gives the expected due date. The cooperative tells her which documents to submit and informs the client, agreeing to pause the engagement during her absence and resume it on her return. Sara receives the allowances under INPS rules through her payslip. Her partner Luca, an employee of another company, requests paternity leave from his own employer. On her return, Sara discusses reduced hours for a few months with the cooperative and the client.

## What to check in your case

- Contribution requirements and current amounts, on the INPS website or through a patronato.
- The rules of the collective agreement applied to your contract.
- If you come from a VAT number, how earlier periods connect with those as an employee.
