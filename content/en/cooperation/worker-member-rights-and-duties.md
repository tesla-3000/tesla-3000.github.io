Joining a worker cooperative means signing not one agreement but two. You are a member, part of the business, and a worker with a contract. Each relationship carries its own rights and duties. Knowing them upfront avoids surprises and helps you experience the cooperative for what it is: a shared business, not a service provider. Here we lay them out, with due caution: the details always depend on the by-laws, regulation and contract.

## The dual relationship in a nutshell

Italian Law 142/2001 states that the worker-member enters into, besides the membership relationship, a "further and distinct working relationship" (art. 1, para. 3). The first links them to the cooperative as a member; the second governs their work. For a general refresher, read [what being a worker-member means](/en/articles/what-worker-member-means/).

Article 1, paragraph 2 of the same law describes what worker-members do: they contribute to managing the business, take part in forming the cooperative's bodies and shaping its structure, take part in drawing up development plans, contribute to share capital and to the economic risks and results, and make their professional skills available. That list already hints at both rights and duties.

## The member's rights

### Rights that come with membership

- **Information.** Members are entitled to know the key documents: by-laws, internal regulation, financial statements. The Civil Code also gives cooperative members the right to inspect the members' register and the book of members' meetings and resolutions, and to obtain extracts (article 2545-bis), in the ways provided.
- **Participation.** Members attend the members' meeting, where financial statements and the regulation are approved and the governing bodies are appointed; they can ask questions, bring proposals and request clarifications. See [how the members' meeting works](/en/cooperation/members-meeting-how-it-works/).
- **Patronage refunds.** If there is a surplus and the members' meeting so resolves, members may receive refunds in proportion to the mutual exchange (article 2545-sexies). They are not guaranteed.
- **Share repayment.** On leaving, the share is repayable as set out in the by-laws (see [share capital](/en/articles/share-capital-what-it-is/)).

### Rights that come with the job

In an employment relationship, employee protections apply: INPS contributions paid by the cooperative, INAIL insurance, holidays, sick leave, maternity and paternity leave, severance pay (TFR), a monthly payslip and the annual CU certificate, and NASpI unemployment benefit after involuntary job loss under the legal conditions. Law 142/2001 also requires pay not to fall below the minimums of the relevant collective agreements (art. 3).

## The member's duties

- **Share capital.** Subscribe and pay it as set out in the by-laws.
- **Respect the rules.** By-laws, internal regulation and resolutions of the cooperative's bodies apply to every member.
- **Work as agreed.** Carry out the agreed activities diligently, report activities and deliveries, meet client deadlines.
- **Fair conduct.** Behave loyally towards the cooperative and other members; for example, do not misuse the cooperative's name and keep client information confidential.
- **Participation.** It is not only a right: staying informed, reading communications and attending the members' meeting is what keeps a member-owned business alive.
- **Keep data up to date.** Report changes of residence, contact details and relevant circumstances, which affect payroll and paperwork.

## How the two relationships connect

Rights and duties in the two relationships do not run separately. The law links the end of membership to the end of the working relationship: when a member withdraws or is expelled, the working relationship also ends (art. 5, para. 2, Law 142/2001). So when you consider leaving, look at both sides, as explained in [leaving the cooperative](/en/articles/leaving-the-cooperative/).

## Fictional example: Davide, developer

Fictional example: imagine Davide, a developer working remotely for a German software house. After joining, he pays his share and signs his employment contract. Each month he logs his activity on the platform and receives a payslip. When the notice of the members' meeting on the financial statements arrives, he downloads the documents from the members' area, reads them and sends two questions about the cost items. One day the client asks him to sign a non-disclosure agreement: Davide forwards it to the cooperative, which is the party to the commercial contract. In this way he uses his information rights and keeps his duty of fair conduct towards cooperative and client.

- Which specific obligations the regulation sets (reporting, exclusivity, confidentiality).
- How and when meeting notices and documents are sent.
- The grounds for expulsion in the by-laws.
- The collective agreement applied to the working relationship.
