A sponsored post for a Swedish clothing label, a video series for a Korean tech accessories maker, a campaign with an American software company. For many Italian content creators, foreign brands are not an exception: they are a large part of the work. But a brand deal is a real contract, with deliverables, deadlines, rights and payments, and deserves to be treated like one.

This page explains how a creator can handle deals with foreign brands as a worker-member of {{< brand >}}, and above all what stays outside or must be checked separately. If you work behind the scenes, editing videos for other creators, see the page on [video editors for foreign creators](/en/articles/video-editor-foreign-creators/).

## Two different income streams

A creator usually has at least two kinds of income, and it pays to keep them clearly apart.

### Brand deals

A company asks for specific content (videos, posts, stories, reviews, appearing at an event) in exchange for a fee. There is a client, an assignment, a result to deliver: this is the situation that fits the cooperative model. The brand signs a commercial contract with the cooperative, the cooperative invoices and collects, and the creator receives a salary through a payslip.

### Platform revenue

Ad revenue, follower subscriptions, creator funds, live tips: these are run by the platforms' own programmes, with their own rules and accounts held by a specific person or entity. They do not work like a client contract and should not be handled the same way. Whether and how they could fit into a relationship with the cooperative must be checked case by case, and the answer may well be no. Better to say so clearly at the outset.

## Image rights and copyright

This is the most delicate point. In a brand deal you usually grant two different things:

- **use of your image**, meaning your face, voice and name;
- **rights to the content** you create: the video, photos, texts.

The contract should state precisely for how long, in which countries, on which channels (only your own profiles? also the brand's paid ads? television too?) and for what fee. Usage "without limits of time and territory" is worth far more than a post shared for a month.

Creative works and copyright follow specific rules: how they are treated in the relationship with the cooperative must be checked before signing, for each type of content. We cover the general picture in [intellectual property in delivered work](/en/articles/intellectual-property-delivered-work/).

## Fictional example

Fictional example: imagine Sara, a creator from Naples who talks about slow travel and hiking gear. A Norwegian backpack company offers her three dedicated videos and two posts over two months, with the option of using the content in its own ads.

Sara negotiates the fee and asks to limit ad usage to six months and to European countries. The cooperative turns the agreement into a commercial contract: deliverables, dates, rounds of revisions, duration and territories of the licence, advertising disclosure rules. It invoices the brand, collects and pays Sara through her payslip.

The ad revenue from Sara's channel, on the other hand, stays outside this engagement: for that, Sara has asked for a specific assessment, separate from her brand deals.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **You become a member** by subscribing your share of capital and signing an employment contract, under Law 142/2001 and the internal regulation.
2. **You receive the brand's offer** and discuss it with the cooperative: what they want, where the content will be used, for how long.
3. **Rights are checked:** image rights and copyright are clarified before signing.
4. **The cooperative signs the commercial contract** with the brand and handles invoicing and collection, in foreign currency too.
5. **You produce and deliver** the content, logging activity and any expenses (travel, products, equipment) on the digital platform.
6. **You receive your payslip** with INPS contributions, INAIL cover and the protections of employment.

## Other things to watch

- **Advertising disclosure:** sponsored content must be labelled as such, under the rules of the platforms and of the countries where it is shown. Brands often specify their requirements: read them carefully.
- **Gifted products:** items received for free may also be relevant; consider them together with the cooperative.
- **Agencies and intermediaries:** if the deal comes through a talent agency or a matching platform, who is the client? Clarify it first.
- **Minors and sensitive content:** sectors such as alcohol, gambling, finance or health have their own rules in each country.
- **Residence:** if you live abroad for long periods, your tax position must be assessed separately.
