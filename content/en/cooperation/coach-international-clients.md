Coaching works remarkably well at a distance. A video call, a shared calendar, a few working documents between sessions: the client may be in Dublin, Singapore or Toronto and the way you work hardly changes. What does change, quite a lot, is the paperwork. Who invoices? Under which contract? How do you handle packages paid upfront and sessions used over time?

This page explains how a coach (life, business, career, executive) can work with foreign clients as a worker-member of {{< brand >}}, with their own employment contract and without opening a VAT number.

## Who the coach's clients are, and why it matters

First, an honest distinction. A coach's clients can be:

- **companies and organisations** paying for programmes for managers, teams or employees (business and executive coaching, development programmes, outplacement);
- **professionals and small businesses** buying the programme for themselves, but as a business activity;
- **private individuals** paying for a personal programme out of their own pocket.

The cooperative model fits the first two naturally: there is a commercial contract between the cooperative and an organisation, and an invoice issued to that organisation. With private individuals, especially those living abroad, the rules on invoicing, VAT and consumer protection are different and must be checked beforehand, case by case. Better to know now than at the first invoice.

## Packages, sessions and payment terms

Coaching is often sold as a package: a set number of sessions, perhaps with an initial assessment and a final review. The commercial contract should spell out:

- **what the package includes:** number and length of sessions, any materials, email support between meetings;
- **timing:** the period within which sessions must be used;
- **cancellations and rescheduling:** how much notice is needed to move a session and what happens to unused ones;
- **payment terms:** upfront, in instalments, at the end;
- **confidentiality:** what stays between coach and coachee, and what is reported to the paying company (a sensitive point in executive coaching).

That last point deserves care. When a company pays but the programme concerns one person, it helps to agree in writing which information goes back to the client. See also our page on [confidentiality and NDAs with foreign clients](/en/articles/confidentiality-nda-foreign-client/).

## Fictional example

Fictional example: imagine Chiara, a career coach based in Bologna who works in English and Spanish. A consulting firm headquartered in Madrid asks her for a ten-session programme for six newly promoted managers, plus a closing meeting with the HR lead.

Chiara designs the programme and sets her rate, then passes the details to the cooperative. The cooperative drafts the commercial contract with the Spanish firm: number of sessions, schedule, rescheduling rules, a confidentiality clause covering the content of the conversations. It invoices under the rules that apply to business-to-business services within the EU, collects payment and pays Chiara the salary set by her employment contract, with contributions and protections.

Meanwhile Chiara also coaches two professionals in London who pay through their own companies: two separate engagements, each with its own contract. A third lead, a private individual, is assessed separately precisely because they are a consumer.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **You become a member.** You subscribe your share of capital and sign an employment contract with the cooperative, under Law 142/2001 and the internal regulation.
2. **You bring the client.** You agree programme, number of sessions and rate with the client: you lead the negotiation.
3. **The cooperative signs the commercial contract.** Scope, payment terms, cancellations, confidentiality: all in writing.
4. **You work and log your activity.** On the member's digital platform you record sessions delivered and any expenses.
5. **Invoicing and collection.** The cooperative invoices the client, in foreign currency too, and follows up payments.
6. **Payslip.** You receive your salary with a payslip, INPS contributions, INAIL cover and the protections of employment.

## Why it can make sense for a coach

Many coaches start with a few clients and a practice that grows step by step. Opening a VAT number means handling e-invoicing, tax returns, contributions and often an accountant: for some it is the right choice, for others a disproportionate burden in the early phase or when coaching sits alongside another job. The cooperative offers a different route: an employment contract, a payslip, and admin handled by a structure shared with other members.

There is also the matter of image. Approaching a foreign company with a commercial contract signed by a structured organisation can help turn "I'd love to work with you" into a signed order. More in [credibility with foreign clients](/en/articles/credibility-with-foreign-client/).

## Things to check first

- **Private clients:** as mentioned, assessed separately.
- **Certifications and professional associations:** not a legal requirement for coaching in Italy, but some clients ask for them; they remain your choice.
- **The line with regulated professions:** coaching is not psychotherapy. If the work strays into areas reserved to registered professions, it cannot be handled through the cooperative.
- **Tax residence:** if you live abroad on a stable basis, your position must be assessed case by case.
