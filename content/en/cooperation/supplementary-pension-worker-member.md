The state pension is the main pillar, but for many it will not be the only one. Alongside the INPS contributions paid by the cooperative there is supplementary pension provision: pension funds and other schemes for building extra savings for retirement. For a worker-member, the choice is tied to the TFR, the Italian severance pay. This page explains the mechanism in general terms, with no promises of returns.

On the state pension, see [pension contributions when working through a cooperative](/en/articles/pension-contributions-working-through-cooperative/); on the TFR itself, see [severance pay (TFR) for the worker-member](/en/articles/severance-pay-tfr-worker-member/).

## The two pillars

- **Compulsory pension:** the INPS contributions paid by the employer (for the member, the cooperative) that determine the state pension.
- **Supplementary pension:** voluntary membership of a pension fund, fed by the TFR and, if you wish, by extra contributions from the worker and, where the collective agreement provides, from the employer.

The second pillar does not replace the first: it sits beside it.

## What forms exist

In Italy the main forms of supplementary pension are:

- **contractual (closed) funds:** set up by collective agreements for workers in a sector;
- **open funds:** run by banks, insurers or asset managers, which individuals can also join;
- **individual pension plans (PIP):** insurance products with a pension purpose.

Each has its own costs, investment options and rules. COVIP, the pension fund supervisory authority, publishes information and cost indicators on its website for comparing them.

## The TFR choice

The TFR is a share of pay set aside each year and paid out when employment ends. On being hired, the worker must decide what to do with it:

1. **keep it with the employer**, where it grows under the Civil Code rules and is paid on termination;
2. **pay it into a pension fund**, where it is invested and becomes part of the pension pot.

The rules give a period after hiring within which to make the choice using the relevant form. If the worker does not choose, **silence-consent** applies: the TFR goes to the pension scheme identified under the law and the collective agreement. Exact timing and destination should be checked when you are hired.

One important point: if you keep the TFR with the employer you can always decide later to move it to a fund, while the reverse is very limited. That is why it is a choice to make calmly and with the right information.

## What to weigh up

There is no answer that fits everyone. Some elements to consider:

- **age and time horizon:** the longer it is, the more costs and investment option matter;
- **employer contribution:** in some contractual funds, joining with your own contribution also entitles you to an employer contribution set by the collective agreement;
- **taxation:** supplementary pensions have specific tax treatment, both on contributions and on benefits, to be explored with a professional or through the fund's information documents;
- **advances:** funds allow advances in cases set by law, under conditions different from those of a TFR kept with the employer;
- **flexibility:** if you expect to change type of work often, it helps to know the fund position can be kept or transferred.

We do not quote returns or simulations: they depend on the fund, the markets and the duration, and nobody can guarantee them.

## Fictional example

Fictional example: imagine Davide, 31, an SEO consultant working from Naples for clients in Germany and Austria through the cooperative. When hired, he receives the TFR choice form. He has never had a pension fund and does not want to decide blindly: he reads the information note of the fund set by the collective agreement, checks the costs on the COVIP website and asks the cooperative about deadlines. In the end he joins and also pays a small personal contribution. Another member, Elena, who plans to buy a home soon, prefers to keep the TFR with the employer for now, knowing she can change her mind later.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Hiring.** With the employment contract you receive the information and the TFR choice form.
2. **Information.** We tell you which fund the collective agreement provides and where to find the official documents.
3. **Choice.** You decide within the set period; if you do not, silence-consent applies.
4. **Payments.** If you join, the cooperative deducts and pays the agreed amounts to the fund, shown on your payslip.
5. **Over time.** You can adjust your contribution or, if you kept the TFR with the employer, join later.
