What does a year as a worker-member actually look like? The pages on admission, payslips and tax returns explain each step. Here we lay them out on a calendar to show the rhythm of a typical year. The path from application to first salary is covered in detail in [becoming a member: from sign-up to first paycheck](/en/articles/becoming-a-member-first-paycheck/).

## Fictional example: Giulia's year

Fictional example: imagine Giulia, a translator and editor of technical texts in Padua, who works with a manuals publisher in Belgium and, from spring, with a design studio in Denmark too. Let us follow her year.

## January: admission

Giulia spoke to the cooperative in December. In January she applies for admission, subscribes her share and accepts the by-laws and internal regulation. She activates her profile on the member's digital platform and uploads the required documents.

## February: the first engagement

The Belgian publisher signs the commercial contract with the cooperative: activity, rate, payment terms. Giulia is hired with her own employment contract. From now on the client receives invoices from the cooperative and Giulia issues none. On what the contract contains, see [commercial contract contents](/en/articles/commercial-contract-contents/).

## March: the first full month

Every day, or at the end of the week, Giulia logs activities and hours on the platform. At month end the cooperative invoices the client and prepares the payslip. The following month Giulia finds it in her area, with contributions, tax withholdings and accruing TFR shown.

## April: a second engagement and expenses

The Danish studio asks for three months of editing work. A second engagement is added: Giulia now logs hours on two projects. In April she attends a trade fair in Brussels at the publisher's request and uploads tickets and receipts as expense claims, reimbursed under the internal regulation. See [expense reimbursements](/en/articles/travel-equipment-expense-reimbursements/).

## May and June: CU and 730

By March Giulia received the Certificazione Unica for the previous year from her former employer; from next year the CU will come from the cooperative. In spring, through a CAF (tax assistance centre) or the pre-filled 730, she files a return to deduct some medical expenses. Any refund or balance arrives in her payslip over the summer. Details in [tax return for the employee member: 730 and CU](/en/articles/tax-return-employee-member-730-cu/).

## July: a lighter month

The Danish engagement ends. Giulia continues with the Belgian publisher on a lighter load. She talks to the cooperative about how to manage the period and look for new clients.

## August: holidays

Giulia books her holidays, agreed in advance with the client. Holidays accrued under the contract are paid: the August payslip arrives as usual.

## September and October: new clients

Back at work, Giulia introduces the cooperative to a Swiss communications firm interested in her services. The cooperative checks the client and prepares the engagement. On presenting the model to a client, see [introducing the cooperative to a client](/en/articles/introduce-cooperative-to-client/).

## November: a late payment

One client pays a few weeks late. Giulia does not chase it: the cooperative does, and can also consider advancing payment through its dedicated fund.

## December: thirteenth month and year-end review

If the collective agreement and individual contract provide for it, the thirteenth-month payment arrives in December. Giulia reviews the year with the cooperative: engagements, hours, rates, what worked and what to change. She also joins an online meeting of the members' community, where the cooperative's progress and plans for the new year are discussed.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Admission and contract.** Share, by-laws, internal regulation and employment contract.
2. **Engagements.** Each client signs a commercial contract with the cooperative.
3. **Every month.** You log hours and expenses; the cooperative invoices, collects and prepares the payslip.
4. **Every year.** You receive the CU, consider the 730, and holidays and TFR accrue.
5. **Always.** Support via WhatsApp and email, and a members' community to share with.

A year has full months and empty ones: nobody can guarantee a steady flow of engagements, but the structure stays the same. Want to picture your own year as a member? [Contact us](/en/contact/).
