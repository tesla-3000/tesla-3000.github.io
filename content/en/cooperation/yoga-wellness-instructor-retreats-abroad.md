People who teach yoga, pilates, meditation or other wellbeing disciplines increasingly work across borders: a centre in Portugal running a week-long retreat, a Dutch travel organiser looking for a teacher for a group trip, a British platform streaming live classes. Short jobs, spread across the year, with different clients. The usual problem is how to set them up properly without turning a vocation into a small business full of paperwork.

## Work made of events and continuity

A wellness instructor's work has two sides. On one side, events: a seven-day retreat, an intensive weekend, a festival. On the other, continuity: an online class every Tuesday and Thursday, perhaps for months. Both need a clear contract with the organiser, a simple way to get paid and some protection for the periods when you are not teaching, or when you are off sick.

With an Italian VAT number, every engagement becomes an invoice, with VAT rules for cross-border services, bookkeeping and contributions to pay yourself. It is a legitimate choice and it works for many. But for people who alternate busy and quiet periods, or who are just starting out with foreign clients, there is an alternative.

## Teaching as a worker-member

At the {{< brand >}} cooperative, the instructor joins as a member and is hired with their own employment contract, as provided by Law 142/2001. The retreat organiser or platform does not sign with the instructor but with the cooperative: a commercial contract, the engagement, setting out dates, place, fee and expenses. The cooperative invoices, collects payment, also in foreign currency, and turns the fee into a payslip.

That means INPS contributions paid, INAIL cover, and holidays, sick leave and severance pay (TFR) accruing under the contract. For what changes compared with invoicing, see [a payslip instead of invoices](/en/articles/payslip-instead-of-invoices/).

## Typical questions from retreat teachers

### Travel

A retreat abroad means travel, sometimes long. Documented work-related expenses can be reimbursed under the cooperative's internal regulation, but it is worth agreeing beforehand with the organiser what they cover directly (accommodation, meals, flights) and what is to be reimbursed. See [travel and equipment expense reimbursements](/en/articles/travel-equipment-expense-reimbursements/) and, for longer periods outside Italy, [working abroad and secondment](/en/articles/working-abroad-secondment/).

### Certifications

Many organisers ask for a training certificate recognised in their market. The cooperative does not issue teaching qualifications: they remain personal and are shown to the client. The commercial contract can, however, describe the required profile and skills clearly.

### Responsibility towards participants

Working with people's bodies calls for care. Besides the worker's INAIL cover, the cooperative's package includes professional liability insurance; read its limits and conditions carefully, as some specific activities may need checking. See [INAIL and professional liability insurance](/en/articles/inail-professional-liability-insurance/).

### Online classes for foreign platforms

If the platform is the client and signs the engagement, online classes follow the same pattern. Hours and activities are recorded on the member's digital platform and flow into that month's payslip.

## Fictional example

Fictional example: imagine Chiara, a yoga teacher in Bologna. Every year she leads three retreats for a German organiser in Sardinia and Greece, and from September to June she teaches two weekly online classes for a London-based platform. Instead of opening a VAT number, she becomes a member of the cooperative. Each organisation signs an engagement with the cooperative. Chiara records her retreat days and teaching hours on the platform, uploads the train receipts for getting to the ferry and receives her payslip every month. When she stops for a week with flu in February, the medical certificate follows the rules for employees. She negotiated her rates with the clients herself; the cooperative put everything else in writing and handled it.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Contact and check.** Tell us about your work and clients: we check that the activity fits and that the client can sign a commercial contract.
2. **Admission.** You become a member by subscribing the share and accepting the by-laws and internal regulation.
3. **Engagement.** The cooperative signs a contract with the organiser or platform covering dates, activity, fee and expenses.
4. **Employment contract.** You are hired with your own contract, consistent with the type of activity.
5. **Work and records.** You teach, log hours and days and upload expenses on the platform.
6. **Invoice and payslip.** The cooperative invoices and collects; you receive a monthly payslip.

## What to consider first

- If your clients are mostly individual participants paying you directly, the model needs careful assessment.
- If you live abroad for long periods, tax residence and double taxation treaties must be checked case by case.
- If you teach in Italian gyms under specific sports contracts, those rules have to be considered separately.
