An IT project with developers in Poland, designers in Portugal, the product owner in London and the client in New York: the project manager holds people, deadlines and budget together across time zones. When the client hands project management to an external professional, it wants a service with clear results and a monthly invoice. {{< brand >}} proposes a work structure with protections.

## What a PM actually does in a remote team

The work is concrete: sprint and roadmap planning, agile ceremonies (planning, daily, retrospective), backlog management in Jira or similar, risks and dependencies, sponsor reports, budget control. In a distributed team, communication rules weigh heavily: overlap hours, channels (Slack, email, calls), documentation. The client judges predictability: regular deliveries, reliable estimates, early warning of problems.

Agree in the contract:

- scope of the service and responsibilities (what the PM decides, what the client approves);
- deliverables and reports (milestones, releases, progress);
- availability windows for team meetings;
- tools and access;
- duration and notice.

## The cooperative's role

The cooperative signs a service contract with the client and is accountable for it; then it invoices. For EU clients the reverse charge normally applies (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72); for non-EU clients generic B2B services are normally outside Italian VAT: checked case by case. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have a work relationship with INPS contributions, INAIL and, if employment, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip.

## Typical pitfalls

- **A supplier role, not a client employee.** If the client wants to set your hours and manage you like one of its employees, it is not a service the cooperative can handle: in that case the right route is direct hiring. We do not place people with companies that manage them as their own employees.
- **Responsibility for outcomes.** Clarify what is an obligation of means and what of result.
- **Budget and scope.** Scope changes without budget review are the classic problem.
- **Responsibility for other people.** Coordinating does not mean disciplinary power.
- **Endless meetings.** Set a cap on hours and cadence.

## Example

Example: imagine Stefano, a project manager in Rome, to whom a fintech scale-up in Dublin entrusts managing the release of a new app: six months, a team of eight across Poland, Portugal and Ireland. The contract sets milestones, sprint cadence, a monthly sponsor report and a monthly fee tied to the phases. The cooperative invoices; Stefano organises his own work and logs activities and reports on the platform and receives a payslip with employee protections. Fictional character.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Intro call.** You explain project, duration, deliverables, price.
2. **Joining.** Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Contract.** Service contract with the client.
4. **Employment contract.** Your relationship with the cooperative.
5. **Activity.** Activities, reports and expenses on the platform.
6. **Invoicing and payslip.** The cooperative invoices and collects; you receive the payslip.

INAIL, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

## Compared with a VAT number

The flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) works if you have several clients and margin; for long projects with defined goals and deliverables the cooperative simplifies admin and offers more protections.

## Checklist before signing

Before accepting, ask for: the org chart and who sponsors the project; the budget and how it is tracked; the methodology in use (Scrum, Kanban, hybrid); backlog status and technical debt; tools and licences. Ask for a week of onboarding with all stakeholders. Define report frequency and the criteria by which your work is judged: deadlines, quality, team satisfaction. In a remote team, a shared communication-rules document from day one avoids a lot of friction.
