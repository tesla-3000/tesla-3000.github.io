A Berlin scale-up has three hundred enterprise customers and a customer success team in Germany. For its Italian customers it needs someone who knows the language and the market: onboarding, periodic reviews, renewals. Rather than hiring, it wants to hand this piece of work to an outside professional with measurable goals. That is a service you can offer through the cooperative.

## The service in practice

Customer success is not technical support: the goal is that customers use the product and renew. A well-defined service includes:
- **onboarding** new Italian customers with a 30-60-90 day activation plan;
- **quarterly business reviews** with usage metrics;
- **churn-risk management**, with signals such as drops in logins or repeated tickets;
- **flagging upgrade opportunities** to the sales team;
- a **monthly report** with feedback for the product team.

The metrics (renewal rate, adoption of core features, satisfaction) become the contract's KPIs.

## Where people stumble

1. **Time zones**: some calls with the foreign office are unavoidable. Agree availability windows for meetings in the contract, not working hours.
2. **Targets on inherited accounts** in poor shape: set a measured starting baseline.
3. **Scope**: without a written scope the service drifts into technical support and sales at once.
4. **Contacts**: a single contact person on the client side for priorities and approvals avoids requests from many people.

## Example: a year of service

*A fictional example.* Imagine Elena, four years in customer care at a Milan software house. The German scale-up asks her to manage its twenty Italian accounts for twelve months, with renewal KPIs and a monthly report. She prefers a stable relationship with holidays and sick leave, without opening a VAT number. The cooperative signs the service contract with a monthly fee; Elena is offered an employment contract as a member. She organises her own work and records activities and reports on the platform; the payslip includes INPS contributions and INAIL cover.

## Signals the project is healthy

Before accepting, ask for the churn rate of the accounts involved, the number of accounts and who decides renewal discounts. An overly large portfolio makes a quality service impossible. Check that there is an escalation path to product and technical support. Write these conditions into the contract and review them after the first quarter, when real numbers are visible. And keep building other clients: depending on just one is a risk for anyone.

## And if I prefer a VAT number?

Under the flat-rate regime (85,000 euro threshold, 15% or 5% in the first five years with requirements) you gain flexibility but bear Separate Management contributions and paperwork; it works best with several clients. The cooperative is more straightforward when you want employee protections and no admin work.
