A Copenhagen clothing brand has decided to produce in Italy and needs someone who can choose fabrics and workshops. An American footwear e-commerce company wants a trend scout for the next season. Italy remains a reference for fashion, and foreign brands look for local consultants. Those with taste and a network meet a fragmented kind of work: days in the studio, photo sets, trips to Milan, Prato or Florence.

## What fashion consulting means for a foreign brand

Recurring activities are:
- **styling for shoots** and lookbooks: selecting garments and accessories, coordinating photographer and models;
- **sourcing**: finding fabrics, leathers and workshops with sample runs;
- **collection consulting**: palette consistency, sizing, price tiers;
- **accompanying visits** to fairs and showrooms with buyer appointments;
- **quality control** of samples and prototypes.

Deliverables are moodboards, sourcing sheets, image sets and visit reports. Pay is often **per day** (shoots, visits) plus a **per-project** fee for the collection.

## Where time and money are lost

1. **Advancing expenses**: garments, rentals and accessories paid out of pocket. Better a client budget.
2. **Returning garments**: who pays if a sample is damaged?
3. **Cancelled shoots**: you need a minimum fee and notice period.
4. **Relations with workshops**: the consultant introduces, but the production contract stays between brand and workshop.
5. **Use of your image**: if you appear in photos, you need a release and an agreement.

## Organising the work on set

Prepare a sheet for each look with references, sizes, accessories and the origin of garments, and keep the paperwork for withdrawals and returns from showrooms. Agree with the client who handles transport and insurance of valuable items. Define set hours and breaks in writing, because shoots tend to stretch. At the end, deliver an orderly archive with a list of garments used and supplier credits: a detail foreign brands appreciate and that helps win new assignments.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Tell us about the project**: duration, days, deliverables, sample budget.
2. **Membership** as a worker-member with a share of capital refundable on exit (Law 142/2001).
3. **Contract** with the brand: day rates, expenses, image rights.
4. **Platform**: you log days and expenses; documents are signed online.
5. **Invoicing and foreign clients**: the cooperative invoices the brand (EU or non-EU) under B2B rules verified case by case.
6. **Pay**: payslip with contributions, INAIL and professional liability included; advance if needed.

## VAT number or cooperative

With the flat-rate regime (85,000 euro threshold, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years with requirements) you can build your own studio with several brands, handling invoices, contributions and returns yourself. It is a good base if clients are many and continuous. The cooperative suits seasonal projects with a single foreign client wanting a structured counterpart. If you work with creative works and copyright, the rules are specific and must be verified.
