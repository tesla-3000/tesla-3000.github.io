A company in Sao Paulo has opened a sales office in Milan with twelve people. Headquarters has an IT department, but everything is far away and in another time zone: someone is needed to configure computers, solve everyday problems, manage the office network and bridge to central IT. The IT technician who follows a foreign client in Italy is a very concrete figure, halfway between sysadmin and local support.

## What an assignment like this involves

- **Onboarding** new employees: preparing laptops, accounts, access, software.
- **Daily support**: printers, VPN, email, video-conference links.
- **Local network management**: Wi-Fi, firewall, dealings with the connectivity provider.
- **Inventory** of equipment and warranty tracking.
- **Basic security**: updates, backups, credential management and lost devices.
- **Liaison** with the central IT team, via tickets and periodic meetings.

Such an assignment often translates into a **support contract with service levels (SLA)**: scheduled maintenance, on-site interventions on call, remote tickets and guaranteed response times for emergencies, for a monthly fee.

## The delicate points

1. **Scope**: where local support ends and systems administration, which stays with central IT, begins.
2. **On-call**: should a network outage at 6 pm be fixed immediately? If yes, it needs pay.
3. **Purchasing materials**: authorisation processes for spending, so you do not advance large sums.
4. **System access**: minimum privileges and traceability. A technician with too many permissions is a risk for everyone.
5. **Continuity**: record configurations and passwords in a shared manager, so the client does not depend on one person.

## Documenting so you are not indispensable

A technician following a site alone risks becoming the only one who knows where everything is. Keep a configuration log, a list of accounts and a calendar of licence and warranty expiries, shared with central IT. Agree a stand-in for holidays, even among members of the community. A written procedure for employee joining and leaving reduces errors and security risks and gives even a distant client the impression of an orderly service.
