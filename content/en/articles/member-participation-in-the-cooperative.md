In many companies, people who work stay far from the direction of the business. In a worker cooperative it is different: members are part of the cooperative and have a voice in its life. Taking part means being informed, being able to speak up, proposing ideas and contributing to a community. Here is what it means for those working with {{< brand >}}.

## Why participation matters

In a capital company, workers are usually outside ownership. In a cooperative the worker-member is part of the enterprise: mutuality, the aim of offering members work and services on better terms than they would find alone, only really works if members are informed and involved. Someone who has just joined has the same access to information and discussion as someone who has been there for years. The precise rules are in the by-laws and the internal regulation.

## Where participation happens

**The members' assembly.** It brings together all members and deals with the fundamental topics: accounts, company offices, by-laws and use of surpluses. It meets as the by-laws provide, at least once a year, and may allow remote participation, useful for those working in other cities or countries.

**The board of directors.** It manages the cooperative within the framework set by the assembly and reports to members. It is the natural point of contact for questions, reports and structured proposals.

**The internal regulation.** Law 142/2001 requires the cooperative to have a regulation governing how members' work is organised. Every member receives it and can know it in detail, and it goes through the assembly.

**The community.** Not everything is formal: members compare notes, propose services and training, flag problems, share contacts and advice. It is where many ideas are born before being brought to the cooperative's attention.

## Example: Beatrice and her proposal

Imagine Beatrice, a translator who has been a member for a year. She notices that many colleagues ask for legal English courses. She raises the proposal in the community, gathers interest from other members and presents it to the cooperative. It is discussed in terms of costs and interest, and assessed for inclusion among training activities. There is no guarantee that every proposal is accepted: taking part does not mean always getting what you ask for, but having the chance to contribute, be heard and receive a reasoned answer.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Becoming a member.** You subscribe the share capital quota and are admitted.
2. **Notice.** You receive the notice of the assembly with agenda and documents.
3. **Information.** The accounts and topics under discussion are made available to you.
4. **Participation.** You speak and share your view, in person or remotely, under the rules.
5. **Decisions.** What the assembly establishes applies to all members.
6. **Updates.** You learn the outcomes through the platform and the community.

## Responsibilities that come with participation

Taking part also means taking on responsibilities: informing yourself about the topics, respecting shared decisions, behaving fairly towards other members. Participation concerns the life of the cooperative, not individual work contracts: your pay and conditions follow your contract and the regulation, and are not reopened each time.

## You do not need to be an expert

You do not need to know company law to take part: documents should be understandable and you can always ask support for explanations. If you work from afar, ask which remote participation methods are provided. If you prefer to stay in the background, you can be a member who works, reads the updates and speaks up when you see fit.

## Transparency and information

Real participation requires understandable information. Before the assembly you are entitled to know the documents under discussion, in particular the accounts and proposed amendments. If a point is unclear, ask support for explanations before the meeting: a question raised in advance often leads to a more useful discussion than an improvised objection. Remember too that you can stand for office, if the by-laws allow, and contribute directly to guiding the cooperative.
