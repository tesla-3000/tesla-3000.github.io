When a series is released in a new language, behind the subtitles stands a chain of professionals: localisation studios, subtitlers, dialogue adapters, reviewers. The Italian subtitler often works for European or American studios serving streaming platforms. {{< brand >}} proposes a structure for working steadily, with a contract and a payslip.

## How the work runs

Jobs usually arrive through the studio (vendor), which assigns episodes or films with very short deadlines, often 24 to 72 hours. You are paid **per minute of finished product** (with different rates for subtitles, adaptation, review, SDH for the deaf and hard of hearing, spotting) or hourly for reviews. Studios impose style guides (characters per line, reading speed, handling of names) and use their own tools. Contracts include strict confidentiality, often an NDA per title.

## How the cooperative fits

The cooperative signs the agreement with the studio and invoices. For EU clients the reverse charge normally applies (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72); for non-EU clients generic B2B services are normally outside Italian VAT: checked case by case. The worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) has a work relationship with the cooperative: INPS contributions, INAIL, and if employment, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. Studio payments at 30 to 60 days can be bridged with the fund advance.

## Typical pitfalls

- **NDAs and security.** Unreleased material: strict rules on where and how you work.
- **Quality scores.** Many studios score work: clarify the consequences of a low score.
- **Workload peaks.** Compressed deadlines, uneven volumes: the employment contract should be set realistically.
- **Rework.** Clarify who pays for changes after quality control.
- **Adaptation copyright.** Dialogue adaptation has a specific regime: check case by case.

## Example

Example: imagine Francesca, a subtitler in Rome, working for two studios, one in Dublin and one in Los Angeles, translating 40 to 50-minute series from English into Italian. Each studio has its own agreement with a per-minute rate. Francesca logs delivered episodes and review hours on the platform; the cooperative invoices the studios and Francesca receives her payslip. Character and data are fictional.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Intro call.** You send studios, rates, volumes and tools.
2. **Joining.** Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Agreements.** Contract with each studio.
4. **Activity.** Episodes and hours on the platform.
5. **Invoicing.** The cooperative invoices and collects.
6. **Payslip.** Monthly pay and documents online.

INAIL, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

## And a VAT number?

Many subtitlers have worked for years under the flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met). It remains valid, though contributions and paperwork are yours. The cooperative is a choice for those who prefer worker protections.

## Checklist before signing

Before accepting a new studio, verify: per-minute rates for each activity; the final platform's style guide; tools and licences; the policy on deadlines and rework; NDAs and where you may work; the scoring and payment system (monthly, at 45 or 60 days). Ask for a paid test before committing. Keep a sheet with minutes delivered, studio and payment date: it shows which clients pay best over time. Share it with us to set up agreements and advances.
