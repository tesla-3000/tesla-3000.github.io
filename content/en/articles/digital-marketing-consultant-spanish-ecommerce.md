A Spanish cosmetics e-commerce wants to bring its shop to Italy: it needs someone to set up Meta and Google campaigns, optimise the product feed, run email marketing and the purchase funnel. For an Italian consultant it is recurring, well-paid work, but Spanish clients (often small businesses) want a supplier with an invoice and a contract. {{< brand >}} provides the structure.

## How retainers work in e-commerce

Consultants usually work on a **monthly retainer** for a package of activities (campaign management, reporting, creative tests, feed and landing page optimisation), sometimes with a **variable extra** tied to generated revenue. The ad budget stays with the client. E-commerce is seasonal: Black Friday, sales, Christmas. The contract should state:

- included activities and estimated hours;
- frequency of reports and calls;
- accesses (Meta Business, Google Ads, Analytics, Shopify) and who owns them;
- indicative goals, without guarantees of results.

## The cooperative's role

The cooperative signs the agreement with the Spanish company and invoices the retainer. For B2B services to the EU the reverse charge generally applies (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72), checked case by case. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have a work relationship with INPS contributions, INAIL and, if employment, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. A sales-linked bonus can be provided for in the contract, without promising amounts.

## Typical pitfalls

- **Shaky tracking.** Poorly configured pixels and conversions: check before committing to KPIs.
- **Account ownership.** Ad accounts must belong to the client, not to you.
- **ROAS expectations.** Guaranteeing returns is risky; better a plan with tests.
- **Client budget.** A budget too low limits results: say so.
- **Language and market.** Copy and offer for Italy must be adapted, not translated literally.

## Example

Example: imagine Andrea, a consultant from Padua, working with a Valencia e-commerce selling pet products. Six-month retainer: Meta and Google campaign management for Italy, one monthly report, two calls. The ad budget is the client's with shared access. The cooperative invoices the retainer in euro under reverse charge; Andrea logs hours and activities on the platform and gets a monthly payslip. Fictional character.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Intro call.** You explain client, activities, rate, KPIs.
2. **Joining.** Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Agreement.** Contract with the Spanish e-commerce.
4. **Activity.** Hours, reports and expenses on the platform.
5. **Invoicing.** The cooperative invoices and collects.
6. **Payslip.** Monthly pay and documents online.

INAIL, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

## Compared with a VAT number

Under the flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) a consultant with many small clients gets autonomy and low costs, but contributions and paperwork are theirs. The cooperative may be preferable with a few stable clients.

## Checklist before signing

Before starting, ask the client for: read-only access to existing accounts for an audit; historical sales and margin data; the catalogue and seasonality; the monthly ad budget and who approves it; the customer service lead, because campaigns bring questions and returns. Set a first month of analysis and a test plan. Agree how work is judged: not only sales but also tracking quality, tests run and funnel improvements. Document everything in a dashboard shared with the client.
