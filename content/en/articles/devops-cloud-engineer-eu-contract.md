Infrastructure as code, CI/CD pipelines, Kubernetes, observability, cloud costs: an experienced DevOps or cloud engineer is in high demand from European companies, especially those without an in-house team. Engagements are often on contract, three to twelve months, and carry serious responsibility for production systems. {{< brand >}} supplies the contractual structure and protections.

## How engagements are set up

A typical DevOps engagement is a project (cloud migration, pipeline build, security hardening) or an ongoing service (infrastructure management, on-call support). Clients ask for availability in defined hours, access to cloud consoles and repositories, documentation (runbooks) and compliance with security policies. Points to define in the agreement:

- scope: which systems and environments;
- service levels and response hours;
- on-call duty and compensation;
- handling of credentials and privileged access;
- cloud costs: who pays and who monitors.

## The cooperative's role

The cooperative signs the agreement with the EU company and invoices. For B2B services to the EU the reverse charge generally applies (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72), checked case by case. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have a work relationship with the cooperative: INPS contributions, INAIL and, if employment, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. The professional liability insurance in the package is useful for work with operational responsibility; limits and conditions should be checked.

## Typical pitfalls

- **Unpaid on-call.** The informal "can you be around if needed?" does not hold: write it down and compensate it.
- **Privileged access.** Use named accounts and MFA; avoid shared credentials.
- **Runaway cloud costs.** A misconfiguration can inflate the bill: define budgets and alerts.
- **Missing documentation.** Without runbooks the client depends on you; a risk for both.
- **Scope changes.** From "pipeline" to "run the whole platform": redefine.

## Example

Example: imagine Gabriele, a DevOps engineer from Turin, hired by a software house in Stockholm to migrate infrastructure to Kubernetes in the cloud: eight months, four days a week, with rotating on-call one weekend a month. The agreement regulates on-call and compensation, access and cloud budget. The cooperative invoices under reverse charge; Gabriele logs activities and interventions on the platform and receives a monthly payslip. Fictional character.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Intro call.** You present scope, duration, days, on-call, rate.
2. **Joining.** Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Agreement.** Contract with the EU company with SLAs and responsibilities.
4. **Activity.** Days, interventions and expenses on the platform.
5. **Invoicing.** The cooperative invoices and collects.
6. **Payslip.** Monthly pay and documents online.

INAIL, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

## Compared with a VAT number

Under the flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) a DevOps engineer with several clients has full autonomy; contributions, paperwork and insurance are yours. The cooperative makes more sense for long engagements with one main client.

## Checklist before signing

Before kickoff, request: the inventory of existing infrastructure and its documentation; the roles and permissions you will be given; the security policy (MFA, secrets management, audits); the incident management and escalation process; monthly cloud costs and a spending cap. Agree with the client a shadowing period and a handover at the end. Assess the risk: an engagement with responsibility for critical production needs clear hours, on-call terms and insurance limits.
