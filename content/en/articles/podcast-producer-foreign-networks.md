A Los Angeles podcast network has an English series on history and cooking and wants an Italian version; a German mobility brand launches a corporate podcast and seeks a producer to handle everything, from recording to release. Audio production is a craft and repetitive job: one episode a week, every week, with fixed deadlines.

## What a producer does

- **Ideation and outline** of the episode, finding guests and questions.
- **Recording**: in studio or remote, with audio quality control.
- **Editing and mixing**: cleaning speech, music, theme, loudness for platforms.
- **Publishing**: title, description, cover art, chapters, distribution.
- **Promotion materials**: audio clips, transcripts, social copy.
- **Statistics** and client reports.

Deliverables are the finished episodes, an archive of working files, publication texts and a monthly report.

## Where time is lost

1. **Guests who cancel**: you need a cancellation fee and a plan B.
2. **Remote audio quality**: unstable connections force hours of cleaning.
3. **Growing episodes**: a twenty-minute episode becomes sixty without an adjustment of fee.
4. **Music and licences**: who guarantees the right to use a track?
5. **Calendar**: seriality is unforgiving. One late episode shifts everything.

## Care of sound and seriality

Set a template for episodes with a fixed structure and a checklist before publishing: audio levels, titles, description, cover. Always record a minute of room silence to clean background noise and ask guests for suitable headphones and microphone. Keep a buffer of two or three finished episodes so an unforeseen event does not interrupt the schedule. Keep the original files and editing projects: if the client asks for a new version, you will not start from zero.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Tell us about the project**: number of episodes, length, format, deadlines.
2. **Membership** with a share of capital refundable on exit (Law 142/2001).
3. **Contract**: fee, cancellations, music licences, audio rights.
4. **Platform**: episode log, expenses (studio hire, subscriptions), documents.
5. **Invoicing**: the foreign network, in dollars too, under B2B rules verified case by case.
6. **Pay**: payslip with INPS, INAIL and professional liability included; advance available.

## VAT number or cooperative

A producer with several clients may prefer the flat-rate regime (85,000 euro threshold, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years with requirements), with Separate Management contributions and their own invoices. It is a good base for working with many different podcasts. If instead you follow a long series from a single network, the cooperative turns episodes into a regular payslip, with holidays, sick leave and severance pay. Creative works and music rights have specific rules to be checked.
