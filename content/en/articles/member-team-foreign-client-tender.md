Foreign companies and organisations publish requests for proposals: a website rebuild, market research, a video production, a training programme. Responding takes varied skills, a credible financial proposal and a solid contracting party. For a single freelancer it is hard; for a team of {{< brand >}} members it can become an orderly process.

## Why the cooperative helps with tenders

Many tenders and RFPs ask for a structured supplier able to issue invoices, handle foreign-currency payments, provide insurance and clear documentation. A group of freelancers without structure struggles to present as one bidder. The cooperative can act as the contracting party with the client, while members provide the work. The foreign client signs an engagement with the cooperative, not with individuals.

Note: taking part in a specific tender depends on the requirements it sets; we check them together case by case, without promising outcomes.

## Example: Chiara's team

Example: imagine Chiara, a project manager, who finds a request for proposals from a Belgian organisation for an e-learning platform. It needs an instructional designer, a developer and a graphic designer. Chiara brings in Paolo and Sara, both members or about to join.

The team prepares the proposal: Chiara coordinates, Paolo estimates content, Sara development hours. The cooperative checks requirements, rates and payment terms, then submits the offer. If the organisation accepts, the engagement starts and each person has their own employment relationship; if not, the team still has a ready proposal to reuse.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Report.** You send us the RFP: deadline, requirements, estimated value.
2. **Feasibility check.** We verify whether the cooperative can bid and which documents are needed.
3. **Team composition.** You identify the members needed and their roles.
4. **Estimate and proposal.** The team prepares time and cost; the cooperative reviews terms, invoicing and payment.
5. **Submission.** The offer is submitted by the cooperative.
6. **Award and engagement.** If you win, the commercial contract is signed and each member has their employment relationship.
7. **Delivery.** Activities and deliveries are logged on the platform; the cooperative invoices and collects.

## Practical points

Time spent preparing the bid is a delicate point: agree beforehand how to treat it, because until an engagement exists there is no paid employment relationship. Better to decide together how much to invest in the proposal and with which responsibilities.

Another point is payment terms. Foreign clients often pay slowly, and the cooperative can consider an advance through the fund. For VAT on invoices to EU or non-EU clients we refer to a check on the actual case.

Finally, professional liability: professional indemnity insurance is included in the package for members, but limits and coverage should be compared with what the tender requires.

## Frequent mistakes in foreign tenders

Underestimating time: tender deadlines are rigid and clarification requests to the client take days. Promising in the offer what nobody in the team can guarantee: better to state roles and limits clearly. Forgetting payment terms: between advance, progress payments and balance, set a realistic calendar. Finally, ignoring language: an offer written in polished English, or in the client's language, counts for more than you might think.

## Questions to ask before taking part

Does the value of the engagement justify the preparation time? Has the team worked together before or is this the first time? Does the client state a budget? Are there references requested that you must be able to document? Answering honestly avoids chasing unsuitable tenders and lets you focus energy on those where you have a real edge, whether in experience, language or specialisation.
