One question always comes up: "if the client pays X, how much do I receive?" The honest answer is that there is no universal coefficient: it depends on contract, job classification and costs. There is, however, a logic, and understanding it lets you do realistic sums without being promised figures.

## The starting point: the agreed rate

You start from the price the client is willing to pay for the work: hourly, daily or per project. This is the figure that appears in the agreement and on the invoice. Everything else builds on it.

## From rate to labour cost

In an employment relationship, the labour cost for the cooperative consists of:

- the member's **gross pay**, per contract and job classification;
- **employer social security contributions** (INPS) and **workplace accident insurance** (INAIL);
- **accruals** for holiday, leave, any additional monthly payments and **severance (TFR)**;
- **other charges** provided by the applicable contract.

On top come service costs: administrative management, platform, invoicing and collection, professional liability insurance, support, training. It is the part you would otherwise bear yourself through an accountant, software and policies.

## From gross to net

From gross pay you get to net by deducting the worker's own contributions and applying progressive income tax with withholding at source and year-end adjustment, plus regional and municipal surtaxes. Net pay therefore also depends on deductions and family situation. That is why it is right to speak of a personalised estimate, not a fixed percentage.

## Reasoning backwards

Often the member knows the pay they want and must work out which rate to ask. The reasoning is reversed: start from the desired gross pay, add employer costs and the service, and you get the minimum sustainable rate. A margin for unpaid days (holidays, illness, gaps between assignments) should be included realistically.

## Example: Paolo, backend developer

A fictional example, with deliberately unreal numbers: Paolo wants a monthly gross pay "R". The cooperative computes the client's total cost as R plus employer contributions and charges, accruals and TFR, plus the service fee. Paolo notices the client likes a fixed monthly fee: he converts the sum to a daily rate, using actual working days. He then asks the cooperative for a net pay simulation with his personal situation. He uses the result to set a price that leaves him a margin, rather than relying on generic gross-to-net ratios.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Data gathering.** You provide the activity, expected commitment and the rate discussed with the client.

3. **Member simulation.** The payslip is estimated using personal data.
4. **Alignment.** If the rate does not cover costs, you discuss it with the client or revise the terms.
5. **Periodic check.** Through the platform you verify that hours and amounts match what was planned.

## What to check

- How costs are presented in the quote (line by line).
- Which days are paid and which are not.
- The effect of expense reimbursements (they are not pay).
- Client payment times and the advance fund.
