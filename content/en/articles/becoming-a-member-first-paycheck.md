Joining a worker cooperative is not like opening an account: it is a path with a few formal steps, but a straightforward one. Here is the typical sequence at {{< brand >}}, from first contact to first payslip, with the points where it pays to stop and check.

## Before you start: what you need

You need an activity to carry out (usually for a client, possibly foreign), identity documents and tax code, an IBAN, and a clear description of the work: what you will do, how often, for how long. If your profession requires registration with a professional body holding exclusive rights, this path may not suit you: check first.

## The steps, one by one

### 1. Initial conversation
You describe your activity and the client. The cooperative assesses whether the work fits its model and explains conditions and costs.

### 2. Admission request
You fill in the membership application. Admission is decided by the cooperative according to its by-laws.

### 3. Share capital
The member subscribes a share of capital, refundable on leaving according to the by-laws. Amounts and terms are in the documents, not in a blog article.

### 4. Regulation and employment contract
The member receives the internal regulation and signs the employment contract (normally as an employee) with the cooperative. Read both calmly: this is the right moment to ask questions.

### 5. Agreement with the client
The cooperative signs the commercial contract with the client. The member does not sign as a supplier: the agreement is between cooperative and client.

### 6. Platform access
You receive credentials to log hours and activities, upload expense claims and consult documents.

### 7. Work, reporting, payslip
You work, report within the stated deadlines, the cooperative invoices the client, runs payroll and pays contributions and withholdings. Then the first pay arrives.

## Example: Andrea, marketing consultant

A fictional example: Andrea is a marketing consultant working with an Irish company. On Monday he has the intake call, within a few days receives the admission paperwork, subscribes the share and signs contract and regulation. Meanwhile the cooperative agrees the commercial contract with the company. Andrea starts working, logs hours and activities on the platform and at month end sees his first payslip. The real time between steps depends on how quickly client and member respond.

## Where time is lost

- Incomplete or unreadable documents.
- A commercial contract with client-specific requests to negotiate.
- Late reporting: the payslip follows the monthly cycle.
- Client payment delays: ask how timing and the advance fund work.

## What to check before signing

- Amount of the share and refund conditions.
- Type of employment contract, duration, job classification.
- Service costs and how they are calculated: see also the guide on how client cost is worked out.
- Indicative payment times.
