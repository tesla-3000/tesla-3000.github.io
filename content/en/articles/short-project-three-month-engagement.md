Many international projects are short by nature: a prototype to deliver, a systems migration, a campaign launch, a large technical translation. Three months, one client, one clear goal. For a self-employed professional, though, opening a VAT number for such a limited job means paperwork that can weigh more than the job itself. This is one of the cases where working as a worker-member of {{< brand >}} is simply more practical.

## Why a short project is a special case

On a three-month engagement the fixed costs of your own set-up are very noticeable: opening and managing a tax position, bookkeeping, electronic invoicing, contribution deadlines. You would start all of that and then possibly close it right after the project ends. With the cooperative, the relationship is framed as employment: Italian Law 142/2001 provides that the member enters, besides the membership relationship, a further and distinct work relationship, which may be employment, self-employment or another form.

In the employee case you get a monthly payslip, INPS social contributions paid by the cooperative, INAIL insurance, holidays, sick leave and TFR accrual. Your foreign client does not need to know any of this: it signs a commercial contract with the cooperative and receives invoices from it.

## Example: Marta and the three-month project

Example: imagine Marta, a front-end developer, whom a Dutch software house asks to rebuild a portal interface in three months. Marta has no VAT number and does not want one for a single project. She contacts the cooperative, joins by subscribing the share capital quota, and tells us the scope, duration and rate agreed with the client.

The cooperative signs the engagement with the Dutch company and assigns the activities to Marta. Each week she logs her activities on the platform, and each month she receives a payslip. On delivery the engagement closes and the relationship ends with the end-of-employment entitlements.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Initial call.** You describe the project: client, duration, type of work, working arrangements.
2. **Membership.** You become a member by subscribing the share capital quota, refundable on exit according to the by-laws.
3. **Commercial contract.** The cooperative formalises the engagement with the foreign client; you sign nothing with them.
4. **Employment contract.** We define your employment relationship for the length of the project, in line with the internal regulation.
5. **Delivery.** You log activities and attendance on the platform and can ask for support via WhatsApp or email.
6. **Invoicing and collection.** We invoice the client, including in foreign currency, and manage the collection.
7. **Closing.** At the end you receive your final payslip with accrued entitlements and your documents, including the annual tax certificate (CU).

## Things to keep in mind

A short project leaves little time on the administrative side too: it is best to contact us before work starts so that membership and contract are ready. VAT treatment of the invoice, whether the client is EU or non-EU, is assessed case by case, and we take care of it. If you live abroad, your tax residence must be checked separately and cannot be taken for granted.

Another practical point: if the client offers an extension or a new project after the three months, your position is already active and you can continue without starting over. If the relationship ends instead, there are no open positions to close.

Income tax on employment income is applied through withholding at source with year-end adjustment, plus regional and municipal surtaxes. We do not give net-pay simulations upfront because they depend on your personal situation.
