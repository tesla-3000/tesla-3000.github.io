Someone receiving a first proposal from a foreign client faces a fork: do I do it as occasional work, open a VAT number, or go through a cooperative? None of the three is wrong in the absolute: each has a perimeter where it works best. This guide gives criteria for choosing, without replacing an adviser's opinion.

## Occasional services

Designed for sporadic activity, without organisation or continuity. Typically single assignments, with limits on amount and duration set by the applicable rules, to be verified for your own situation.

**Strengths:** no VAT registration, light obligations for truly isolated jobs.
**Limits:** unsuited to stable relationships; with several assignments for the same client, the classification may be questioned; with foreign clients, tax and withholding need checking.

## VAT number (self-employment)

The professional or sole trader invoices directly. Under the flat-rate regime, when requirements are met, there is a revenue threshold of 85,000 euro and a 15% substitute tax (5% in the first five years if requirements apply).

**Strengths:** maximum autonomy, ability to have many clients, direct control of pricing and organisation.
**Limits:** obligations (e-invoicing, returns, accountant), contributions at your own expense, no employee-style cover (holiday, sickness, severance), insurance to arrange yourself.

## Worker cooperative

The member has an employment relationship with the cooperative (normally as an employee, under Law 142/2001): INPS contributions paid by the cooperative, INAIL, holiday, sickness, maternity, severance (TFR), unemployment benefit (NASpI) under legal conditions, monthly payslip. The cooperative invoices the client.

**Strengths:** less bureaucracy, employee protections, a commercial contract with the client, services included.
**Limits:** less managerial autonomy, member share, internal rules, service cost, not suited to regulated professions with exclusive rights.

## A grid of questions

- Is the work **continuous** or sporadic?
- Do I have **one main client** or many?
- Do I want **employee protections** or prefer autonomy?
- How much time do I want to spend on **administration**?
- Does the client require **a company** as supplier?
- Does my profession have a **professional register** with exclusivity?

## Example: Francesca, UX consultant

A fictional example: Francesca receives a three-week assignment from an English startup. She thinks of occasional work. After a month the client proposes continuing with two days a week for a year. Occasional status no longer holds. Francesca compares the alternatives: with a VAT number she would have autonomy and future clients, but would handle bookkeeping and contributions; with the cooperative she would have a payslip and fewer obligations. She picks the second for this client, leaving open the chance to change. It is only an example: the choice depends on personal priorities.
