Among the documents to sign when joining a worker cooperative there is a term that worries people coming from the VAT-number world: the member share. It looks like an entry fee, but its nature is different. Understanding it avoids misunderstandings and last-minute questions.

## What the share is

A cooperative is a company: it has share capital, made up of members' contributions. Whoever joins subscribes a share, whose amount is set by the by-laws or the cooperative's governing bodies. It is not a fee or a commission: it is a stake in the capital of the company you become part of.

At {{< brand >}}, the share serves the typical function of cooperative capital: giving the company the means to operate and confirming the member's role as co-owner rather than a mere customer.

## How it differs from a service cost

It helps to separate two things:

- **The share**, which is the member's capital and, per the by-laws, refundable on leaving.
- **Service costs**, meaning what the cooperative retains to manage contract, payroll, invoicing, insurance and support. These are an expense, not capital.

Mixing them up leads to over- or underestimating the real cost of joining.

## When you get it back

The share is refundable when the member leaves, but conditions depend on the by-laws and the law. Things to check:

- the **cases** in which membership ends (withdrawal, expulsion, other);
- the **deadlines** for the refund;
- the possibility of **deductions** or set-offs, for instance against sums owed to the cooperative;
- the refunded value compared with the nominal value: generally to be read in the document.

There are no identical rules for every cooperative: the by-laws are the source to read.

## Example: Sara, freelance graphic designer

A fictional example: Sara, a designer working for a French studio, joins the cooperative and subscribes the required share. After two years she decides to return to a traditional job and gives notice of withdrawal. Before doing so she rereads the by-laws: she finds the notice periods, how the refund works and the conditions for any set-offs. She asks support for a calculation of what is due and receives the refund within the timeframe set in the documents. A real case may differ: every set of by-laws must be read.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Advance information.** Before joining, ask for the amount and conditions of the share.
2. **Subscription.** The share is subscribed with the admission request; the cooperative indicates how to pay it.
3. **Receipt and records.** Keep the paperwork: it proves your stake in the capital.
4. **During the relationship.** The share stays registered as long as you are a member.
5. **Exit.** With withdrawal or other end of membership, the refund procedure starts under the by-laws.

## What to check

- Amount and payment method.
- Rules on withdrawal and notice.
- Refund terms and conditions.
- Whether other items are charged to the member besides the share.

## A realistic view

The share is a reasonable commitment if you see it for what it is: capital that remains yours, with written rules. It is not a figure to promise or predict with certainty in an article: the definitive answer is in the by-laws and regulation you receive before signing.
