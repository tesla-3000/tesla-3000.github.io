A concert production in Berlin wants a lighting technician for a six-date tour; an events agency in Vienna needs a front-of-house engineer for a congress. Technical work in live entertainment is among the most intermittent: a few intense days, then pauses. Between load-in, rehearsal, show and load-out, the day often runs twelve hours, and those who do it need a form of work that holds up under frequent travel.

## The phases of a gig

A live event is structured as:
- **site visit and technical sheet** (rider): rigging, available power, control positions;
- **load-in and set-up**, often overnight;
- **rehearsals and soundcheck**;
- **the show**, with continuous presence at the desk;
- **load-out** and packing.

Gigs are paid **by the day** (a different rate for travel days and show days) or as a **package** for the whole event. Payment follows the event, often at 30-45 days.

## Typical pitfalls

1. **Unpaid hours**: set-up and load-out outside the show must be included in the rate or counted separately.
2. **Safety**: work at height, electrical installations, rigging. The client must state who is responsible for stage safety and supply protective equipment.
3. **Travel**: flights, trains and hotels must be defined beforehand. Itemised reimbursement is clearer than a flat sum.
4. **Own equipment**: if you use your gear, agree rental and liability for damage.
5. **Schedule changes**: dates moved or cancelled; you need a cancellation clause.

## Checklist before leaving

Before each trip check: updated technical rider, who is responsible for stage safety, load-in and load-out times, lodging near the venue, documents for entering the country and transporting equipment. Bring essential spares and a list of personal gear. Photograph the state of the rig on arrival to avoid disputes. Upload all receipts to the platform within a few days so expenses are reimbursed in the next payroll cycle.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Describe the gig**: dates, places, rider, proposed rate.
2. **Membership** with a refundable share of capital (Law 142/2001).
3. **Contract** with the production: day rates, overtime, travel, cancellations.
4. **Operations**: activities, expenses and documents on the platform; WhatsApp support during the tour.
5. **Protections**: INPS contributions, INAIL cover, holidays, sick leave and severance pay under the employment relationship; professional liability included.
6. **Invoicing**: the cooperative invoices the foreign production, in foreign currency if needed, under rules verified case by case.

## Another route: the VAT number

Many technicians work under the flat-rate regime (up to 85,000 euro in revenue, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years with requirements), with contributions to Separate Management or other schemes depending on the activity. It is valid for those with several recurring clients. Some entertainment sectors have specific pension rules that need checking. The cooperative helps when foreign gigs are occasional and the client wants a structure to sign with.
