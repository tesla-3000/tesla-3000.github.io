For a product designer, having two clients at once is more common than it seems: a Dutch fintech startup asking for twenty hours a week and a Swedish software company for another fifteen. The challenge is balance: schedules, confidentiality, design tools and, of course, how the work is framed and paid.

## The craft in two contexts

A product designer works through a cycle of user research, flows, wireframes, prototypes and developer hand-off. In two different companies this cycle repeats at its own pace: two-week sprints in one, monthly releases in the other.

Typical deliverables:
- files in the design tool with components, styles and documentation;
- interactive prototypes for user testing;
- developer specs with states and edge cases;
- contributions to a shared design system.

## Risks of serving two clients

1. **Overlapping deadlines**: two releases in the same week. Communicate early and state your capacity.
2. **Conflicts of interest**: if the clients compete, a clause may bar the collaboration.
3. **Confidentiality**: files, prototypes and research data must stay separate, with distinct accounts and workspaces.
4. **Different contracts**: one wants partial exclusivity, the other full assignment of rights. Check they are compatible.
5. **Payment times**: one client pays at 30 days, the other at 60. Personal cash flow suffers.

## Keeping the two projects apart

Use separate workspaces, folders and accounts for each client and never reuse one client's proprietary components for the other. Plan the week in blocks: for example mornings for one client and afternoons for the other, with a buffer for urgent matters. Tell both your real availability and your holiday dates well ahead. If one client asks for more hours, assess the impact on the other before replying and renegotiate the contract instead of working nights to hold everything together.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Gathering information**: the type of contract each client proposes, hours, duration, confidentiality clauses.
2. **Membership** as a worker-member with a share of capital refundable on exit (Law 142/2001).
3. **Two independent contracts**: each with its own rate, deliverables and payment terms.
4. **Logging** hours per contract and expenses (licences, user testing) on the platform.
5. **Single payslip** with INPS contributions and INAIL cover; professional liability included.
6. **Periodic review**: if one client ends, that contract stops without affecting the other.
