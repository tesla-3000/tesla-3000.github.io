Many professionals arrive at the cooperative after years as self-employed with a VAT number. The question is always the same: do I have to close it? Can I keep it? What about my existing clients? The answer needs a few distinctions, and a conversation with your own accountant.

## Two paths, two ways of working

With a **VAT number** you are a sole trader or professional: you invoice, keep the books, pay taxes and contributions, and deal with filings. Under Italy's flat-rate regime, for example, there is a revenue threshold of 85,000 euro and a 15% substitute tax (5% for the first five years if requirements are met), with contributions paid by you.

With the **cooperative** you are a member with an employment relationship, generally as an employee: the cooperative invoices the client and pays you via payslip, paying contributions and withholdings. These are not choices to judge in the abstract: they depend on income, type of clients, appetite for autonomy and for protections.

## What happens to the VAT registration

There is no single rule. There are three possibilities:

1. **Closing it.** If all your activity moves to the cooperative, this may be the most coherent choice. Final obligations must be considered (last return, any contributions due, closing the position).
2. **Keeping it for other activity.** If you have distinct activities, you may keep it. It is important to avoid overlap with what you do as a member, and to assess the effect on the flat-rate regime.
3. **De facto pause.** A formal "standby" does not always exist: an open but inactive VAT registration still has minimal obligations and costs, to be assessed with your accountant.

The decision always lies with the professional and their adviser: the cooperative cannot give personalised tax advice on your VAT registration.

## Existing clients

The switch works best when the client is willing to sign a new agreement with the cooperative. Practical tips:

- do not end the old relationship before agreeing the new one;
- check deadlines and notice periods of the current contract;
- avoid periods in which you work for the same client through two structures;
- align the start date of the new agreement with the end of previous invoicing.

## Example: Davide, translator

A fictional example: Davide has had a flat-rate VAT number for four years and works for two clients, one in Spain and one in Italy. He wants to move the Spanish client to the cooperative, because it asks for more structure, and keep the VAT number for the other for now. With his accountant he checks that the two activities stay distinct and assesses the effect on flat-rate limits. He closes current invoices, starts the agreement with the cooperative and continues the other work as before. A different solution may suit another person better.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Conversation.** You explain your current activity, clients and tax regime.
2. **Compatibility check.** We verify that work as a member is distinct from VAT-registered work, if you keep it.
3. **Transition planning.** Dates for the end of invoicing and the start of the agreement.
4. **Admission and contract.** Application, share capital, contract and regulation.
5. **Start.** You begin with the platform; for tax doubts you stay in touch with your accountant.

## What to check

- Effects on the flat-rate regime if you keep the VAT number.
- Exclusivity clauses with other clients.
- Pension position (Separate Management, Artisans/Traders) and closing formalities.
- Any incentives or benefits tied to the VAT number that you might lose.
