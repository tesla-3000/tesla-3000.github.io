One advantage of independent work is the ability to diversify: more clients, less dependence on one. This applies in a cooperative too, but with a few more rules, because the member's activity sits inside a formal employment relationship.

## How it works in practice

Each client has its own agreement, signed by the cooperative. The member works across several assignments and records activities and hours for each on the platform. There is one employment relationship, with the cooperative; there are several clients. The payslip is monthly and single, covering total hours.

This simplifies things: no more invoices to issue, collections to manage and positions to follow. But it requires organisation.

## What can complicate things

### Hours and workload
The employment contract sets commitment and hours. With several assignments you must fit everything within those limits, or update the contract if the overall commitment changes.

### Exclusivity and non-compete
Some agreements provide for exclusivity, or prevent you from working for competitors. With several clients in the same sector, check there are no conflicts.

### Confidentiality
If you work for two competing companies, you must separate information and documents carefully. A mistake can cost a contract.

### Time zones and priorities
Several clients in different countries mean meetings at awkward hours. You need a personal rule on availability and response times.

## Useful strategies

- **Single agenda.** One calendar with blocks dedicated to each client.
- **Maximum capacity.** Set a weekly hours ceiling and do not exceed it.
- **Clear communication.** Tell each client when you are available and for what.
- **Reporting per assignment.** Record activities immediately, per client.
- **Safety margin.** Do not fill your calendar to a hundred percent: surprises happen.

## Example: Silvia, translator and reviewer

A fictional example: Silvia works for two clients, a French publisher (twenty hours a week) and a German software company (twelve hours). At the cooperative she has two assignments. Before accepting the second she checks that the first has no exclusivity clause and asks the cooperative to update her employment contract to reflect the new commitment. Each week she uses the platform to log activities per client and closes on Friday with a summary. When the German client asks to increase hours, she assesses her capacity and politely declines.

5. **Daily management.** Hours and activities for each client are recorded on the platform.

## What to check

- Whether your employment contract allows several assignments and within what hour limits.
- Exclusivity and non-compete clauses in the agreements.
- How hours are handled between clients with different rates.
- Impact on the payment calendar.
