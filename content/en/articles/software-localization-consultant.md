A Californian fitness app wants to launch an Italian version; a German business-software platform must localise its interface, error messages and documentation. Translating an interface is not like translating a book: strings are short, without context, with variables and placeholders. A localization consultant knows the product, respects the available space and knows when a literal translation goes wrong.

## Typical activities

- **String translation** with resource files in various formats.
- **Building the glossary** and style guide, for instance how to address the user (formal or informal).
- **Linguistic review and QA** directly in the app, with screenshots.
- **Localizing documentation**, help articles, emails and notifications.
- **Cultural adaptation**: date formats, currencies, examples that make sense in Italy.
- **Pseudo-localization and testing** of text length.

Deliverables are translated files, a glossary, QA reports and reporting of linguistic bugs.

## Where problems arise

1. **Strings without context**: is "Home" a menu or a house? Ask for screenshots and comments.
2. **Placeholders and plurals**: variable errors break the interface. A technical check is needed.
3. **Continuous updates**: software changes every week; the work is recurring, with irregular volumes.
4. **Translation memories**: who owns them? Write it down.
5. **Per-word rates** with discounts for repetitions: understand how they are calculated.

## Quality and delivery times

Ask for access to a test build or to screenshots with strings in context, and report ambiguities in a single ordered list. Agree an update calendar: often the heaviest work is not the first translation but chasing continuous changes. Check how plurals and gender are handled, which in Italian require different forms from English. A shared glossary and a short style guide, updated with each release, reduce inconsistencies and revisions.
