Online course platforms, language academies, coding bootcamps, corporate training companies: many are abroad and look for teachers and trainers with a precise skill, often in Italian. The teacher prepares materials, runs live lessons, marks exercises and answers students. {{< brand >}} structures the relationship.

## How assignments work

Platforms usually pay **per lesson hour** (live or recorded), **per course** (a fixed fee for the content) or a mix. Activities divide into course design, content production (slides, videos, exercises), live delivery, tutoring and assessment. Preparation hours often exceed teaching hours: build it into the rate. Agree on:

- number of teaching and preparation hours recognised;
- calendar and hours (different time zones);
- ownership and licence of materials and videos;
- assessment and student support after lessons.

## Rights in content

Recorded courses, slides and exercises are creative works. The platform usually asks for a broad licence or assignment; the teacher may prefer a limited licence or a royalty. Copyright rules are specific and must be checked case by case before signing: the cooperative cannot guarantee the same formula for everyone.

## The cooperative's role

The cooperative signs the agreement with the platform and invoices. For EU clients the reverse charge normally applies (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72); for non-EU clients generic B2B services are normally outside Italian VAT: checked case by case. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have a work relationship with INPS contributions, INAIL and, if employment, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. The cooperative also offers training for members and a community.

## Typical pitfalls

- **Unpaid preparation.** Include it in the rate.
- **Cancelled lessons.** If students do not enrol, who pays for the lesson? Set a rule.
- **Reused recordings.** Without an agreement, the platform can resell your lesson for years.
- **Student ratings.** A low score can affect future assignments: ask for transparent criteria.
- **Regulated professions.** Some teaching activities require accreditation: check with us.

## Example

Example: imagine Federica, a communication trainer from Trieste, hired by a German course platform for a twelve-lesson live business Italian course with handouts. The agreement recognises 12 teaching hours and 20 preparation hours, with a two-year licence on the materials. Federica logs hours and lessons on the platform and receives a payslip; the cooperative invoices under reverse charge. Fictional character.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Intro call.** You present course, hours, rates, rights requested.
2. **Joining.** Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Agreement.** Contract with the platform, with calendar and licence.
4. **Activity.** Preparation and teaching hours on the platform.
5. **Invoicing.** The cooperative invoices and collects.
6. **Payslip.** Monthly pay and documents online.

INAIL, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

## Compared with a VAT number

The flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) is common among trainers; the cooperative is an alternative for those who want worker protections and less admin.

## Checklist before signing

Before accepting the assignment, check: the course syllabus and student level; expected number of participants; the technical platform (Zoom, proprietary LMS) and support for problems; the calendar with time zones; rules on recording and reuse. Ask for a tutor or platform contact for administrative matters. Prepare with a pilot lesson and gather feedback: it improves the course and gives you arguments to renegotiate the rate. If the materials are yours, specify what remains yours and what you grant.
