A Korean cosmetics maker wants to sell in Europe; an American furniture brand wants a storefront on Italian and German marketplaces. Neither knows catalogue rules, local logistics, reviews or commission costs. The e-commerce consultant who speaks the language and knows the channels becomes the point of reference. The assignment is usually recurring, but the payment model is anything but standard.

## What you do on a marketplace

The work splits into phases:
1. **Market analysis**: categories, competitors, prices, seasonality.
2. **Account opening** and compliance: company data, restricted categories, product and labelling requirements.
3. **Catalogue**: titles, attributes, images, Italian translation, keywords.
4. **Logistics**: choosing between direct shipping, marketplace warehouse or local operator, plus returns handling.
5. **Advertising and promotions**: in-marketplace campaigns, timed offers.
6. **Periodic analysis**: margin per product, conversion rate, reviews and complaints.

Deliverables are monthly reports, an uploaded and optimised catalogue, and a quarterly operating plan.

## Pay models

There are three: a **fixed monthly fee**, a **percentage of incremental revenue**, or a **mix**. The mix is the most balanced, but you must say how revenue is measured (net of returns and fees?), from which date, and for how long the share is due after the relationship ends.

## Agreeing indicators

A consulting contract holds up if indicators are well chosen. Separate metrics the consultant controls (listing quality, catalogue upload times, complaint response) from those that also depend on the client (prices, stock, shipping times). Agree how promotional periods are treated, when sales rise for seasonal reasons, and which method calculates the uplift against the previous period. A simple monthly report with three or four numbers is more useful than a complicated dashboard.

## Dangers to avoid

- **Account access**: the consultant should operate through a user role on the client's account, not personal credentials, and should not be the shop's owner of record.
- **Product liability**: compliance, warranties and refunds remain with the seller.
- **Sales data**: the agreement should say whether the consultant can use it anonymised in case studies.
- **Growth expectations**: promising results is risky; commit to activities and process indicators.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Find out** which marketplaces and products are involved.
2. **Join** as a member with a share of capital refundable on exit (Law 142/2001).
3. **Contract** with the client: activities, reports, variable part, data use.
4. **Operations** on the platform: activities, expenses, documents to sign.
5. **Invoicing** the foreign client, in foreign currency too, under B2B rules verified case by case.
6. **Payslips** with contributions, INAIL and professional liability included; advance if payment slips.

## Cooperative or VAT number

Independent consultants often have several clients and prefer the flat-rate VAT number (85,000 euro threshold, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years with requirements): maximum freedom, Separate Management contributions and their own invoices and returns. If the job is one long consultancy for a single brand, the cooperative provides a more linear relationship with holidays, sick leave and severance pay and reduces admin.
