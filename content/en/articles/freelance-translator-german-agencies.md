Translating for German agencies almost always means juggling several clients: orders arriving through portals or email, tight deadlines, payment at 30, 45 or sometimes 60 days. Translation is the craft; the admin around it is what eats your time. {{< brand >}} proposes moving that admin to the cooperative.

## How work with German agencies tends to run

Agencies (Übersetzungsbüros) usually pay per source or target word or line, with different rates for translation, revision (Lektorat) and post-editing, plus minimum fees for short jobs. Many use CAT tools and translation memories, and apply discounts on repetitions and fuzzy matches. Before accepting a job, clarify:

- the unit of measure and rates for matches and repetitions;
- the CAT tool and delivery format;
- payment term and the procedure for disputes;
- confidentiality and ownership of translation memories.

## Where the cooperative fits

The cooperative signs the commercial contract with the agency, which treats it like any other supplier. As a worker-member you have the work relationship provided by Italian Law 142/2001 with the cooperative. If it is an employment relationship, you get social security (INPS), INAIL cover, paid leave, sick leave, parental leave, severance (TFR) and a payslip. You do not open a VAT number and do not issue invoices.

For EU clients the invoice is normally issued under reverse charge as a B2B service (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72); the cooperative checks this for each agreement.

## Typical pitfalls

- **Long payment terms.** With 60-day terms, a month of work is paid two months later. An advance from the cooperative's fund can bridge the gap.
- **Uneven volumes.** A heavy month followed by a light one: with an employment relationship, your pay is set consistently with the agreed activity, not only raw volumes.
- **Sworn and certified translation.** These have their own rules (registers of experts, courts); check with us what can be handled.
- **Memory rights.** Settle who owns the translation memory.

Example: imagine Chiara, a German-to-Italian translator specialising in technical manuals. She works with two agencies in Munich and Hamburg. ## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Gathering information.** You list agencies, rates and payment terms.
2. **Joining.** You subscribe a share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Agreements.** The cooperative contracts with each agency.
4. **Activity.** You enter projects, delivered words and expenses on the platform.
5. **Invoicing and collection.** The cooperative invoices and collects, then runs payroll.
6. **Support.** WhatsApp and email for any question, with professional liability insurance included.

## A fair comparison with a VAT number

Under the flat-rate regime (revenue ceiling of 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) fixed costs are low and you are fully autonomous, but contributions and paperwork are yours. The cooperative makes more sense if you want worker protections and less bureaucracy, and have a few stable clients.

## Checklist before signing

Before taking a new job from an agency, check: the language pair and field (technical, legal, marketing); the per-word or per-line rate and match percentages; whether payment runs from delivery date or invoice date; the contract language, often German, for which you can ask a courtesy translation; penalty clauses for delays and complaints. Keep a sheet with agency, rate, payment term and contact person: it helps you see which clients are truly worthwhile and which pay too late.
