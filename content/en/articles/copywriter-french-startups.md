French Tech startups communicate a lot and fast: landing pages, newsletters, LinkedIn posts, product sheets for the Italian market. They look for native Italian copywriters who grasp the brand's tone. For a writer it is a great opportunity, but it needs a structure for contracts and payments. {{< brand >}} provides it.

## How French startups work

The most common model is the **retainer**: a monthly fee for a set number of pieces (say four articles and a newsletter). Sometimes it is project-based, such as copy for a site about to launch. Startups think in sprints and change priorities often, so agree on:

- a brief and tone-of-voice guide at kickoff;
- revision rounds per piece;
- client response times;
- monthly invoicing and payment terms.

## The cooperative's role

The cooperative signs the agreement with the startup and invoices the fee. As these are B2B services to an EU client, the reverse charge generally applies (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72), checked case by case. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have a work relationship with the cooperative: if employment, INPS contributions, INAIL, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. No VAT number, no e-invoicing to manage.

## Typical pitfalls for writers

- **Vague briefs.** A thin brief breeds endless revisions: ask for examples of copy the client likes.
- **Growing scope.** From blog to ads and video scripts: redefine the agreement.
- **Translation versus creation.** Transcreation (adapting rather than translating) is quoted separately.
- **Funding cycles.** A startup's cash can be unstable: set clear payment terms.
- **Credit and byline.** If text goes out under your name (ghostwriting or byline), define it.

## Example

Example: imagine Elena, a copywriter from Genoa, working with a Lyon startup on the Italian localisation of an expense-management app. Six-month retainer: eight help articles, four newsletters, three site pages a month. The cooperative signs the agreement and invoices monthly under reverse charge. Elena logs deliveries on the platform and gets a monthly payslip. When the startup pays late, an advance from the fund avoids the wait. Fictional character.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Intro call.** You explain client, volumes, languages, rates.
2. **Joining.** Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Agreement.** Commercial contract with the startup.
4. **Activity.** Deliveries and hours on the platform.
5. **Invoice and collection.** The cooperative issues the invoice and collects.
6. **Payslip.** Monthly pay with documents and annual tax certificate.

INAIL cover, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

## Compared with a VAT number

The flat-rate regime (85,000 euro ceiling, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) is a real option, especially with many small clients. But contributions and paperwork are yours. With one or two steady startups, the cooperative simplifies life.

## Checklist before signing

Before starting with a startup, ask for: the brand guide and an archive of approved copy; who approves pieces and how many days they take to reply; the working tools (Notion, Google Docs, CMS) and access permissions; the channel for urgent requests; words to avoid. Agree a paid trial piece: it is the quickest way to grasp the tone and check the relationship works. If the client changes direction mid-sprint, decide how retainer volumes are recalculated. All of it goes in writing in the agreement.
