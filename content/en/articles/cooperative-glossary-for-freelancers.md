Anyone coming from the VAT-number world meets a new vocabulary in a cooperative. No term is hard, but it helps to know them before signing. Here is a practical glossary, in alphabetical order, with simplified explanations (the by-laws, regulation and contract always prevail).

## A-C

**Member assembly.** The body in which members decide on the cooperative's main matters, for example approving the accounts, according to the by-laws.

**Payslip.** The monthly document showing gross pay, contributions, withheld taxes and net pay for a member with an employment relationship.

**Worker cooperative.** A company whose members are also workers and pursue a mutual-benefit purpose, meaning continuity of work and better conditions.

**Assignment (commessa).** The commercial contract between cooperative and client for a service. The member does not sign it.

**CU (Certificazione Unica).** Annual document in which the employer certifies income, withholdings and contributions.

## D-N

**Secondment.** Temporary sending of a worker to another place or country to carry out their activity while keeping the relationship with the employer.

**Dual relationship.** Principle of Law 142/2001: the member has a membership relationship and a further, distinct working relationship.

**Advance fund.** Mechanism by which the cooperative, under its own rules, may advance payments before collecting from the client.

**INAIL.** Institute insuring against workplace accidents and occupational diseases; cover is paid by the employer.

**INPS.** Social security institute: contributions for employment are paid by the employer.

**NASpI.** Unemployment benefit for those who involuntarily lose their job, under legal conditions.

**Expense claims.** Requests for reimbursement of costs incurred for the client, with supporting documents.

## P-R

**Platform.** The digital system in which the member records activities and deliveries, uploads expense claims, and consults payslips and documents.

**Share capital (quota sociale).** Capital the member subscribes on joining; refundable on leaving under the by-laws.

**Withdrawal.** Voluntary exit of the member, with the procedures and notice set by the by-laws and regulation.

**Internal regulation.** Document, required by Law 142/2001 and filed, governing members' working relationship.

**Member refund (ristorno).** Part of the surplus the cooperative may distribute to members in proportion to work done. Not guaranteed (art. 2545-sexies of the Civil Code).

**Professional liability insurance.** Insurance for damage caused to third parties while carrying out the activity.

## S-Z

**Worker-member.** Someone who is a member of the cooperative and works for it.

**By-laws.** The act governing the cooperative as a company: members, bodies, capital, operating rules.

**Withholding agent.** A party, such as the employer, that withholds taxes from pay and remits them to the tax authority.

**TFR.** Severance pay: a share of pay set aside, recognised at the end of an employment relationship.

**730.** Simplified income tax return form for employees and certain other taxpayers.

**Reverse charge.** VAT mechanism in which the customer accounts for the tax; applies to certain B2B services, case by case.

A fictional example: Beatrice, a freelance editor, receives the regulation and contract and finds terms she does not know. She uses the glossary to distinguish share capital (refundable capital) from service costs (expense), the assignment (contract with the client) from the employment contract (her relationship with the cooperative), TFR from member refund. - Precise definitions in the cooperative's documents.
- That terms used in the contract match those in the regulation.
- For tax or social security matters, professional advice.
