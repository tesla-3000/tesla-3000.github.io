A US sportswear brand wants an Italian presence on Instagram, TikTok and Discord, with replies in Italian and community initiatives. It looks for someone who knows the language, culture and habits of Italian followers. For a community manager it is stimulating work, but with a client nine hours behind and different payment habits. {{< brand >}} offers a structure for working in order.

## How the role works

The community manager handles comments, messages, moderation, the content calendar, online events, micro-influencer collaborations and reports. For a US brand, requests usually come in English; content must be adapted to Italy, not translated literally. The most concrete question is **when** presence is needed: peak hours for the Italian audience, and calls with the US team in the Italian afternoon or evening. Agree in advance:

- response windows and holidays;
- brand tone and voice guidelines;
- crisis and escalation protocol;
- tools (Sprout, Hootsuite, Notion, Discord) and who pays for them.

## The cooperative's role

The cooperative signs the agreement with the US brand and invoices. For non-EU clients generic B2B services are normally outside Italian VAT, checked case by case. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have a work relationship with INPS contributions, INAIL and, if employment, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip: especially useful when hours are structured like an employee's. Dollar payments are handled by the cooperative.

## Typical pitfalls

- **Borderless availability.** Messages arrive at all hours: set working hours and paid on-call.
- **Moderation and wellbeing.** Handling hostile comments is draining: plan breaks and a protocol.
- **Client accounts.** Never manage with personal credentials.
- **Influencers and contracts.** Dealings with third parties must be approved by the client.
- **Crises.** A wrong reply hurts the brand: an approver is needed.

## Example

Example: imagine Camilla, a community manager from Cagliari, running the Italian presence of a running brand based in Portland. The agreement describes the service: a monthly editorial plan, publishing and moderation with defined response times, a weekly alignment call, a monthly report and a crisis protocol, for a monthly fee. Camilla organises her own schedule. The cooperative invoices in dollars; Camilla logs activities on the platform and receives a monthly payslip. Fictional character.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Intro call.** You describe brand, hours, channels, tools.
2. **Joining.** Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Agreement.** Contract with the US brand with hours and crisis protocol.
4. **Activity.** Hours, reports and expenses on the platform.
5. **Invoicing.** The cooperative invoices and collects, also in foreign currency.
6. **Payslip.** Monthly pay and documents online.

INAIL, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

## Compared with a VAT number

The flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) is a valid route for those with several clients. With a single brand and structured hours, the cooperative can offer protections closer to an employee's.

## Checklist before signing

Before starting, ask the brand for: the global editorial calendar and how much room local content has; typical moderation cases and approved replies; permissions to answer independently; the budget for campaigns and collaborations; escalation channels. Also agree American and Italian holidays and how to handle peaks, such as Black Friday. Ask who has the final say on tone and sensitive content. A shared document with rules and sample replies reduces mistakes and speeds up the work.
