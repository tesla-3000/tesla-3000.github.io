When an indie game looks for audio, it needs everything: footsteps, weapons, interface, ambience, adaptive music, sometimes voice and mix. Many studios, especially small ones scattered across Europe and North America, have no in-house sound designer and turn to external professionals. {{< brand >}} structures the work with a contract and a payslip.

## What you deliver in game audio

Deliverables are split by type: **SFX**, **ambience**, **UI audio**, **music** (loops, stingers, adaptive versions), **voice processing**, **implementation** in the engine with middleware such as Wwise or FMOD, and final **mix**. The work often includes an **asset list** with names, durations, formats and variants to deliver per milestone (prototype, alpha, beta, release). The project can last months, peaking near milestones. Pay is per **asset** (per effect or per minute of music), per **day** or per **milestone**. Agree on:

- asset list and number of variants;
- delivery format (wav, ogg) and technical specs;
- revision rounds and acceptance criteria;
- who does the implementation (middleware or engine);
- licences of libraries and plugins used.

## Rights and licences

Effects and music are protected works. Game studios usually want full assignment or a broad licence of sounds made for the game. Copyright and neighbouring rights have specific rules and must be checked case by case. If you use third-party sound libraries, their licences must allow commercial use in a game.

## The cooperative's role

The cooperative signs the agreement with the studio and invoices. For EU clients the reverse charge normally applies (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72); for non-EU clients generic B2B services are normally outside Italian VAT: checked case by case. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have a work relationship with INPS contributions, INAIL and, if employment, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. Milestone payments can be bridged with the fund advance.

## Typical pitfalls

- **Slipping milestones.** If the studio delays the game, payments slip: ask for a fixed monthly share.
- **Revisions after integration.** A sound may work in the DAW and not in the game: set integration hours.
- **Library licences.** A "free" sound may not be allowed in commercial products.
- **Mix loudness.** Specs differ between PC, console and mobile.
- **Credits.** Agree how you appear in the end credits.

## Example

Example: imagine Tommaso, a sound designer from Bergamo, hired by an indie studio in Helsinki for an adventure game: 450 effects, ambience for six levels and FMOD implementation, over four months. The agreement provides milestone payments and a minimum monthly share. Tommaso logs days on the platform and receives a payslip; the cooperative invoices the studio. Fictional character.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Intro call.** You present project, assets, milestones, rate.
2. **Joining.** Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Agreement.** Contract with the studio, with assignment and licences.
4. **Activity.** Days, deliveries and expenses on the platform.
5. **Invoicing.** The cooperative invoices and collects, also in foreign currency.
6. **Payslip.** Monthly pay and documents online.

INAIL, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

## Compared with a VAT number

The flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) is widely used by those with many clients. For long projects with few studios, the cooperative simplifies admin and gives worker protections.

## Checklist before signing

Before starting, ask the studio for: a playable build or gameplay video; the design document and asset list; the engine and middleware; loudness and format specs; the milestone calendar. Propose a short creative test (two or three sounds) to align on style. Keep an organised library with clear names and project backups, and define what you deliver beyond final files (DAW sessions, sources). Agree the level of post-release support, such as patches and updates.
