A Dutch logistics company wants to predict delivery delays, a British health startup needs to classify images, a Swiss retailer seeks a recommendation engine. For projects like these, in-house teams often fall short. People who work with data and models, including in Italy, are approached for fixed-term assignments. The point is to give a stable form to work made of very different phases, with peaks and waits.

## The life of a project

1. **Scoping**: define the problem, the success metric and available data.
2. **Data exploration**: quality, gaps, bias, legal constraints.
3. **Prototype**: a first model with a baseline for comparison.
4. **Experimentation**: iterations, validation, error analysis.
5. **Going to production**: pipelines, monitoring, documentation.
6. **Handover** to the in-house team.

Deliverables include reproducible notebooks, versioned code, a model with a documentation card and a presentation for non-technical managers. Pay is often by **milestone**, because the duration of experimentation is uncertain.

## Where problems arise

- **Insufficient data**: the discovery that data is not enough comes after weeks. A separately paid initial feasibility phase protects both parties.
- **Ambiguous metrics**: "improve forecasts" is not a goal; you need an agreed threshold.
- **Intellectual property**: what belongs to the client (project model and code) and what to the professional (pre-existing libraries and methods).
- **Personal data**: training models on data about people requires legal bases and agreements; it is not only the consultant's responsibility.
- **Maintenance**: who fixes the model when data drifts? Decide whether it is included.

## Tips to make the project last

Document modelling choices and data assumptions from day one: the client's team will have to pick them up. Agree on a simple success metric and a baseline to compare against. Arrange a short handover period at the end and state whether it is paid. When data is limited, prefer interpretable models: they are easier to defend before a non-technical manager and simpler to maintain after delivery.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Present the project**: goal, data, milestones, duration.
2. **Membership** with a share of capital refundable on exit (Law 142/2001).
3. **Contract** with IP, confidentiality, data protection and payment terms.
4. **Platform**: days, expenses, documents and signing.
5. **Monthly payslip** with INPS and INAIL; professional liability included. The cooperative invoices at milestones, in foreign currency too.
6. **Advance** from the fund if a milestone is far off.

## Before signing: three questions

- Is the feasibility phase paid even if the project does not continue?
- Who decides that a milestone is accepted, and within how many days?
- Does pre-existing code I bring stay mine, licensed to the client?
