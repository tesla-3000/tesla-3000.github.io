A Manchester agency has too many clients and too few developers, a Canadian marketing studio gets requests for online shops and has no one to build them. For these studios, a subcontractor who can build WordPress sites and Shopify stores is precious. For the Italian developer these are recurring jobs, with clear deadlines and small budgets. The risk is ending up with many invoices to issue, in different currencies, to different clients.

## How a subcontractor works

The typical flow:
1. The agency sends a **brief** with approved design, content and features.
2. The developer gives an **estimate** in hours or a flat fee.
3. **Development** on a staging environment; delivery with review.
4. **Go-live** and testing on devices and browsers.
5. **Handover**: access, documentation, client training.
6. Optional **maintenance** (updates, backups, support).

Deliverables are the working site, the theme or child theme, basic documentation and transferred access.

## Mistakes that cost

- **Vague brief**: "a site like the one I mentioned" produces endless changes. Better a scope listing pages and features.
- **Late content**: texts and images arrive later; state that delay moves the dates.
- **Licences**: premium themes and plugins should be registered to the client or agency.
- **Security and backups**: who answers if the site is attacked after delivery? Define the warranty scope.
- **Trickle of changes**: a set number of included revision rounds prevents endless work.

## How to reduce endless modifications

Ask for the design to be approved in writing before starting and deliver a first version on staging by a fixed date. Collect comments in one document and answer in a batch rather than handling scattered chat requests. Specify that changes beyond the planned rounds are quoted separately. At handover, supply a short user manual and a list of installed plugins and versions: it reduces unpaid support requests in the following months.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Send us the brief** or the agency's quote.
2. **Membership** with a share of capital refundable on exit (Law 142/2001).
3. **Contract**: scope, revision rounds, white label, confidentiality, separate maintenance.
4. **Platform**: hours, expenses (domains, testing), signed documents.
5. **Invoicing**: the cooperative invoices the foreign agency, in pounds or dollars too, under B2B rules verified case by case.
6. **Payslip** with contributions, INAIL and professional liability included.

## Before accepting: a quick list

- Is the design approved before starting?
- Who holds access to the domain and hosting?
- How many revisions are included in the price?
- Is maintenance a separate fee?

## VAT number or cooperative

Many web developers work under the flat-rate regime (85,000 euro threshold, 15% substitute tax, 5% in the first five years with requirements), with Separate Management contributions: valid with many clients and small projects. The cooperative is handy when contracts come from a few foreign agencies wanting a single contact and you prefer to avoid invoicing and bookkeeping.
