Four, six, ten years abroad have passed. You return to Italy for family, for the climate, as a life choice. Your client is in London, Dubai or New York and you do not want to lose it. In practical terms, though, the relationship changes: until now you may have been employed by a local company of theirs, or paid as a resident elsewhere. Now you live in Italy, and the relationship must be redesigned.

## What changes with the return

1. **Residence**: the move requires checking tax residence, which depends on precise elements. There are conventions between states and specific rules, and exiting the foreign regime may need professional assistance.
2. **Employment contract**: the existing relationship is often tied to the foreign office. The client may be unable to continue without a local solution.
3. **Social security**: periods worked abroad and contributions paid elsewhere may be considered in Italy under European rules and conventions.
4. **Healthcare, bank, home**: practical aspects of the return, which continuous work makes much easier to manage.
5. **Return incentives**: favourable regimes may exist, with requirements and limits: check case by case.

## The client's problem

For the foreign client, the question is: "How do I keep paying you?" Typical answers are three: open a company in Italy (costly and bureaucratic), continue with a freelance contract (requires a VAT number and obligations), or lean on a local structure that signs the contract. The last option gives the client a single counterpart who issues invoices and keeps you in an employment relationship with protections.

## A list for the first months

In the first months after returning, keep a few documents in order: employment and residence certificates from abroad, previous contracts, statements of contributions paid, correspondence with the foreign tax office. Agree with the client a precise date for moving from the old relationship to the new one, to avoid gaps in payments. Plan liquidity for the transition period: the payment advance offered by the cooperative's fund can serve exactly here.

## Other routes

You can open a VAT number (flat-rate with a 85,000 euro threshold, 15% substitute tax, 5% in the first five years if requirements are met), which gives autonomy and allows several clients, with Separate Management contributions and obligations of its own. You can propose that the client set up a presence in Italy, a solution for long-lasting relationships. The cooperative is often a good bridge for the first months after returning, when there is already a lot to organise.
