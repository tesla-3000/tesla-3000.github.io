When you leave a VAT number for an employment relationship, one of the new items is TFR. Many have heard of it without knowing how it works. Let us look at the mechanism in simple terms, without figures, which always depend on the actual case.

## What TFR is

TFR, the end-of-service payment, is a sum the employer sets aside during an employment relationship and pays out when it ends. It builds up each year under the rules of the Civil Code and collective contracts. It can be left with the employer or directed to a supplementary pension scheme, according to the worker's choice within the set deadlines.

For a worker-member with an employment relationship at {{< brand >}}, TFR follows these general rules. The precise items and methods are shown on the payslip and in the contract. We cannot give amounts: they depend on pay, duration and applicable rules.

## What changes compared with a VAT number

Those working under a VAT number have no TFR: the invoiced fee is the only income, and any saving for the future depends on personal choices, such as pension funds or reserves. In employment, by contrast, there is a mandatory accrual that grows with the length of the relationship and sits alongside social security contributions.

It is neither a gift nor a hidden cost: it is part of the overall pay package. So when comparing different models it helps to consider it as a component of deferred pay.

## Example: Paola after three years

Example: imagine Paola, a project manager, who has been employed through the cooperative for three years on engagements for Nordic clients. Each year the payslip shows the accrual. When the relationship ends, the cooperative pays out the accrued TFR with other end-of-employment entitlements. Paola remembers having chosen at the start to leave TFR with the company; she could have directed it to a supplementary pension fund.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Initial choice.** At the start you complete the required declarations on where TFR goes.
2. **Accrual.** The cooperative calculates and sets it aside under the applicable rules.
3. **Information.** Items are visible in documents and payslips, which you find on the platform.
4. **Advances.** In cases provided by law you can request an advance, following the indicated procedure.
5. **Payout.** On termination the cooperative pays TFR with the other final entitlements.
6. **Documentation.** You receive the settlement statements and tax paperwork.

## Things to know

Paid-out TFR is subject to separate taxation under tax rules: we do not go into detail or run simulations, because they depend on individual circumstances. If you have had several relationships over time, each employer pays out the TFR for its own period.

TFR is a right of the employee: if your relationship with the cooperative is of another kind, for example self-employment, it does not accrue. That is why it is useful to clarify the type of relationship at the start.

One last tip: check your payslips regularly. They are your official record of how pay, contributions and accruals build up.

## TFR and pension funds

Directing TFR to a supplementary pension fund is a personal choice with pros and cons: the fund may offer different returns and tax advantages, but carries constraints on when the capital is received. We give no investment recommendations. If the decision matters to you, talk to an independent adviser or an advice centre before signing the form, remembering that in some cases the choice can only be changed under specific rules.
