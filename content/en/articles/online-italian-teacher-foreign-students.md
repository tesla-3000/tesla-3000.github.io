Teaching Italian online to students abroad is a growing activity: expats, people of Italian descent, culture and food enthusiasts, professionals relocating. Many teachers work across several channels: private lessons, language schools, tutoring platforms. The risk is fragmentation: PayPal payments, transfers from different countries, no contract. {{< brand >}} helps bring order.

## How the work runs

There are three typical channels. **Private lessons**: the student pays per lesson or per package (say ten lessons). **Foreign language schools and academies**: they pay hourly, with fixed calendars. **Tutoring platforms**: they keep a commission on the lesson price. Lessons take place by video call with materials, exercises and corrections. Non-billable time is considerable: preparation, messages, finding new students. Terms of sale should state prepayment, cancellations and refunds.

## The cooperative's role

With the cooperative the relationship changes: for foreign schools and companies, the agreement is between them and the cooperative; for private students suitable arrangements are agreed case by case. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have a work relationship with INPS contributions, INAIL and, if employment, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. For EU clients the reverse charge normally applies (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72); for non-EU clients generic B2B services are normally outside Italian VAT: checked case by case.

## Typical pitfalls

- **Private students and invoicing.** Private customers (B2C) follow VAT rules different from B2B: check case by case.
- **Last-minute cancellations.** Without a rule, you lose income.
- **Platform commissions.** Factor them into your rate.
- **Time zones.** A clear calendar with local times avoids mistakes.
- **Required qualifications.** Some schools ask for certificates (for example in teaching Italian as a second language): check in advance.

## Example

Example: imagine Silvia, an Italian teacher from Siena, working with a language school in Toronto (three groups a week) and twelve private students across the US and Australia. The cooperative signs the agreement with the school and works out with us how to handle the private students. Silvia logs hours on the platform and receives one monthly payslip. Fictional character.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Intro call.** You present channels, students, rates, hours.
2. **Joining.** Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Agreements.** Contract with each school; review for private students.
4. **Activity.** Lessons and preparation hours on the platform.
5. **Invoicing.** The cooperative invoices and collects, also in foreign currency.
6. **Payslip.** Monthly pay and documents online.

INAIL, professional liability insurance, training and the members' community are included.

## Compared with a VAT number

The flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) is very common among private teachers, especially with many individual students; the cooperative works well with schools and stable clients and for those who want worker protections.

## Checklist before signing

To organise your activity well, prepare: a clear offer (single lesson, ten-pack, group course); cancellation terms written and accepted; a shared calendar with time zones; materials by level (A1-C2) and goal (travel, work, citizenship, certificates); a method for checking progress. If you prepare students for exams like CILS or CELI, state that you do not guarantee the outcome. Collect student feedback: it is your best advertising.
