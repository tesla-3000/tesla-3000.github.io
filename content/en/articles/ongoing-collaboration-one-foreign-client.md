It happens often: you start as a freelancer, find a satisfied foreign client and that client starts sending you one project after another. With a VAT number all the risk is yours: if you fall ill or the client pauses, your income stops with it. The cooperative offers another route, on one condition: the work stays a **service** that you organise, with a defined scope and deliverables.

## What changes with the cooperative

With {{< brand >}} you can have an employment relationship, often as an employee, governed by Law 142/2001 and the cooperative's internal regulation: INPS contributions paid by the cooperative, INAIL, holidays, sick leave, maternity and paternity leave, TFR severance pay and, if you lose work involuntarily, NASpI under the legal conditions.

The client signs a service contract with the cooperative for each project, or a framework agreement with successive orders, and receives invoices from the cooperative.

## What we do not do

We do not place people with companies that manage them as their own employees. If the client wants you on its staff, on its hours and under its lead, the right route is direct hiring. We handle projects and professional services: translation, development, consulting, content, training.

## Example: Luca and the German client

Example: imagine Luca, a technical translator. For a year a Munich company has been sending him manuals and catalogues to translate, almost every month. He invoices under his own VAT number and feels poorly protected: if he falls ill, income stops.

The cooperative signs a framework agreement with the German company: per-word rate, delivery times, review and confidentiality; each new manual is an order. Luca organises the work as before, records activities and deliveries on the platform and now receives a monthly payslip with holidays and TFR. Meanwhile he uses the members' community to find other clients too.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Review of the work.** You describe the projects: scope, deliverables, frequency, prices.
2. **Membership.** You subscribe the share capital quota and accept the internal regulation.
3. **Contract with the client.** The cooperative offers the client a service contract or framework agreement replacing your individual invoices.
4. **Employment contract.** We define your relationship with the cooperative.
5. **Day-to-day.** You log activities and deliveries on the platform, download payslips and request expense reimbursements.
6. **Collection.** We invoice and manage payments, including in foreign currency, with a possible advance through the fund.

## Points to watch

The change must be explained well to the client: for them, the contract holder and the invoicing entity change, and the contract describes more precisely what is delivered. Many foreign companies like the arrangement because it simplifies supplier management.

For VAT treatment of invoices to foreign clients (B2B services, reverse charge within the EU, not subject to Italian VAT outside the EU) we refer to a check on the individual case. If you live abroad, your tax residence and any double taxation treaties must be assessed separately.

Remember too that the price is negotiable: the member defines their own service and price, and the cooperative formalises it in the contract.

## Mistakes to avoid

The first is continuing to invoice personally after the switch: once the contract is with the cooperative, individual invoices no longer make sense and should be closed in an orderly way. The second is not warning the client in time: it takes a few weeks to update supplier records, purchase orders and payment details. The third is assuming the terms stay identical: scope, price, deliverables and duration should be rewritten in the contract, so everyone knows what was agreed.
