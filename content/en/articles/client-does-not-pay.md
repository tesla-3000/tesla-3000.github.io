It is the most common fear for anyone working for distant clients: what if they do not pay? With a foreign client the risk seems higher, because of distance, language and different legal systems. Having a structure behind you does not remove it, but it changes who handles it and how.

## Why the cooperative model changes things

In the cooperative model there are two separate relationships. The member has an employment relationship with the cooperative, with payslip and contributions. The cooperative has a commercial relationship with the client, with invoice and collection. If the client is late, the commercial problem belongs to the cooperative, which holds the contract and the standing to act.

This does not mean the member is always sheltered from every consequence: cover depends on the regulation, the contract and how the payment advance fund works. That is why it is important to ask in advance how timing between invoice, collection and payslip is handled.

## What to do at the first signs

1. **Check the due date.** Delay often comes from an internal client approval not yet given.
2. **Tell the cooperative immediately.** The earlier the problem is flagged, the easier it is to solve.
3. **Gather documentation.** Activity records, delivered work, client confirmations, approval emails.
4. **Do not promise anything to the client alone.** Concessions on discounts or instalments must be agreed with the cooperative.
5. **Keep a professional tone.** The relationship may still recover.

## Recovery tools, in general

They depend on the contract, the amount and the client's country. Typically one proceeds with reminders, a formal demand letter, negotiation of a repayment plan and, in the most serious cases, court or arbitration action if the contract provides for it. With foreign clients, governing law and chosen forum greatly affect time and cost. Each step is decided case by case.

## Prevention is cheaper

- **Check the client before starting:** existence of the company, details, history, any reviews from other suppliers.
- **Ask for a deposit** on long projects or new clients.
- **Invoice at a reasonable frequency:** monthly, not at project end.
- **Set clear payment terms** and late-payment interest in the agreement.
- **Provide for suspension:** a clause may authorise suspension of service after a certain delay.
- **Avoid concentrating all your work on one client.**

## Example: Gianni, developer

A fictional example: Gianni works for a foreign e-commerce company. On the third invoice, payment has not arrived after 45 days. Gianni tells the cooperative, which chases the client and discovers the approval was stuck in one office. Payment arrives fifteen days late. Meanwhile, Gianni receives his payslip according to the agreed rules. To avoid a repeat, a shorter deadline and fortnightly invoicing are added to the agreement. It is not always this simple, but prevention helps.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Clear terms in the agreement.** {{< brand >}} includes deadlines, interest and suspension clauses.
2. **Collection monitoring.** The cooperative tracks invoice due dates.
3. **Reminders.** In case of delay, it sends reminders to the client.
4. **Advance through the fund.** Where available, the fund can reduce the impact on the member's payment timing, under rules and limits to be checked.
5. **Recovery actions.** If needed, they are decided by the cooperative.

## What to check

- How the cooperative treats non-collection towards the member.
- Conditions and limits of the advance through the fund.
- Suspension clauses and penalties in the agreement.
- Costs of any court recovery.
