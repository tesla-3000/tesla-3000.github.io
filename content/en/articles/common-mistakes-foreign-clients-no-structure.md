Working for a foreign client is a great opportunity, but those who do it alone, without a structure behind them, tend to trip over the same things. This list is not meant to frighten: it is meant to help you spot problems before they cost time and money.

## 1. Starting work without a written contract

A verbal or email agreement seems enough until a dispute arises over timing, scope or payment. **How to avoid it:** even for small assignments you need a text defining activities, fee, deadlines, confidentiality and ownership of results.

## 2. Underestimating tax

VAT on services to foreign clients, tax residence, withholdings applied by the client's country: these change case by case. **How to avoid it:** ask an accountant before issuing your first invoice, not after.

## 3. Invoicing incorrectly

Wrong client details, missing wording, unverified VAT numbers, muddled currency. **How to avoid it:** always verify data before invoicing and use correct templates.

## 4. Not tracking payments

Receipts into assorted accounts, no reconciliation, delays no one monitors. **How to avoid it:** keep a register of due dates and check receipts regularly.

## 5. Accepting payment terms that are too long

Ninety days from invoice may seem normal, but weighs on liquidity. **How to avoid it:** agree reasonable deadlines, deposits on long projects and periodic invoicing.

## 6. Ignoring exchange-rate risk

Fees in a currency other than the euro can fluctuate. **How to avoid it:** define in the contract who bears the risk and provide for review clauses.

## 7. Signing clauses without reading them

Exclusivity, non-compete, assignment of all rights, unlimited liability: often hidden in standard templates. **How to avoid it:** read everything, ask for help on technical parts and negotiate.

## 8. Having no insurance cover

A professional error or an accident can cause damage far greater than what you earned. **How to avoid it:** consider professional liability insurance and accident cover.

## 9. Forgetting social security and protections

Without a structure, holiday, sickness, maternity and pension depend entirely on you. **How to avoid it:** plan an emergency fund and consider pension schemes.

## 10. Depending on a single client

If the client stops, income drops to zero. **How to avoid it:** diversify where possible and keep contacts and pipeline active.

## Example: Nicola, developer

A fictional example: Nicola works for a US startup with no written contract, invoicing as a generic service. After six months the startup changes contact person, disputes hours already worked and delays three payments. Nicola has no clear record of the agreement, does not know whether there were withholdings to recover, and has no cover. With an adviser's help he reconstructs the situation, formalises a new contract and decides to move to a structure that handles administration and invoicing.
