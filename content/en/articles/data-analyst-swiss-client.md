A Swiss company needs an analyst for dashboards, reports and models. It offers a twelve-month engagement, Monday to Thursday, with access to its systems and weekly team meetings. It asks for a monthly invoice. From your side it is a real job with one client, and a VAT number feels imposed. {{< brand >}} suggests a different path.

## The typical picture

An analyst working for a single principal typically uses SQL, Python or R and BI tools such as Power BI or Tableau; delivers dashboards, recurring reports and ad hoc analyses; works set hours with the team; and handles sensitive data. When dependence on the client is high, a work relationship with protections often makes more sense than a VAT number with one client.

## How it is set up

The cooperative signs an agreement with the Swiss company: term, working days, daily or monthly rate, confidentiality, data protection. As the client is non-EU, generic B2B services are normally outside the scope of Italian VAT (checked case by case). The cooperative invoices in Swiss francs and collects. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have an employment contract with the cooperative: INPS, INAIL, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip.

## Typical pitfalls for analysts

- **Data access.** Define who authorises access and how it is tracked; personal data protection must be regulated in the agreement.
- **Model ownership.** Queries, notebooks and dashboards: clarify assignment and reuse.
- **Residence and tax.** If you live in Italy, work normally stays in Italy; if you relocate or work near the border, residence rules and double taxation treaties must be assessed case by case.
- **Swiss franc.** The exchange rate affects collections: agree conversion timing.
- **On-call.** Avoid informal out-of-hours requests not covered by the agreement.

## Example

Example: imagine Luca, a data analyst from Como, hired by a logistics company in Lugano to build shipping dashboards, three days a week for a year. The cooperative signs the agreement with a daily rate; Luca records his days on the platform, the client approves them, the cooperative invoices in francs. He receives a payslip and employee protections. Residence and any tax implications are reviewed before starting. Fictional character.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Intro call.** Describe client, term, days, rate, place of work.
2. **Joining.** Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Check.** We review residence, place of work and clauses.
4. **Agreement.** Contract with the Swiss company.
5. **Activity.** Days and expenses on the platform.
6. **Invoice and payslip.** Invoicing, collection also in foreign currency, monthly payslip.

Professional liability insurance, INAIL and support are included.

## The VAT-number alternative

Under the flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) you have autonomy and low costs, but contributions and paperwork stay with you, and with one client there is a risk of economic dependence. Weigh it calmly.

## Checklist before signing

Before starting, clarify with the client: the work environment (remote access, VPN, company or personal devices); data classification and confidentiality rules; working language (German, French, Italian or English depending on the canton); the local holiday calendar; how deliverables are approved. Also ask whether a trial period is planned and how it is recognised. If you plan to move to Switzerland, say so right away: social security and tax change and a prior assessment is needed.
