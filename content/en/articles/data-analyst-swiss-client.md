A Swiss company needs a set of dashboards and reports on its sales and logistics data. It asks you for a quote to build it and then maintain it for a year, with a monthly invoice. You do not want to open a VAT number just for this project. {{< brand >}} suggests a different path.

## The typical picture

A data analysis project usually covers: collecting and cleaning data with SQL, Python or R; dashboards in Power BI or Tableau; recurring reports and on-demand analyses; documentation for the people who will use the tools. The value for the client lies in the **results delivered**, not in hours spent at the computer, and that is what the contract should be built on.

## How it is set up

The cooperative signs a service contract with the Swiss company: scope (which dashboards, which reports, how often), phases and deadlines, acceptance criteria, a price per phase or a monthly fee for maintenance, confidentiality and data protection. You organise the work independently; the client names a contact person who provides requirements and approves deliveries. As the client is non-EU, generic B2B services are normally outside the scope of Italian VAT (checked case by case). The cooperative invoices in Swiss francs and collects. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have an employment contract with the cooperative: INPS, INAIL, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip.

## Typical pitfalls for analysts

- **Data access.** Define who authorises access and how it is tracked; personal data protection must be regulated in the contract.
- **Model ownership.** Queries, notebooks and dashboards: clarify assignment and reuse.
- **Scope.** New requests outside the scope are handled as changes, with their own price and timing.
- **Residence and tax.** If you live in Italy, work normally stays in Italy; if you relocate or work near the border, residence rules and double taxation treaties must be assessed case by case.
- **Swiss franc.** The exchange rate affects collections: agree conversion timing.

## Example

Example: imagine Luca, a data analyst from Como. A logistics company in Lugano asks him for a set of shipping dashboards. The contract has three phases (data model, five dashboards, automated monthly report) priced per phase, then a twelve-month maintenance fee. Luca records activities and deliveries on the platform; the client's contact approves each delivered phase; the cooperative invoices in francs. Luca receives a payslip and employee protections. ## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Intro call.** Describe client, project, deliverables, price, place of work.
2. **Joining.** Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Check.** We review residence, project scope and clauses.
4. **Contract.** Service contract with the Swiss company.
5. **Activity.** Activities, deliveries and expenses on the platform.
6. **Invoice and payslip.** Invoicing, collection also in foreign currency, monthly payslip.

Professional liability insurance, INAIL and support are included.

## The VAT-number alternative

Under the flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) you have autonomy and low costs, but contributions and paperwork stay with you. It works best with several clients. Weigh it calmly.

## Checklist before signing

Before starting, clarify with the client: the technical environment (remote access, VPN, devices); data classification and confidentiality rules; working language (German, French, Italian or English depending on the canton); how deliverables are approved; how out-of-scope requests are handled. If you plan to move to Switzerland, say so right away: social security and tax change and a prior assessment is needed.
