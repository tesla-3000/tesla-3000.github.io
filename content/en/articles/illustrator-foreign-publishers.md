The illustrator delivers spreads, covers, character designs; the publisher pays in stages. {{< brand >}} offers a structure to manage it all steadily.

## How an editorial assignment works

A typical book assignment splits into phases: roughs, approval, final art, colour, corrections. At each phase the publisher responds and sometimes asks for changes. Payment usually comes in instalments: one on signing, one on delivery of roughs, the balance on final art. Deadlines are often tight and tied to printing, so publisher delays in approving affect your schedule.

Agree in the contract: number of illustrations, format and resolution, revision rounds, scope of use (editions, languages, territories), cover credits.

## Rights, advances and royalties

Caution is needed here. Copyright in illustrations, assignment for specific editions and any royalty payments follow their own rules and are not automatically part of a work relationship. The agreement states what is commissioned and how it is assigned; each case deserves a check with us.

## What the cooperative does

The cooperative signs the agreement with the publisher, invoices the fee for the commissioned work and collects (also in foreign currency). For EU clients the reverse charge normally applies (art. 7-ter of decree 633/72); for non-EU clients generic B2B services are normally outside Italian VAT, checked case by case. As a worker-member (Italian Law 142/2001) you have a work relationship with contributions, INAIL and, if employment, paid leave, sick leave, severance and a payslip. No VAT number needed.

## Typical pitfalls

- **Unlimited revisions.** Fix the number of rounds in the agreement.
- **Approval times.** If the publisher is slow, define extensions or fees.
- **Format change.** Moving from paperback to hardcover may need new art: quote it.
- **Merchandising and secondary uses.** Not included unless written.
- **Originals.** Clarify who keeps the physical originals.

## Example

Example: imagine Irene, an illustrator from Bologna, hired by a French publisher for a 32-page picture book. The agreement covers twelve double-page spreads, two revision rounds, a four-month deadline and payment in three instalments. Irene logs activities on the platform and the cooperative invoices at each instalment; Irene receives a monthly payslip. A fund advance spares her the wait between instalments. Fictional character.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Intro call.** You present assignment, deadlines, fee, rights requested.
2. **Joining.** Share of capital, refundable on exit under the bylaws.
3. **Agreement.** Contract with the publisher, with phases and instalments.
4. **Activity.** Phases, days and expenses on the platform.
5. **Invoicing.** The cooperative invoices and collects.
6. **Payslip.** Monthly pay and documents online.

INAIL, professional liability insurance and WhatsApp/email support are included.

## Compared with a VAT number

The flat-rate regime (revenue up to 85,000 euro, 15% substitute tax, 5% for the first five years if conditions are met) is common among illustrators. It offers autonomy but means handling contributions, invoices and returns. The cooperative is an alternative for those who prefer a work relationship.

## Checklist before signing

Before signing with a publisher, check: the spread list and format; interim and final deadlines; the payment schedule; the scope of assignment (editions, languages, territories, duration); ownership of originals; cover credits. Ask to see a standard contract and compare it with the copyright rules, to be checked case by case. If the publisher is small, ask other illustrators about payment punctuality.
