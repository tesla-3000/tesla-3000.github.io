Working in pairs on the same project is common: a designer and a copywriter, an architect and a technician, two developers splitting front-end and back-end. Sometimes the two are also a couple in life. For a foreign client a single point of contact is convenient; for the two professionals, however, the administration doubles if each has their own VAT number.

## Typical problems of working as a pair

With two VAT numbers you need two invoices, two sets of accounts, two deadline calendars and an agreement on how to split pay and responsibility. If one of you falls ill or stops, the project risks stalling and the client does not know whom to ask. Setting up a company, on the other hand, costs more to start and run than a few months of work justify.

With {{< brand >}} each of you becomes a worker-member (Law 142/2001) with your own employment relationship, while the client has just one commercial contract with the cooperative. Internal responsibilities are organised in the engagement, which can assign different activities to each member.

## Example: Giulia and Andrea for a French agency

Example: imagine Giulia, an illustrator, and Andrea, an animator, who produce a series of short videos together for an agency in Lyon. Until now they worked with two separate invoices and constantly argued over deadlines. They both join the cooperative.

The cooperative signs one commercial contract with the agency, listing distinct activities: illustrations and animations. Each logs their own activities on the platform and receives their own payslip. If Andrea has a week of sick leave, his coverage follows the rules of his employment relationship; Giulia carries on and the cooperative informs the client about any delays.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Joint call.** You explain the project, the roles and the relationship with the client.
2. **Double membership.** Each of you subscribes your own share capital quota: the two relationships are independent, even where there are personal ties.
3. **Single engagement.** The cooperative signs one contract with the client, listing activities by role.
4. **Individual employment contracts.** Each member has their own relationship, with their own hours, pay and protections.
5. **Activity logging.** Each uses the platform for their own activities; the client receives a coherent summary.
6. **Invoicing and collection.** One invoice for the engagement; the cooperative manages collection and payments, including in foreign currency.

## What to clarify before starting

Even as a couple, the two relationships remain distinct: each member has their own rights, contributions and position. This protects both, for example if personal circumstances change. It helps to put in simple writing who does what and how hours are divided, to avoid misunderstandings.

For tax, each of you is subject to progressive income tax withheld at source with year-end adjustment, plus regional and municipal surtaxes; we do not estimate net pay, as it depends on each person's family and personal situation. For invoicing foreign clients (EU or non-EU) VAT treatment is checked case by case.

If one of you later wants to work on other projects, they can do so while remaining a member, without involving the other. And if the project grows and a third collaborator is needed, the cooperative can add them to the same engagement with their own relationship.

## If one of you leaves the project

It happens: a personal commitment, a new opportunity, a change of priorities. With two distinct relationships, one person leaving does not drag the other down. The engagement can be reshaped, the client informed by the cooperative and, if needed, another member can take over the uncovered activities. With two VAT numbers and an informal agreement the same situation is far more fragile.
