Working alone has many advantages, but also a price: when a doubt arises, there is often nobody to ask. An administrative deadline, a strange message from the client, a payslip to understand. That is why at {{< brand >}} WhatsApp and email support is part of the service.

## Why a quick channel matters

Work for foreign clients is made of small urgent questions. I got a request to change the plan, how do I put it in the engagement? They asked for a document, what can I send? I am ill today, who do I tell? A slow channel, such as a form to fill in or a ticket answered in days, does not suit these cases. WhatsApp, which we all use every day, is immediate and informal, while still leaving a trace.

## What to use it for

- **Platform questions**: where to upload a document, how to log an activity.
- **Payslips and documents**: understanding an item, requesting an attestation.
- **Expense claims**: checking what is reimbursable before a trip.
- **Absence notices**: illness, holidays, unforeseen events.
- **Doubts with the client**: how to respond to a request outside the engagement.
- **Orientation**: working out whom to turn to for a specific topic.

For formal matters, such as disputes or contract changes, you may be asked to send an email or document as well, to leave an orderly record.

## Example: Nicola and the nine o'clock question

Example: imagine Nicola, a consultant working from home for an Irish client. One evening the client writes asking to add two days of work to the project, with earlier delivery. Nicola does not know whether he can accept without amending the engagement. He writes to the cooperative's support on WhatsApp, describes the request and next morning receives a clear answer: an addendum to the engagement must be formalised, and the cooperative contacts the client. Nicola avoided a hasty yes and ending up with uncovered work.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **First contact.** On joining you receive support details and hours.
2. **Message.** You write, describing the question clearly, with any attachments.
3. **Pick-up.** An operator replies within the stated times or tells you when to expect an answer.
4. **Guidance.** You are told what to do and, if needed, which documents to upload on the platform.
5. **Formalisation.** For contractual matters the answer is confirmed in writing.
6. **Closing.** If the request is resolved you say so; if not, it continues.

## The limits of support

Support does not replace a personal legal, tax or pension adviser: it can explain how the relationship with the cooperative works, but cannot advise on your whole personal situation, such as other income or a mortgage. Nor is it an emergency health or safety service. And it does not guarantee instant replies at every hour: hours and timings are those declared.

For important decisions, such as choosing the type of relationship, returning after a long absence or changing client, it is better to schedule a short call instead of a quick chat.

## The community as a second channel

Alongside official support there is the members' community: colleagues who have faced similar situations and can suggest contacts, tools or practical tips. It is not a cooperative service in the strict sense, but it is one of the most useful resources for people working alone. Those working alone need not feel isolated: they have a structure behind them and other colleagues nearby.

## Writing a good message

A clear message gets a faster answer. State who you are and which engagement you mean, describe the question in two or three sentences, say what you have already tried and attach any document or screenshot. Mention the deadline if there is one. Avoid sending sensitive documents or passwords in a chat: support will tell you where to upload them safely. Good habits on your side make the service faster for everyone.
