Negotiating the rate is the part many freelancers dread most. With a foreign client there is the added difference of market, currency and distance. But negotiation is above all method: if you arrive prepared, the conversation becomes concrete, not emotional.

## Before talking numbers

### Know your real cost
A sustainable rate must cover: the pay you want to earn, labour and cooperative service costs, unpaid days (holiday, sickness, gaps), tools and training. If you start from the number that "feels right", you risk finding out later that it does not hold. The guide on calculating the client's cost can help you structure the reasoning.

### Know the value to the client
What does your work solve? How much would it cost the client not to do it, or to hire in-house? A rate is easier to defend when tied to a result, not only to hours.

### Research the market
Look at typical rates for your profession in the client's country, without copying them mechanically: your context may differ.

## During the negotiation

1. **Anchor the price.** If possible, make the first proposal yourself, at a value slightly above your sustainable minimum.
2. **Present a package, not a number.** State what is included (hours, deliverables, availability) and what is not.
3. **Offer options.** Two or three formulas (hourly, monthly, per project) ease the choice without lowering the price.
4. **Explain the structure.** If you work through a cooperative, the client must know the invoice is issued by it.
5. **Close in writing.** Terms, currency and reviews go in the agreement.

## Answering "it is too expensive"

Do not discount right away. Ask: compared to what? Then you can choose between reducing the scope, extending delivery times, proposing a longer commitment with a slightly lower rate, or holding the price while explaining the value. Discounts without something in return set precedents that are hard to recover.

## Currency

If the client pays in a currency other than the euro, clarify who bears the exchange-rate risk: you can fix the rate in euros, or in foreign currency with a review clause if the exchange rate moves beyond a threshold. Also agree the fees in advance.

## Example: Luisa, SEO consultant

A fictional example: Luisa proposes 55 euro an hour to a Norwegian company. The client says it is high. Luisa does not cut the price: she proposes a monthly package with a fixed number of hours and a report, justifying the value with agreed traffic goals. The client accepts a six-month commitment. The agreement provides for a rate review after the first half-year. The number is invented and does not represent a market value.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Sustainability estimate.** {{< brand >}} shows you the cost breakdown so you can check the minimum rate.
2. **Negotiation.** You lead it with the client, with the cooperative's support if needed.
3. **Formalisation.** The agreed rate goes into the agreement.
4. **Invoicing.** The cooperative invoices at the scheduled dates, including in foreign currency.
5. **Review.** If the workload changes, the agreement is updated.

## What to check

- That the rate covers the cost of the employment relationship.
- Payment terms: they affect liquidity.
- Review and renewal clauses.
- Extra expenses: reimbursed or included?
