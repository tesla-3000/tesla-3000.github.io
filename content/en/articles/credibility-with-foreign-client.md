When a foreign company considers entrusting work to someone in Italy, it does not only look at skills. It also asks: who will invoice me? What happens if the person falls ill? Do I have a contact if something goes wrong? An individual professional can answer all of that, but has to prove it every time. An organisation has already built the answers into how it works.

## What a foreign client really looks for

For a procurement or legal office abroad, every new supplier is a small administrative risk. Typical questions are:

- Is there a company with clear details, VAT number and a registered office?
- Who handles contributions, insurance and obligations towards the worker?
- Is there insurance cover in case of professional error?
- Do invoices and documents follow a predictable format?

A contract with a worker cooperative answers these questions in a standard way. The client does not need to assess an individual's tax position: it signs a commercial contract with an organised entity.

## Reliability signals an organisation brings

With {{< brand >}}, the client gets concrete elements: a clear commercial contract, invoices issued by the cooperative, administrative management of the member's employment relationship, workplace accident cover (INAIL) and professional liability insurance. For the person working, this means less time spent reassuring and more time producing.

Do not overstate it: the organisation does not replace skills or guarantee results. It adds structure, not quality. The quality of your work remains the first source of credibility.

## Example: Giulia, technical translator

A fictional example: Giulia translates technical manuals for a Dutch company. The company's purchasing department, used to working with firms, repeatedly asks for documents about her status. When Giulia proposes formalising the relationship through the cooperative, the company receives a commercial contract, a regular invoice and a single administrative contact. The client's internal process gets simpler, and Giulia no longer has to explain her tax situation alone.

## How it works with the cooperative

1. **Outline agreement with the client.** The member agrees on content, duration and the principle of the rate.
2. **Commercial contract.** The cooperative prepares and signs the contract with the client; the member does not sign as supplier.
3. **Member's employment relationship.** In parallel, the member works under a contract with the cooperative (normally as an employee), with a monthly payslip.
4. **Activity and reporting.** Timesheets and activities are recorded on the platform, which also helps document the work to the client.
5. **Invoice and collection.** The cooperative invoices the client, including in foreign currency, and collects payment.

## What to check

- That the client accepts a cooperative as counterparty: some have internal supplier rules.
- Any requests for documents (company records, certificates, policies): ask the cooperative in advance.
- Contract clauses on liability and governing law, to be read carefully.

## In short

Credibility with a foreign client is built on competence and administrative reliability. The second can be delegated to a structure, which is one reason many professionals choose a cooperative.
